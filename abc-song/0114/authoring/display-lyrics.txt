[Intro]
Accordion squeezes air, violin can bow, piano presses keys, kazoo begins with a hum.
A music maker changes the gesture to fit the instrument.

[Verse 1]
A — Accordion! | Free-reed instrument; bellows push or pull air through reeds while buttons or keys select notes.
B ... B ... Bell! | Metal idiophone rings when struck or when an internal clapper hits it; shape and size influence pitch.
C? Clarinet! | Single-reed woodwind with cylindrical bore; covered/open holes and keys change the effective air-column length.
D is Drum. Membranophone whose stretched head vibrates when struck; drum shape and tension affect tone. |

[Verse 2]
E — Electric Piano! | Keyboard instrument using electric/electronic tone generation or amplified pickups rather than an acoustic piano soundboard.
F ... F ... Fiddle! | A violin played in folk or traditional styles; the instrument is not a separate biological type from violin.
G? Gong! | Suspended metal percussion disc that vibrates after a mallet strike; size and shape affect pitch and resonance.
H is Harp. Plucked string instrument with strings running between soundboard and neck; each string usually supplies a pitch before pedal/lever changes. |

[Chorus]
Press, pluck, bow, breathe—A-B-C!
One motion does not fit every sound.

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument! | General word for a device used to make or control music; it is a category, not one sound or family.
J ... J ... Jaw Harp! | Small lamellophone held at the mouth; a flexible tongue is plucked while the mouth cavity shapes the tone, without biting the reed.
K? Kazoo! | Membrane instrument that changes a hummed voice through a vibrating membrane; blowing silently through it does not create the usual kazoo sound.
L is Lyre. Ancient-family plucked string instrument with strings stretched from a soundbox to a yoke. |

[Verse 4]
M — Maracas! | Rattles with loose material inside hollow shells; shaking makes particles strike the walls.
N ... N ... Note Card! | Learning card that displays a note symbol or musical prompt; it represents music but makes no sound itself.
O? Oboe! | Double-reed woodwind; two thin cane blades vibrate together to start its focused tone.
P is Piano. Keyboard instrument whose keys trigger felt-covered hammers to strike strings in an acoustic piano. |

[Chorus]
Press, pluck, bow, breathe—A-B-C!
One motion does not fit every sound.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quena! | Traditional Andean end-blown flute with a notched rim and finger holes.
R ... R ... Recorder! | Duct flute with finger holes; a built-in windway directs air to an edge.
S? Shaker! | Percussion instrument with loose particles or moving parts that sound when the body is shaken.
T is Trumpet. High brass instrument; buzzing lips feed a cylindrical-to-flared tube while valves redirect air through extra tubing. |

[Verse 6]
U — Ukulele! | Small four-string plucked instrument, commonly tuned with a high G in many soprano/concert setups.
V ... V ... Vibraphone! | Tuned metal-bar percussion instrument with resonator tubes; motor-driven fans can add tremolo on many models.
W? Whistle! | Air blown across an edge or through a fipple creates a high tone; loud whistles should stay away from ears.
X is Xaphoon. Compact single-reed wind instrument designed to produce a saxophone-like tone through a simple cylindrical body. |

[Chorus]
Press, pluck, bow, breathe—A-B-C!
One motion does not fit every sound.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yangqin! | Chinese hammered dulcimer; bamboo beaters strike many strings stretched over a trapezoidal soundboard.
Z ... Z ... Zills! | Small finger cymbals used in pairs; metal discs ring when brought together.
