[Intro]
A guitar starts with string, an oboe with reed, an udu with clay-and-air resonance.
Before the melody, ask what is vibrating.

[Verse 1]
A — Acoustic Guitar! | Hollow-bodied plucked string instrument; vibrating strings drive the soundboard and air in the body.
B ... B ... Bongos! | Pair of small open-bottom hand drums of different sizes; striking the heads produces contrasting pitches.
C? Cello! | Bowed string instrument held upright between the knees; its long strings and large body give a low-rich range.
D is Dulcimer. Name covers related zithers; a hammered dulcimer uses small beaters on stretched strings, while an Appalachian dulcimer is fretted and usually plucked. |

[Verse 2]
E — Euphonium! | Low brass instrument with valves and a conical bore; buzzing lips start the sound.
F ... F ... Flute! | Side-blown woodwind in the concert family; an air jet across the embouchure hole excites the air column.
G? Gong! | Suspended metal percussion disc that vibrates after a mallet strike; size and shape affect pitch and resonance.
H is Harmonica. Free-reed wind instrument; blowing or drawing air through channels makes metal reeds vibrate. |

[Chorus]
String, air, skin, metal—A-B-C!
Find the sound source before the instrument family.

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument! | General word for a device used to make or control music; it is a category, not one sound or family.
J ... J ... Jaw Harp! | Small lamellophone held at the mouth; a flexible tongue is plucked while the mouth cavity shapes the tone, without biting the reed.
K? Kazoo! | Membrane instrument that changes a hummed voice through a vibrating membrane; blowing silently through it does not create the usual kazoo sound.
L is Lyre. Ancient-family plucked string instrument with strings stretched from a soundbox to a yoke. |

[Verse 4]
M — Mandolin! | Small plucked string instrument, commonly with four double courses tuned in fifths.
N ... N ... Noise Maker! | Broad label for a sound-producing toy or percussion object; exact mechanism depends on the pictured item.
O? Oboe! | Double-reed woodwind; two thin cane blades vibrate together to start its focused tone.
P is Piano. Keyboard instrument whose keys trigger felt-covered hammers to strike strings in an acoustic piano. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quarter Note! | Rhythmic notation symbol whose duration is one beat in common 4/4 counting; it is a note value, not an instrument.
R ... R ... Recorder! | Duct flute with finger holes; a built-in windway directs air to an edge.
S? Saxophone! | Single-reed woodwind, usually metal-bodied; reed vibration starts the sound despite its brass appearance.
T is Tambourine. Frame percussion instrument with jingles, sometimes with a drumhead; shaking or striking activates different sounds. |

[Verse 6]
U — Udu Drum! | Nigerian clay pot percussion instrument; hands strike the body and opening to create resonant tones.
V ... V ... Vibraphone! | Tuned metal-bar percussion instrument with resonator tubes; motor-driven fans can add tremolo on many models.
W? Whistle! | Air blown across an edge or through a fipple creates a high tone; loud whistles should stay away from ears.
X is Xylophone. Tuned wooden-bar percussion instrument; bars are struck with mallets and arranged by pitch. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yangqin! | Chinese hammered dulcimer; bamboo beaters strike many strings stretched over a trapezoidal soundboard.
Z ... Z ... Zills! | Small finger cymbals used in pairs; metal discs ring when brought together.
