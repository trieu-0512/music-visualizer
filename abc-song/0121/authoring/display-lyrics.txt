[Intro]
Goggles can protect eyes, a cone marks space, a paddle moves water, a bib identifies an athlete.
Sports objects do different jobs before anyone starts moving.

[Verse 1]
A — Athlete! | Person who trains or participates in physical sport; skill develops through practice, recovery, coaching, and appropriate challenge.
B ... B ... Bicycle! | Two-wheeled pedal vehicle steered by handlebars; helmet, brakes, fit, and traffic-safe space matter.
C? Cone! | Bright marker defines boundaries, turns, stations, or lanes; it organizes space rather than making the movement itself.
D is Diving Board. Springing or rigid platform used for supervised diving at a designed pool; depth, rules, and trained supervision are essential. |

[Verse 2]
E — Exercise Mat! | Padded surface cushions floor exercises; thickness and grip should fit the activity.
F ... F ... Football! | Ball used in football codes; size, shape, and rules differ between association football, American football, rugby codes, and others.
G? Goggles! | Eye protection or swim eyewear; fit and sport determine whether they block water, wind, debris, or impact.
H is Hoop. Circular ring can mark a target or movement station; activity and size determine safe use. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inline Skates! | Boots with wheels in one line; helmet and appropriate protective gear plus a smooth safe area reduce injury risk.
J ... J ... Jersey! | Sport shirt identifies player/team and provides clothing comfort; it is not protective gear by itself.
K? Kayak! | Small paddle craft typically propelled with a double-bladed paddle; personal flotation and water-safety supervision are essential.
L is Lacrosse Stick. Shaft with netted head carries, passes, and shoots a lacrosse ball; protective equipment and rules control contact. |

[Verse 4]
M — Mat! | General padded or marked surface; exact sport decides thickness, grip, shock absorption, and boundaries.
N ... N ... Number Bib! | Wearable number identifies a competitor; it records identity/order rather than measuring performance.
O? Oar! | Long lever used from a fixed oarlock in rowing; different from a free handheld paddle.
P is Paddle. Handheld blade pushes against water to propel or steer a craft; blade and shaft design vary by sport. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quad Skates! | Roller skates with two pairs of wheels; helmet and protective gear plus safe surface are important.
R ... R ... Racket! | Handled frame with strings or solid face used to strike a ball/shuttle in racket sports; type depends on sport.
S? Soccer Ball! | Round association-football ball kicked, passed, or headed under game rules; size varies by age/group.
T is Tennis Ball. Felt-covered hollow rubber ball designed to bounce for tennis; racket striking happens in clear court space. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicycle! | Single-wheel pedal cycle requiring advanced balance; helmet and controlled practice area are important.
V ... V ... Vault! | In gymnastics, vault is an apparatus/event involving a run, springboard, support, and landing; trained coaching and mats are essential.
W? Whistle! | Signal device can start/stop play or attract attention; loud use stays away from ears.
X is X-shaped Agility Marker. X-shaped floor marker indicates a station or foot-placement target; it is a visual cue, not an obstacle to jump blindly. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Jersey! | Yellow sport jersey may identify a team or special competition status; meaning depends on the event.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Cone! | Cone arranged or marked for zigzag routes; the cone defines a path rather than requiring maximum speed.
