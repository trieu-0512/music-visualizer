[Intro]
Fur is one answer. Feathers are another. Flippers solve a different problem.
Cold places do not make every body the same.

[Verse 1]
A — Arctic Fox! Thick fur covers a compact body | small ears reduce exposed surface in severe cold.
B ... B ... Bear! Large furry mammal with broad paws | body size and coat shape differ from smaller cold animals.
C? Cod! Scales and fins belong to a fish living entirely in water | no fur needed below the cold surface.
D is Dog Sled. Wooden or synthetic runners carry a sled over snow | useful human travel context, not a body adaptation.

[Verse 2]
E — Eider! Dense feathers cover a sea duck | soft down beneath outer feathers provides insulation.
F ... F ... Fur Seal! Dense fur and strong flippers | one mammal combines insulation with a swimming body.
G? Goose! Feathers cover the body while webbed feet and wings handle different movement jobs | bird design, not furry design.
H is Harp Seal. Streamlined body and flippers work in water | coat pattern gives the adult its name.

[Verse 3]
I — Iceberg! Frozen water has no body covering | context matters because animals live around physical cold, not inside a costume story.
J ... J ... Jellyfish! Soft bell and tentacles drift in water | no bones, fur, feathers, or flippers at all.
K? Kittiwake! Feathers and long wings suit a seabird | light body works above cold coasts.
L is Lynx. Thick fur, broad paws, tufted ears | a land-cat body built for cold terrain.

[Verse 4]
M — Musk Ox! Long shaggy outer hair hangs over dense underwool | layered coat turns the animal into a moving wall of insulation.
N ... N ... Narwhal! Whale body stays smooth outside | insulation is carried under the skin rather than as fur.
O? Owl! Feathers cover a bird body and broad wings spread for flight | cold-region owls use plumage, not mammal fur.
P is Puffin. Dense feathers cover a compact seabird | wings work in air and underwater swimming.

[Verse 5]
Q — Qajaq! Traditional narrow Arctic boat | human design solves cold-water travel without being wildlife.
R ... R ... Ringed Seal! Rounded marine body, flippers, coat with pale rings | compact shape suits life around sea ice.
S? Seal! Streamlined body and flippers move through water | many seals rely on insulating fat beneath the skin.
T is Tundra. Cold open land supports low plants and wildlife | habitat is the problem-space, not an animal feature.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Goose! Feathered body, strong legs, wings | one cold-country bird balancing ground and flight needs.
V ... V ... Vole! Small furry mammal stays close to ground and cover | tiny body needs a very different strategy from a whale.
W? Whale! Smooth skin outside, thick insulating tissue inside | tail drives a huge marine body through cold water.
At the warm room, X is Xylophone—our X-word helper | wooden or metal bars have no cold adaptation, only tuned notes.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow-billed Loon! Dense feathers, strong swimming feet, pointed bill | bird body built for northern lakes and coasts.
Z ... Z ... Zoarcid Fish! Long eel-shaped fish lives in cold water | fins and flexible body solve movement without fur or feathers.
