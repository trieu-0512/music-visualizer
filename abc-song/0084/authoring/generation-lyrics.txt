[Intro]
A lizard warms in sun, then chooses shade; a frog may use cool water instead.
Ectotherm does not mean the body stays cold.

[Verse 1]

B ... B ... Boa! Snake thermoregulates by choosing warmer/cooler microhabitats | no internal furnace like a bird or mammal.
C? Crocodile! Basking warms; water can cool or buffer temperature | behavior changes through the day.

[Verse 2]
E — Emerald Tree Boa! Arboreal snake chooses branch positions and microclimates | warmth does not mean endless direct sun.
F ... F ... Frilled Lizard! Australian lizard can bask to warm, then retreat from excess heat | frill is not a thermostat.
G? Garter Snake! May bask in cool conditions and seek shelter when hot | temperature choice changes seasonally.
H is Hellbender. Cool flowing streams buffer this aquatic salamander | ectothermy can be water-based rather than basking.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguana! Basking raises body temperature; shade helps prevent overheating | heat gradient matters in captive care.
J ... J ... Jumping Frog! Amphibian may use water, shade and activity timing to manage heat/moisture.
K? Komodo Dragon! Large ectothermic lizard warms through sun and environment | size affects heating/cooling rate but does not make it endothermic.
L is Lizard. Broad reptile group showing many thermoregulatory strategies | one sun-basking picture is not universal.

S? Skink! Many skinks bask briefly and use cover/burrows | thermal behavior varies by species.

U — Uromastyx! Desert lizard uses strong basking heat but also retreats to burrows | high preferred temperatures still require escape from extremes.
V ... V ... Veiled Chameleon! Moves through sun/shade and changes body orientation/color partly with thermoregulation and signaling.
W? Water Dragon! Water offers a cooling/refuge option beside basking sites | semi-aquatic thermoregulation.
X is Xenosaurus. Crevice-dwelling lizard can use stable rocky microclimates | not every ectotherm sits in open sun.
