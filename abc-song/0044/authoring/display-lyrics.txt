[Intro]
Boot tread, trail mark, one patch of old snow.
We can learn a lot without picking up a single thing.

[Verse 1]
Field note A — Alpine Goat. Sure hooves on steep ground | wildlife evidence belongs at distance, not at the end of our path.
B ... B ... Boulder! Shape, grain, shadow | sketch the large rock and leave it exactly where it sits.
C? Cabin! Human-built shelter becomes a route landmark | useful context, not a natural clue to collect.
D is Deer. Hoofed mammal can leave tracks | identify signs without following the animal.

[Verse 2]
E — Edelweiss! Small pale flower of mountain habitats | observe or photograph, never pluck for the notebook.
F ... F ... Falcon! Fast raptor crosses open sky | one distant motion mark on the field page.
G? Glacier! Thick flowing ice marks a much larger process | route planning respects crevasses and unstable ice.
H is Hiking Boot. Sturdy sole supports feet on rough trail | equipment clue stays on the hiker, not in the ecosystem.

[Chorus]
Notice, mark, leave—A-B-C!
Good scouts learn the clue and leave the mountain free.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Wild goat relative on steep rock | note body shape and distance, then keep the route.
J ... J ... Juniper! Evergreen woody plant | leaf and cone shapes are enough—no cutting needed.
K? Kettle Lake! Basin created after buried glacier ice melted | water becomes a landmark and a geology clue together.
L is Ledge. Narrow rock shelf | if a route reaches exposure, the field note can simply say turn back.

[Verse 4]
M — Marmot! Stocky mountain mammal around burrows and rocks | watch from afar and keep food packed away.
N ... N ... Nutcracker! Mountain bird with strong bill | seed-caching behavior is a clue we observe, not interrupt.
O? Owl! Night-active bird with broad wings and keen senses | a possible sound or sight note, never a nest-search mission.
P is Pine. Needle bundles and woody cones | tree identity comes from features, not souvenirs.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quartz! Hard mineral with glassy luster | point, sketch, leave—the rock stays on the mountain.
R ... R ... Ridge! Long high line guides orientation | skyline becomes a natural map edge.
S? Snow! Ice crystals can linger in shaded mountain patches | condition clue that can change footing and route choice.
T is Tent. Portable human shelter | camping belongs only where rules and suitable sites allow.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Meadow! Open elevated grassland | footsteps stay on durable routes when plants are fragile.
V ... V ... Vulture! Soaring scavenger uses rising air | record the silhouette and let it keep its distance.
W? Waterfall! Sound may arrive before the view | route clue heard around a bend, not a reason to scramble off-trail.
X is Xenolith. Rock fragment inside another rock | compare the inner shape without breaking the stone.

[Chorus]
Notice, mark, leave—A-B-C!
Good scouts learn the clue and leave the mountain free.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarrow! Tiny flowers gather in flat clusters | a leafy meadow plant in the mountain mapping.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Trail! Switchbacks turn repeatedly across a slope | route design becomes the final field-note pattern.
