[Intro]
A penguin has feathers without flying; an ostrich nests on the ground; a woodpecker uses a cavity.
Bird feathers and nests solve different problems.

[Verse 1]
A — Albatross! Long narrow wings suit efficient ocean soaring | dense feathers also insulate the body.
B ... B ... Bluebird! Small thrush; many species nest in cavities | feathers serve flight and insulation, not only blue color.
C? Cardinal! Contour feathers shape the body and provide color signals | cup nests are usually hidden in shrubs or dense growth.
D is Dove. Soft contour feathers cover a compact body | many doves build surprisingly simple platform nests.

[Verse 2]
E — Egret! Long plumes can serve display | wetland birds still depend on ordinary body feathers for flight and insulation.
F ... F ... Flamingo! Feathers gain pink pigments from diet-derived carotenoids | mud mound nests raise eggs above shallow water.
G? Goose! Down feathers insulate beneath outer feathers | ground nests are often lined with down.
H is Heron. Long-legged wader builds stick nests in trees, reeds, or colonies depending species | not one universal nest form.

[Verse 3]
I — Indian Roller! Blue wing patches flash in flight | cavity nesting keeps eggs inside a sheltered space.
J ... J ... Jay! Corvid feathers support agile flight | twig-and-root nests hide in branches.
K? Kingfisher! Compact feathers meet water during plunge dives | many kingfishers excavate nesting tunnels in banks.
L is Lorikeet. Parrot feathers create bright displays | many nest in tree hollows rather than open cups.

[Verse 4]
M — Macaw! Large flight feathers power strong flight | cavity nesting is common among macaws.
N ... N ... Nest! Any bird-built or selected site for eggs and young | cups, cavities, scrapes, burrows and mounds all count.
O? Ostrich! Feathers insulate and display but cannot produce flight | eggs lie in a ground scrape nest.
P is Penguin. Dense overlapping feathers insulate and streamline a swimmer | wings became flippers, yet feathers remain essential.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quetzal! Brilliant body feathers and long tail coverts serve display | nests often occupy cavities in decaying wood.
R ... R ... Raven! Strong flight feathers carry a large corvid | bulky stick nests may sit on cliffs, trees, or structures.
S? Sparrow! Small contour and flight feathers manage heat and motion | nest styles vary from cups to cavities.
T is Toucan. Feathers cover a surprisingly light-bodied bird beneath a huge bill | many toucans use tree cavities for nesting.

[Chorus]

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrellabird! Male crest and throat structures support display | tropical forest bird, not an umbrella-shaped nest builder.
V ... V ... Vulture! Broad flight feathers support soaring | nests may be simple scrapes, ledges, or platforms depending species.
W? Woodpecker! Stiff tail feathers brace against trunks | cavity excavation creates a protected nest chamber.
X is Xantus Hummingbird. Tiny hummingbird with rapid wingbeats | small cup nest is bound with plant fibers and spider silk.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Fine feathers insulate a tiny migrant | cup nest is woven into shrubs or small trees.
Z ... Z ... Zenaida Dove! Dove feathers power direct flight | simple twig nests fit the dove end of our nest spectrum.
