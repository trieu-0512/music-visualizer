[Intro]
A hidden door needs a way to open, a magic key needs a rule, a lantern can simply be a lantern.
Good story magic has limits.

[Verse 1]
A — Amulet! | Small object worn/carried for symbolic, religious, protective, or decorative meaning; magical effects belong to belief/story context.
B ... B ... Bridge! | Structure crosses a gap, river, or road; in a quest it can act as a route choice without being magical.
C? Cauldron! | Large pot historically used for cooking/heating; fantasy stories may make it magical, but a real heated cauldron can burn.
D is Door. Movable barrier controls access to a room/place; a 'magic door' is a plot device unless the story defines otherwise. |

[Verse 2]
E — Elf! | Folklore/fantasy being from Germanic/Norse-derived and later traditions; modern pointed-ear depictions are only one convention.
F ... F ... Forest! | Real ecosystem dominated by trees/woodland vegetation; fairy tales may add fictional beings while the forest itself remains a real habitat type.
G? Giant! | Very large humanoid being in folklore/fantasy; giant size and temperament depend on the tale.
H is Hidden Door. Concealed entrance in a story/building; hidden does not necessarily mean magical. |

[Chorus]
Rule, limit, clue—A-B-C!
Magic works better when the story tells us how.

[Verse 3]
I — Island! | Land surrounded by water; a fantasy island can host invented places while island geography is real.
J ... J ... Jester! | Historical court entertainer associated with comedy, music, performance, or satire; not every jester wore the same costume.
K? Key! | Object or code opens a lock; in stories it can also symbolize permission or discovery.
L is Lantern. Portable light source; historical lanterns used flame while modern ones may use batteries, and fire safety matters. |

[Verse 4]
M — Moon Gate! | Architectural circular opening/gateway associated especially with Chinese gardens; a fantasy story may repurpose it magically.
N ... N ... Noble Crown! | Crown symbolizes royalty/status in stories/history; wearing one does not create authority outside its social context.
O? Orb! | Spherical object; crystal/magic orbs are fantasy divination props unless otherwise specified.
P is Potion. Fantasy liquid with invented effects; real drinks/medicines should never be mixed or consumed as pretend potions. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quill Pen! | Writing tool made historically from a prepared bird feather; it uses ink and requires careful handling.
R ... R ... Royal Throne! | Ceremonial seat associated with a monarch/ruler; it symbolizes office rather than granting power by itself.
S? Spell Book! | Fantasy book containing fictional spells; it is a story device, not a real source of supernatural power.
T is Tower. Tall structure or building section used for defense, observation, bells, living space, or symbolism depending context. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicorn! | Mythical one-horned horse-like creature in European and other later fantasy traditions; it is not a real zoological species.
V ... V ... Village Well! | Well accesses groundwater; real wells are fall/water hazards and children stay behind barriers.
W? Witch! | Figure in folklore, history, religion, and fantasy with widely varying meanings; fantasy witch traits should not be treated as facts about real people.
X is Xylophone Charm. Project fantasy charm shaped like or themed around a xylophone; charm magic is fictional. |

Z ... Z ... Zodiac Amulet! | Fantasy amulet decorated with zodiac symbols; symbols have cultural/astrological meaning but no demonstrated magical force.
