[Intro]
Before play starts: safe size, strong seams, no loose piece to swallow.
A care item is not automatically a toy.

[Verse 1]
A — Angelfish! Fish enrichment comes from suitable habitat complexity, compatible social setup, and room to swim | tapping glass is not play.
B ... B ... Bunny! Rabbits benefit from tunnels, digging, chewing and foraging | lifting or chasing is not enrichment.
C? Collar! Identification/control equipment, not a toy | fit should allow comfort and safe attachment.
D is Dog. Play may include sniffing, tug, retrieve, training games, and social interaction | individual preference and health matter.

[Verse 2]
E — Exercise Wheel! Appropriate for hamsters and some small rodents when solid, stable, and correctly sized | not universal pet equipment.
F ... F ... Fish! Habitat enrichment should not obstruct swimming or damage fins | species behavior guides design.
G? Grooming Brush! Care tool can feel pleasant for some pets, but brushing is not play and must stop if the animal is stressed.
H is Hutch. Housing/shelter structure, not enrichment by itself | rabbits still need far more usable space and activity.

[Verse 3]
I — ID Tag! Safety/identification object | keep it readable and securely attached, never use as a jingle toy.
J ... J ... Jumping Toy! Only useful if designed for the species with safe landing height and no entrapment risk.
K? Kibble! Food can become enrichment through puzzles or training portions | count it within the daily diet.
L is Litter Box. Toilet resource, not toy | clean placement supports normal behavior.

[Chorus]
Safe, sized, supervised—A-B-C!
Play works when the animal can choose and the object fits.

[Verse 4]
M — Meal Bowl! Feeding tool; puzzle feeding may add enrichment, but mealtime nutrition remains the priority.
N ... N ... Nest! Bird or small-animal nesting area/material supports rest or reproduction | do not turn an occupied nest into play.
O? Outdoor Kennel! Containment/shelter area, not a complete play plan | exercise and interaction require more.
P is Puppy. Young dogs need short age-appropriate play, training, sleep and safe socialization | overtired puppies need rest too.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground bird enrichment can include cover, dust-bathing and foraging | handling is not the default game.
R ... R ... Rope Toy! Tug toy needs strong intact fibers and supervision | retire it when strands become swallowable.
S? Scratching Post! Cat enrichment supports scratching and stretching | stable structure lets the cat choose the behavior.
T is Treat. Small reward can support training or foraging | extra calories mean treats are limited, not an unlimited game.

[Verse 6]
U — Undercoat Brush! Grooming tool, not toy | use only on appropriate coat with gentle pressure.
V ... V ... Vet Kit! Health-care context stays out of play | veterinary tools are not pretend pet toys during actual care.
W? Wheel! Exercise device belongs only to species and size for which it is safe | correct diameter and solid surface matter.
X is X-ray Vet Image. Diagnostic image, never a play object | it helps a veterinarian see internal structures.

[Chorus]
Safe, sized, supervised—A-B-C!
Play works when the animal can choose and the object fits.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn Ball! Loose yarn can be swallowed or entangle a cat | use safer designed toys and supervise string-like play.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Finch! Small social bird benefits from flight space, perches, bathing and foraging | forced handling is not enrichment.
