[Intro]
Beat tells when; duration tells how long; pitch tells how high or low.
Rhythm and melody can meet, but they are not the same thing.

[Verse 1]
A — Accordion! | Free-reed instrument; bellows push or pull air through reeds while buttons or keys select notes.
B ... B ... Bell! | Metal idiophone rings when struck or when an internal clapper hits it; shape and size influence pitch.
C? Cello! | Bowed string instrument held upright between the knees; its long strings and large body give a low-rich range.
D is Dulcimer. Name covers related zithers; a hammered dulcimer uses small beaters on stretched strings, while an Appalachian dulcimer is fretted and usually plucked. |

[Verse 2]
E — Electric Piano! | Keyboard instrument using electric/electronic tone generation or amplified pickups rather than an acoustic piano soundboard.
F ... F ... Fiddle! | A violin played in folk or traditional styles; the instrument is not a separate biological type from violin.
G? Guitar! | Plucked string instrument with fretted neck; acoustic and electric versions amplify string vibration differently.
H is Harmonica. Free-reed wind instrument; blowing or drawing air through channels makes metal reeds vibrate. |

I — Irish Whistle! | Simple fipple flute, often six-holed, used in Irish traditional music; finger holes change the air-column length.
J ... J ... Jingle Bells! | Small bells or clusters that ring when shaken; repeated metal impacts create the jingle.
K? Keyboard! | Musical keyboard is a layout of keys used to control pitches; the sound source may be acoustic, electric, or digital.
L is Lute. Plucked string instrument family with a neck and resonating body; many historical and regional forms exist. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quarter Note! | Rhythmic notation symbol whose duration is one beat in common 4/4 counting; it is a note value, not an instrument.
R ... R ... Rhythm Sticks! | Pair of percussion sticks used to tap rhythmic patterns; safe use means striking sticks or approved surfaces, never people.
S? Saxophone! | Single-reed woodwind, usually metal-bodied; reed vibration starts the sound despite its brass appearance.
T is Tambourine. Frame percussion instrument with jingles, sometimes with a drumhead; shaking or striking activates different sounds. |

V ... V ... Violin! | Smallest standard orchestral bowed string instrument; bow friction or plucking starts string vibration.
W? Whistle! | Air blown across an edge or through a fipple creates a high tone; loud whistles should stay away from ears.
X is Xaphoon. Compact single-reed wind instrument designed to produce a saxophone-like tone through a simple cylindrical body. |
