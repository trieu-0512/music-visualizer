[Intro]
Navigation light helps visibility, map helps planning, lifeboat carries trained rescue response.
Rescue starts with preparation and calling the right help—not jumping in.

[Verse 1]
A — Anchor! | Heavy device grips seabed/riverbed to limit vessel drift; dropping/retrieving it requires awareness of depth, bottom, and lines.
B ... B ... Buoy! | Floating marker can indicate channel, hazard, mooring, or information; color/shape meanings depend on local buoyage system.
C? Catamaran! | Boat with two parallel hulls connected by a deck/frame; wide beam can provide stability and space.
D is Dock. Waterside structure/place for mooring and access; wet surfaces, lines, and edges require careful walking. |

[Verse 2]
E — Echo Sounder! | Instrument sends sound pulses downward and times echoes to estimate water depth or detect bottom/fish features.
F ... F ... Fishing Boat! | Vessel used to catch or support harvesting fish; gear and size vary from small boats to large commercial vessels.
G? Gondola! | Traditional Venetian flat-bottomed rowing boat propelled with a single oar from the stern side; not every narrow boat is a gondola.
H is Houseboat. Boat designed primarily for living accommodation; some are mobile while others remain moored. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Boat! | Flexible hull gains shape from pressurized air chambers; puncture risk, capacity, PFDs, and conditions matter.
J ... J ... Jolly Boat! | Traditional small ship's boat/tender; project label should stay historical rather than implying a modern standard type.
K? Keel! | Longitudinal structural/underwater member helps strength and, on many sailboats, stability and resistance to sideways drift.
L is Lifeboat. Rescue/survival craft designed to protect people in emergencies; operation belongs to trained crews and procedures. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marina! | Managed harbor area provides berths and services for recreational/small craft; docks and fuel areas have posted rules.
N ... N ... Navigation Light! | Required vessel lights communicate orientation/status at night or low visibility; patterns depend on vessel/activity rules.
O? Oar! | Long lever works from an oarlock to row; unlike a paddle, it is usually fixed against the boat while stroking.
P is Paddleboat. Boat propelled by human pedaling/paddle wheels or other paddle mechanism depending design; rental rules and PFDs still apply. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quarterdeck! | Traditional ship area on the upper deck toward the stern associated with command/ceremony; exact layout varies by era/vessel.
R ... R ... Raft! | Buoyant platform or simple craft; stability, materials, current, and flotation equipment determine safe use.
S? Submarine! | Submersible vessel controls buoyancy to travel underwater; operation is specialized and not ordinary recreational boating.
T is Trimaran. Multihull craft with one main hull and two smaller outriggers/floats; geometry changes stability and performance. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underwater Scooter! | Diver propulsion vehicle pulls a diver through water; diving training, depth, battery, and entanglement risks require expert use.
V ... V ... Voyage Map! | Navigation/planning map shows route, hazards, places, and scale; real navigation also uses current charts and instruments.
W? Wake! | Disturbed water pattern trails a moving vessel; wake size depends on speed, hull, depth, and conditions.
X is X-ray Sonar Screen. Project label for a sonar display; sonar uses sound, not X-rays, so 'X-ray' describes see-through-like imagery rather than radiation. |

Z ... Z ... Zephyr Sail! | Project poetic label for a sail shown in light wind; zephyr means a gentle breeze, not a standardized sail type.
