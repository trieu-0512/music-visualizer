[Intro]
Read the range map. Watch one behavior. Leave one question unanswered.
A zoo explorer learns without asking an animal to perform.

[Verse 1]
A — Antelope! Read the species name before assuming one antelope body fits all | horns, range and habitat vary.
B ... B ... Bison! North American/European bison species are massive grazers | signage distinguishes them from buffalo.
C? Camel! One or two humps depends on camel species | fat storage, not water tanks.
D is Deer. Antlers occur mainly in males of most deer species and are shed/regrown | sign prevents oversimplification.

[Verse 2]
E — Elephant! Watch trunk exploration, foraging and social behavior | welfare requires complexity and choice.
F ... F ... Flamingo! Filter feeding, preening and flocking reveal more than pink color | distance protects the group.
G? Giraffe! Long tongue and neck support browsing | social and movement space matter beyond height.
H is Hippo. Water use and nighttime grazing shape its behavior | never confuse stillness with harmlessness.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Rocky terrain lets sure-footed movement appear naturally | observe rather than coax climbing.
J ... J ... Jackal! Wild canid behavior may include resting, scent investigation, foraging or social interaction | no dog-like pet expectation.
K? Koala! Long rest periods are normal because eucalyptus diet is low-energy | sleeping is a valid observation.
L is Lemur. Madagascar primates vary widely by species | sign gives exact species before we generalize.

[Chorus]
Read, watch, question—A-B-C!
A hidden animal can still teach us patience.

[Verse 4]
M — Monkey! 'Monkey' is too broad for a field note | write the actual species from signage.
N ... N ... Nyala! Shy woodland antelope may use cover | hidden behavior can be normal and welfare-positive.
O? Ostrich! Flightless running bird needs ground space | wings still serve display/balance roles.
P is Puma. Solitary American cat also called cougar/mountain lion | cover and privacy are important behavior choices.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quokka! Australian marsupial | visitor popularity never makes touching or feeding appropriate.
R ... R ... Red Panda! Arboreal Himalayan mammal | climbing, resting and scent behavior belong in a complex habitat.
S? Serval! African cat with long legs and huge ears | listening and pouncing behavior may appear without visitor prompts.
T is Tiger. Solitary Asian cat | a hidden/resting tiger is not a failed exhibit moment.

[Verse 6]
U — Uakari! Amazonian monkey with unusual short tail and bare face | species sign anchors tropical range.
V ... V ... Vulture! Scavenger ecology is essential ecosystem work | soaring or resting replaces villain stereotypes.
W? Walrus! Arctic marine mammal uses water and haul-out surfaces | tusks are real tools/traits, not props.
X is Xerus. African ground squirrel | digging and social vigilance make small-animal behavior worth watching.

[Chorus]
Read, watch, question—A-B-C!
A hidden animal can still teach us patience.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Mongoose! Southern African ground predator | small size does not make it a pet.
Z ... Z ... Zebu! Domestic cattle type | zoo/farm education should label domestication clearly beside wild animals.

[Call and Response 1]
Field note A ... Antelope! Take two hoof-steps; curve horn shapes.
Sign/behavior B ... Bison! Show massive shoulders and curved horns.
What notice C? ... Camel! Make one hump; take two broad-foot steps.
Explorer note D ... Deer! Take two hoof-steps; branch antler fingers.

[Call and Response 2]
Field note E ... Elephant! Curve a trunk arm; show broad feet.
Sign/behavior F ... Flamingo! Stand one finger-leg tall; bend a filtering bill shape.
What notice G? ... Giraffe! Stretch a long neck; take two tall steps.
Explorer note H ... Hippo! Make a broad body; draw a water line.

[Call and Response 3]
Field note I ... Ibex! Curve horns backward; take two mountain steps.
Sign/behavior J ... Jackal! Show pointed ears, a narrow muzzle, and one bushy-tail sweep.
What notice K? ... Koala! Grip an imaginary tree trunk with both hands.
Explorer note L ... Lemur! Show grasping hands and a long tail.

[Call and Response 4]
Field note M ... Monkey! Show grasping hands, then one agile branch reach.
Sign/behavior N ... Nyala! Trace stripes; curve spiral horn shapes.
What notice O? ... Ostrich! Run two long ground steps—no flying gesture.
Explorer note P ... Puma! Show a tawny cat body and one long balancing tail.

[Call and Response 5]

[Call and Response 6]
