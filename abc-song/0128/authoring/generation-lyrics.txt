[Intro]
Bus reads road signs; dump truck enters a work zone; yacht and zeppelin leave the road map entirely.
Before reading the sign, check whether the vehicle is even on a road.

[Verse 1]
A — ATV! | All-terrain vehicle uses low-pressure tires and straddle seating for off-road travel; it requires trained adult operation, helmet, and approved terrain.
B ... B ... Bus! | Large road passenger vehicle serving routes, schools, charters, or other services; stops and schedules organize shared travel.
C? Cement Mixer! | Truck-mounted rotating drum keeps concrete agitated during transport; drum rotation is a work function, not the vehicle's propulsion.
D is Dump Truck. Truck tips or opens its cargo body to unload loose material; unloading needs a level controlled work zone. |

[Verse 2]
E — Excavator! | Construction machine uses boom, stick, and bucket rotating on an upper structure; it digs rather than simply 'drives'.
F ... F ... Fire Engine! | Emergency vehicle carries firefighters, hoses, pumps, ladders, or equipment; other traffic yields according to local law when emergency signals operate.
G? Golf Cart! | Small low-speed vehicle designed mainly for golf courses or controlled properties; road legality and safety rules vary by location.
H is Hatchback. Passenger car body style with rear door opening into cargo/passenger space. |

[Chorus]
Road, sign, mode—A-B-C!
A road rule only belongs to road traffic.

[Verse 3]
I — Intercity Bus! | Passenger bus links cities over longer routes; service pattern, not body shape alone, makes it intercity.
J ... J ... Jet! | Aircraft propelled by jet engines; jet describes propulsion, not every airplane shape.
K? Kart! | Small low-slung wheeled vehicle used in controlled karting environments; helmet and track rules are essential.
L is Loader. Construction machine scoops and moves loose material with a front bucket; worksite visibility and exclusion zones are critical. |

[Verse 4]

N ... N ... Narrowboat! | Long narrow inland-waterway boat designed for canals; it is water transport, not a road vehicle.
O? Oil Tanker! | Vehicle or vessel carrying bulk oil; the name can mean tanker truck or tanker ship, so the picture decides mode.
P is Plane. Fixed-wing aircraft produces lift as air flows over wings; it belongs to air transport. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quad Bike! | Four-wheeled all-terrain vehicle; it requires trained operation, helmet, and terrain-specific safety.
R ... R ... Road Roller! | Heavy compaction machine presses road base or asphalt with large drums/tires; it is worksite equipment, not ordinary traffic.
S? Scooter! | Name can mean kick scooter or motor scooter; this transport mapping requires the pictured type before assigning propulsion.
T is Tractor. Low-speed high-traction machine pulls or powers farm/utility implements; it is a work vehicle more than passenger transport. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Truck! | Work vehicle carries tools, crews, or service equipment; exact setup varies by utility task.
V ... V ... Van! | Enclosed road vehicle for passengers or cargo; interior configuration defines much of its role.
W? Wrecker! | Tow/recovery truck lifts or pulls disabled vehicles; roadside recovery needs professional traffic control.
X is Xpress Bus. Project label for an express-style bus service; 'Xpress' describes service branding, not a unique vehicle mechanism. |

Y — Yacht! | Recreational sailing or motor vessel; it belongs to water transport and ranges greatly in size.
Z ... Z ... Zeppelin! | Rigid airship uses a structural framework and lifting gas; it is lighter-than-air aviation, not a road vehicle.
