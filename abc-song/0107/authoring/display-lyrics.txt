[Intro]
Before the box opens: what are we making, what surface are we using, and which tool fits?
A small plan keeps a craft from becoming a pile of supplies.

[Verse 1]
A — Apron! | Protective garment helps keep paint or glue off clothing but does not replace hand or surface cleanup.
B ... B ... Brush! | Bristles or fibers carry a medium and release it through strokes; shape and stiffness change the mark.
C? Crayon! | Wax-based color stick leaves pigment by friction and pressure; classroom crayons should be non-toxic.
D is Dot Marker. Broad round applicator makes repeated circular marks; it is a mark-making tool, not a paint stamp for skin. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eraser! | Material lifts many graphite marks; it cannot clean every ink, paint, or surface without damage.
F ... F ... Frame! | Border or support surrounds artwork and helps present or protect it without changing the image itself.
G? Glue! | Adhesive bonds surfaces as it sets; classroom glue should be non-toxic and used in small directed amounts, never tasted.
H is Hole Punch. Lever tool cuts small paper holes; moving metal parts can pinch, so adults manage preschool use. |

[Verse 3]
I — Illustration! | Image explains, decorates, or tells part of a story; it can communicate information without being a photograph.
J ... J ... Jar! | Rigid container stores water or materials; glass jars require adult judgment around young children.
K? Kraft Paper! | Strong brown paper made largely from kraft-process pulp; useful for drawing, wrapping, or sturdy craft shapes.
L is Leaf Print. Print transfers a leaf's outline and vein texture to another surface; the image records surface structure. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marker! | Ink-fed tip makes broad or fine lines; caps help many markers resist drying.
N ... N ... Notebook! | Bound pages collect sketches, plans, notes, or color tests in sequence.
O? Oil Pastel! | Pigment mixed with oil/wax binder makes rich opaque marks that blend differently from chalk pastel.
P is Pencil. Graphite core makes erasable gray marks; pressure and point shape change line value and width. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilt Square! | Fabric patch is one unit in a larger quilt design; seams join many squares into a surface.
R ... R ... Roller! | Cylindrical tool spreads paint or ink across a broad area; pressure changes coverage.
S? Scissors! | Pivoting blades shear material; child-safe scissors still require correct grip, seated use, and adult supervision.
T is Tape. Adhesive strip joins, masks, or temporarily holds materials; different tapes have different tack and surfaces. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Brush! | General-purpose brush can spread paint, glue, or other craft media; exact use depends on its bristles and material.
V ... V ... Varnish! | Clear or tinted finish protects and changes surface sheen; formulations can release fumes, so adult product guidance is essential.
W? Watercolor! | Water-soluble paint can make transparent washes; more water usually lightens and spreads the pigment.
X is X-shaped Sticker. Adhesive sticker carries an X outline; shape is the learning clue, not a cutting instruction. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Paint! | Yellow-colored paint follows the same binder-and-pigment behavior as its paint type; color alone does not define opacity.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Craft! | Project craft uses a zipper as a functional or decorative element; exact construction depends on the designed activity.
