[Intro]
One blade of grass bends while a glacier fills the far horizon.
The alpine meadow is small detail inside a very large place.

[Verse 1]
A — Aspen! Leaves flutter above the meadow edge | one tree-scale frame around smaller grass.
B ... B ... Boulder! Large rock sits among much smaller stems | scale changes before the alphabet does.
C? Cabin! Human shelter belongs near the landscape | it is context, not alpine wildlife or meadow growth.
D is Deer. Hooves carry a large herbivore over land | one moving body beyond our close flower view.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Broad wings cross the far sky | tiny from here, large in reality.
F ... F ... Fir! Evergreen needles hold on branches | taller plant structure beyond low meadow leaves.
G? Glacier! Thick moving ice fills a distant valley | huge background to fragile foreground plants.
H is Hiking Boot. Sturdy human footwear protects feet on rough trails | keep boots on the route, not on meadow flowers.

[Chorus]
Near, far, small, wide—A-B-C!
Meadow detail, mountain distance—hold them both in view.

[Verse 3]
I — Icefall! Broken steep glacier ice flows downhill | jagged far texture behind soft grass.
J ... J ... Jay! Feathered bird hops, perches, then flies | quick motion between near and far.
K? Kettle Lake! A basin linked to old buried glacier ice can hold water | an ice-age shape now reflecting the sky.
L is Lynx. Quiet wild cat moves over land | a distant wildlife clue that we never follow through the meadow.

[Verse 4]
M — Mountain! Massive slopes hold the whole scene | widest scale in the song.
N ... N ... Nutcracker! Mountain bird uses a strong bill to handle and cache seeds | small actions can shape future plant growth.
O? Outcrop! Solid rock exposed at ground surface | hard texture emerging through thin soil.
P is Pine. Needles and woody cones | tree-scale green beyond the meadow layer.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quartz! Hard mineral can flash glassy light | tiny bright detail inside a boulder-sized world.
R ... R ... Rope! Strong flexible equipment used by people | identify it safely; the song never invites climbing practice.
S? Summit! Highest point on the mountain | far above our low meadow viewpoint.
T is Tent. Portable fabric shelter for people | set on suitable ground, not on fragile plants.

[Chorus]
Near, far, small, wide—A-B-C!
Meadow detail, mountain distance—hold them both in view.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Meadow! Open grassy or flower-rich area at elevation | the central habitat frame of the song.
V ... V ... Valley! Low land between higher slopes | broad distance cue beyond close grass.
W? Wolf! Wild canid travels over land | observe from distance and never turn a meadow walk into tracking wildlife.
X is Xenolith. A rock fragment trapped inside another rock | a different stone story within the mountain wall.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yak! Large long-haired bovine of high Asian regions | one high-country animal, not a universal alpine species.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Trail! Switchbacks bend across the slope | a route that makes a steep climb gentler.
