[Intro]
A quest needs a goal, route, clues, choices, and a reason to return; a sword is not a solution by itself.
Adventure can move forward by thinking, asking, or turning back.

[Verse 1]
A — Arrow! | Projectile shot from a bow; weapon item remains picture-only in preschool fantasy play.
B ... B ... Bridge! | Structure crosses a gap, river, or road; in a quest it can act as a route choice without being magical.
C? Cloak! | Loose outer garment; in stories it can signal role or concealment, but real fabric has no magic.
D is Dragon. Mythical creature appearing in many cultures with very different forms and meanings; no single universal dragon design exists. |

[Verse 2]
E — Enchanted Book! | Fantasy story object whose magic comes from the fiction; a real book does not cast spells.
F ... F ... Fairy! | Folklore/fantasy being whose traits vary across traditions; wings and tiny size are not universal features.
G? Goblin! | Folklore/fantasy creature with many regional/literary versions; appearance and behavior depend on the story.
H is Hero. Story role for a central character facing challenges; courage can include help, planning, retreat, or negotiation rather than fighting. |

[Verse 3]
I — Island! | Land surrounded by water; a fantasy island can host invented places while island geography is real.
J ... J ... Jewel! | Cut/polished gemstone or decorative imitation; story jewels may mark clues/status but do not inherently possess magic.
K? King! | Male monarch title in some political systems; story kings vary widely and do not define real governance universally.
L is Lantern. Portable light source; historical lanterns used flame while modern ones may use batteries, and fire safety matters. |

[Verse 4]

O? Orb! | Spherical object; crystal/magic orbs are fantasy divination props unless otherwise specified.
P is Potion. Fantasy liquid with invented effects; real drinks/medicines should never be mixed or consumed as pretend potions. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quest Map! | Story map marks destinations, hazards, or clues for a fictional quest; plot information can be incomplete or misleading by design.
R ... R ... Royal Banner! | Flag/banner represents a ruler, house, place, or group in a story; symbols communicate identity rather than magic.
S? Sword! | Edged historical weapon; in this preschool story set it stays a picture/symbol and is never mimed as an attack.
T is Troll. Folklore creature from Northern European traditions with many later fantasy reinterpretations. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicorn! | Mythical one-horned horse-like creature in European and other later fantasy traditions; it is not a real zoological species.
V ... V ... Village! | Settlement smaller than many towns/cities; in fairy tales it often anchors ordinary life before adventure.
W? Witch! | Figure in folklore, history, religion, and fantasy with widely varying meanings; fantasy witch traits should not be treated as facts about real people.
X is X-shaped Gem. Project gem with an X-like cut/outline; the X shape is a clue, not evidence of magical properties. |
