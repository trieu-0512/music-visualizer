[Intro]
Lion in Africa; Puma in the Americas; Koala in Australia.
Wildlife shares one planet without sharing one neighborhood.

[Verse 1]
A — Antelope! Broad hoofed-animal term mainly across Africa/Eurasia | many unrelated-looking species share grazing/browsing roles.
B ... B ... Baboon! African/Arabian primate | ground travel contrasts with arboreal monkeys elsewhere.
C? Camel! Dromedary roots in arid Africa/Arabia; Bactrian camels in Central Asia | one word spans two camel species.
D is Deer. Deer family ranges across much of Eurasia and the Americas | species replace each other geographically.

[Verse 2]
E — Emu! Australian flightless bird | geographic isolation helped produce a distinctive large-running bird fauna.
F ... F ... Fox! Foxes occupy several continents | species and climate define the actual range.
G? Gorilla! Central African great ape | no wild gorillas outside Africa.
H is Hyena. Hyenas are Old World carnivores, with living species centered in Africa and parts of Asia.

[Chorus]
Range, region, compare—A-B-C!
Same planet, different maps.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Wild goats of Eurasian/African mountain systems depending species | high rock repeats across regions.
J ... J ... Jackal! Old World canids across Africa/Eurasia | different from coyotes of the Americas.
K? Koala! Native to eastern/southeastern Australia | eucalyptus specialization anchors the range.
L is Lion. Native wild lions survive mainly in sub-Saharan Africa plus a small population in India | range is not simply 'Africa only'.

[Verse 4]
M — Monkey! Broad term includes New World and Old World lineages | geography helps separate them.
N ... N ... Nyala! Southern African antelope | regional range much smaller than the word antelope suggests.
O? Okapi! Endemic to rainforests of the Democratic Republic of the Congo | giraffe relative with a narrow African range.
P is Panda. Giant panda is native to central Chinese mountain forests | not a global bamboo animal.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quokka! Southwestern Australian marsupial, especially famous on offshore islands | tiny geographic footprint.
R ... R ... Rhino! Living rhinos occur naturally in parts of Africa and Asia | two continents, several species.
S? Serval! African spotted cat tied strongly to grassland/wetland mosaics | different continent from puma.
T is Tapir. Tapirs occur in Central/South America and Southeast Asia | separated lineages on distant continents.

[Chorus]
Range, region, compare—A-B-C!
Same planet, different maps.

[Verse 6]
U — Urial! Central/South Asian wild sheep | high dry range contrasts with Andean Vicuna.
V ... V ... Vicuna! Wild South American camelid of the Andes | continent and elevation define it.
W? Wolf! Gray wolves range across portions of Eurasia and North America | broad northern distribution.
X is Xenopus. African aquatic frog genus | freshwater range keeps X grounded in Africa.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Mongoose! Southern African species | another compact regional range.
Z ... Z ... Zebu! Domestic humped cattle spread widely through human agriculture | current distribution reflects people as much as wild biogeography.
