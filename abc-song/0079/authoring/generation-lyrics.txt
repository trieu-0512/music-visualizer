[Intro]
Boom-ba, boom-ba—soft safari drum.
Listen for the letter first; then let the animal come.

[Verse 1]
A—Antelope! Antelope on the track | hoof and horn or head clues bring the answer back
B ... B ... Baboon. A baboon is a ground-traveling monkey with a long muzzle and strong limbs.
Camel steps in for C. It has broad padded feet and one or two fat-storing humps depending on the species.
What does D reveal? Deer! A deer has hooves and slender legs; males of many species grow antlers.

[Verse 2]
beside the soft field-note drum, E meets Emu. Emu in its place | wings and legs give clues to trace
F—Fox! It has a pointed muzzle, upright ears, light paws, and a bushy tail.
G ... G ... Gorilla. A gorilla is the largest living primate, with long arms and a powerful knuckle-walking body.
Hyena steps in for H. It has strong jaws, rounded ears, and a back that often slopes downward toward the hindquarters.

[Chorus]

Wild-world chorus, A to Z!

[Verse 3]
What does I reveal? Ibex! Ibex on the track | hoof and horn or head clues bring the answer back
beside the soft field-note drum, J meets Jackal. It is a slender wild canid with pointed ears, a narrow muzzle, and a bushy tail.
K—Koala! It is an Australian marsupial that climbs eucalyptus trees and eats mainly eucalyptus leaves.
L ... L ... Lion. A lion is a large cat with a tawny coat; adult males often grow a prominent mane.

[Verse 4]
Monkey steps in for M. Monkey on the scene | face, hands, and agile limbs keep the meaning clean
What does N reveal? Nyala! A nyala is an African antelope with pale vertical body stripes; adult males have long spiral horns.
beside the soft field-note drum, O meets Okapi. It is a forest-dwelling relative of the giraffe with striped markings on its legs.
P—Panda! A giant panda is a bear with black-and-white fur that eats mostly bamboo.

[Verse 5]
Q ... Q ... Quokka. Quokka from afar | shape and habitat show what you are
Rhino steps in for R. A rhinoceros is a large thick-skinned mammal with one or two horns made of keratin on its snout.
What does S reveal? Serval! A serval is a long-legged spotted African cat with especially large ears.
beside the soft field-note drum, T meets Tapir. It is a plant-eating mammal with a short flexible snout and sturdy body.

[Chorus]
Soft drum, clear name—one-two-three!
Wild-world chorus, A to Z!

[Verse 6]
U—Urial! Urial in its place | sturdy legs and hoofed feet give clues to trace
V ... V ... Vicuna. A vicuna is a slender South American camelid adapted to high Andean grasslands.
Wolf steps in for W. It is a large wild canid with long legs, a strong muzzle, upright ears, and a bushy tail.
What does X reveal? Xenopus! Xenopus is an aquatic frog with webbed hind feet and eyes positioned high on its head.

[Verse 7]
beside the soft field-note drum, Y meets Yellow Mongoose. Yellow Mongoose in its place | body and habitat give clues to trace
Z—Zebu! It is a type of cattle with a noticeable hump over the shoulders.
