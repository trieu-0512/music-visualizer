[Intro]
A pencil idea can change after the first piece of felt bends or the first strip of tape pulls.
Making is a loop: plan, build, look, revise.

[Verse 1]
A — Apron! | Protective garment helps keep paint or glue off clothing but does not replace hand or surface cleanup.
B ... B ... Bead! | Small pierced piece can be threaded for decoration; tiny beads are choking hazards and need close supervision.
C? Canvas! | Woven fabric or prepared board provides a durable painting surface; priming changes absorbency.
D is Drawing. Image built mainly from lines, shapes, values, or marks rather than a single specific tool. |

[Verse 2]
E — Easel! | Upright stand holds paper, board, or canvas at a working angle.
F ... F ... Felt! | Nonwoven textile made by matting fibers; edges do not fray like many woven fabrics.
G? Glue! | Adhesive bonds surfaces as it sets; classroom glue should be non-toxic and used in small directed amounts, never tasted.
H is Hole Punch. Lever tool cuts small paper holes; moving metal parts can pinch, so adults manage preschool use. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 3]
I — Ink! | Pigment- or dye-carrying liquid makes marks and can stain; containers stay closed when not in use.
J ... J ... Jewel Sticker! | Adhesive decorative gem-like piece adds texture and shine; small pieces require supervision.
K? Knitting Needle! | Long pointed tool forms knitted loops with yarn; preschoolers observe or use only with direct adult guidance.
L is Leaf Print. Print transfers a leaf's outline and vein texture to another surface; the image records surface structure. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marker! | Ink-fed tip makes broad or fine lines; caps help many markers resist drying.
N ... N ... Needle! | Thin pointed sewing tool passes thread through material; sharp point makes it adult-managed in preschool art.
O? Oil Pastel! | Pigment mixed with oil/wax binder makes rich opaque marks that blend differently from chalk pastel.
P is Pencil. Graphite core makes erasable gray marks; pressure and point shape change line value and width. |

[Chorus]
Plan, build, revise—A-B-C!
A good maker can change the next step.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilling Paper! | Narrow paper strips are coiled, pinched, and arranged into decorative shapes.
R ... R ... Roller! | Cylindrical tool spreads paint or ink across a broad area; pressure changes coverage.
S? Sponge! | Porous material absorbs and releases paint, making soft blocks, dabs, or textures when pressed.
T is Tape. Adhesive strip joins, masks, or temporarily holds materials; different tapes have different tack and surfaces. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unfinished Wood! | Bare wood has no final protective coating and may have rough fibers or splinters; adult checks it before craft use.
V ... V ... Varnish! | Clear or tinted finish protects and changes surface sheen; formulations can release fumes, so adult product guidance is essential.
W? Wax Crayon! | Pigment in wax binder transfers through rubbing and pressure; it resists watery paint in wax-resist techniques.
X is Xylophone Stamp. Project stamp makes a xylophone-shaped printed image; the stamp is an art tool, not the musical instrument itself. |

[Chorus]
Plan, build, revise—A-B-C!
A good maker can change the next step.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Paint! | Yellow-colored paint follows the same binder-and-pigment behavior as its paint type; color alone does not define opacity.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Craft! | Project craft uses a zipper as a functional or decorative element; exact construction depends on the designed activity.
