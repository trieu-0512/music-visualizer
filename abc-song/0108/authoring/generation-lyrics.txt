[Intro]
A brush pulls a stroke, a sponge can dab, a leaf can print, a roller can lay a band.
Different motions leave different visual evidence.

[Verse 1]
A — Art Board! | Rigid backing supports paper or artwork and keeps the working surface flat.
B ... B ... Brush! | Bristles or fibers carry a medium and release it through strokes; shape and stiffness change the mark.
C? Canvas! | Woven fabric or prepared board provides a durable painting surface; priming changes absorbency.
D is Drawing. Image built mainly from lines, shapes, values, or marks rather than a single specific tool. |

[Verse 2]
E — Easel! | Upright stand holds paper, board, or canvas at a working angle.
F ... F ... Felt! | Nonwoven textile made by matting fibers; edges do not fray like many woven fabrics.
G? Glitter! | Tiny reflective particles add sparkle but can irritate eyes or spread easily; use supervised craft material and avoid inhalation.
H is Handprint. Printed hand silhouette records palm-and-finger shape; use washable skin-safe material and wash afterward. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 3]
I — Illustration! | Image explains, decorates, or tells part of a story; it can communicate information without being a photograph.
J ... J ... Jar! | Rigid container stores water or materials; glass jars require adult judgment around young children.
K? Knitting Needle! | Long pointed tool forms knitted loops with yarn; preschoolers observe or use only with direct adult guidance.
L is Leaf Print. Print transfers a leaf's outline and vein texture to another surface; the image records surface structure. |

[Verse 4]
M — Modeling Clay! | Malleable material changes form under pressing, rolling, pinching, and joining; it hardens only if its formulation is designed to.
N ... N ... Needle! | Thin pointed sewing tool passes thread through material; sharp point makes it adult-managed in preschool art.
O? Oil Pastel! | Pigment mixed with oil/wax binder makes rich opaque marks that blend differently from chalk pastel.
P is Paint. Pigment in a binder forms a colored layer on a surface; opacity, drying, and cleanup depend on paint type. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilling Paper! | Narrow paper strips are coiled, pinched, and arranged into decorative shapes.
R ... R ... Roller! | Cylindrical tool spreads paint or ink across a broad area; pressure changes coverage.
S? Scissors! | Pivoting blades shear material; child-safe scissors still require correct grip, seated use, and adult supervision.
T is Tempera Paint. Water-based opaque school paint dries matte and cleans differently from permanent artist paints. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Brush! | General-purpose brush can spread paint, glue, or other craft media; exact use depends on its bristles and material.
V ... V ... Varnish! | Clear or tinted finish protects and changes surface sheen; formulations can release fumes, so adult product guidance is essential.
W? Wax Crayon! | Pigment in wax binder transfers through rubbing and pressure; it resists watery paint in wax-resist techniques.
X is X-shaped Sticker. Adhesive sticker carries an X outline; shape is the learning clue, not a cutting instruction. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn! | Twisted fiber strand can wrap, weave, or knit; loose lengths stay supervised to prevent tangling.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Scissors! | Patterned blades cut a zigzag edge; they remain scissors and require supervised safe handling.
