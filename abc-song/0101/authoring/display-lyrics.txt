[Intro]
Backpack by the hook, paper at the work table, globe on the reference shelf.
A classroom gets easier to use when every object has a place and a purpose.

[Verse 1]
A — Art Paper! | Drawing/craft paper; thickness and surface vary by medium.
B ... B ... Backpack! | School bag; two fitted shoulder straps help spread a sensible load.
C? Crayon! | Colored wax-based drawing stick makes broad marks without liquid ink; classroom versions should be age-appropriate and non-toxic.
D is Dictionary. Word reference gives spelling, meaning, pronunciation, and usage. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eraser! | Rubber-like material lifts many graphite marks from paper; it does not erase every kind of ink or damage-free mark.
F ... F ... Folder! | Folded cover keeps loose papers together and separates subjects or projects.
G? Globe! | Spherical Earth model reduces many shape distortions of flat maps, while remaining tiny-scale.
H is Highlighter. Translucent marker emphasizes selected text while leaving words visible; highlighting everything defeats its purpose. |

[Chorus]
Place, use, return—A-B-C!
Find the tool, then put the idea back where it belongs.

[Verse 3]
I — Ink! | Colored liquid or paste carries pigment or dye to a surface and can stain, so lids and careful handling matter.
J ... J ... Jigsaw Map! | Map puzzle breaks a geographic picture into interlocking pieces; matching shape and location rebuilds the whole.
K? Knowledge Card! | Project card holds a fact, clue, or concept; it is not one standardized product.
L is Lunchbox. Food container; safe temperature control depends on contents and storage. |

[Verse 4]
M — Map! | Place model uses symbols, direction, scale, and selected features.
N ... N ... Number Chart! | Ordered grid or line displays numerals so learners can notice sequence, place, and numerical patterns.
O? Organizer! | Compartments or labeled sections keep supplies separated so items can be found and returned efficiently.
P is Pencil. Writing tool usually contains a graphite-and-clay core, not metallic lead; pressure changes line darkness. |

[Chorus]
Place, use, return—A-B-C!
Find the tool, then put the idea back where it belongs.

[Verse 5]
Q — Question Card! | Prompt asks for recall, explanation, comparison, or discussion; it is not the answer.
R ... R ... Ruler! | Straight tool marks units such as centimeters or inches; measure from zero.
S? Scissors! | Two pivoting blades cut material by shearing; preschool use requires suitable blunt-tip tools and adult supervision.
T is Tape. Flexible strip carries adhesive on one or both sides to join or hold materials temporarily or permanently. |

[Verse 6]
U — USB Drive! | Flash-memory device stores files; supervised computer use prevents loss or mishandling.
V ... V ... Violin Case! | Protective container supports and shields a violin and bow during storage or transport; the case itself makes no musical pitch.
W? Whiteboard! | Smooth reusable writing surface accepts dry-erase markers and can be wiped for the next idea.
X is Xylophone. Tuned-bar percussion instrument; shorter matching bars generally sound higher. |

[Chorus]
Place, use, return—A-B-C!
Find the tool, then put the idea back where it belongs.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Pencil! | A pencil with a yellow-painted body still writes through its graphite core; body color does not change the writing mechanism.
Z ... Z ... Zero Card! | Card shows 0: no counted items in some contexts and a place-holding digit in others.
