[Intro]
Two rails guide wheels; a hopper carries bulk; a yard engine rearranges cars behind the fence.
Trackside learning happens from a safe public place, never on the track.

[Verse 1]
A — Automatic Gate! | Powered gate opens or closes access when a valid condition is met; at stations it may control fares or passenger flow, while crossing gates protect road-rail intersections.
B ... B ... Boxcar! | Enclosed freight car with sliding/plug doors protects general cargo from weather.
C? Coach! | Passenger rail car, especially for seated accommodation; coach is not the locomotive.
D is Diesel Engine. Compression-ignition engine burns diesel fuel; diesel locomotives often use it to drive a generator feeding electric traction motors. |

[Verse 2]
E — Electric Train! | Train draws electrical energy from overhead wire, third rail, batteries, or other sources depending system; traction motors turn wheels.
F ... F ... Freight Car! | Rail vehicle carries goods rather than passengers; boxcars, hoppers, tank cars, flats, and other types fit different loads.
G? Gate! | Rail/station gate controls access or protects a crossing; the pictured location determines its exact job.
H is Hopper Car. Freight car with sloped bottom gates carries loose bulk material. |

[Chorus]
Look, line, signal—A-B-C!
Observe the railway without entering its path.

[Verse 3]
I — Information Board! | Station display gives routes, times, platforms, rules, or notices; passengers use it to plan rather than guess track access.
J ... J ... Junction! | Location where rail routes meet, split, or cross; switches and signals route trains safely.
K? Key Switch! | Locked/key-operated control allows authorized operation of equipment; passengers do not operate railway control switches.
L is Level Crossing. Place where road/path crosses railway at the same level; users obey lights, bells, barriers, and never go around a closed gate. |

[Verse 4]
M — Monorail! | Railway whose vehicles are guided by or suspended from a single beam/rail structure rather than a conventional pair of running rails.
N ... N ... Night Train! | Train service scheduled overnight; may include ordinary seats or sleeping accommodation depending route.
O? Observation Car! | Passenger car designed with large windows or viewing areas for scenery; it is a service car, not a driving cab.
P is Platform. Raised or level boarding area beside a track; passengers stay behind edge markings until safe boarding. |

[Chorus]
Look, line, signal—A-B-C!
Observe the railway without entering its path.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiet Car! | Passenger area with reduced conversation/device noise under operator rules.
R ... R ... Railway Signal! | Visual/electronic signal communicates movement authority or restrictions to train crews; meanings depend on the railway rulebook.
S? Subway! | Urban rapid-transit railway that may run underground, elevated, or at surface; 'subway' does not mean every section is tunneled.
T is Train. One or more rail vehicles operating together on a guided track system; signals and timetable/dispatch control safe separation. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underground Train! | Train operating on an underground/subway system or tunneled section; stations and signals govern movement.
V ... V ... Viaduct! | Long bridge-like structure carries rail or road across a valley, streets, or low ground using repeated spans/supports.
W? Waiting Room! | Station room provides sheltered waiting; official signs still guide boarding.
X is X-crossing Sign. X-shaped railway-crossing warning sign marks a road-rail crossing; it warns road users, not trains to cross freely. |

[Chorus]
Look, line, signal—A-B-C!
Observe the railway without entering its path.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yard Engine! | Locomotive switches/assembles cars in a restricted rail yard.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Track! | Project label for winding track; real railways use engineered curves, not sharp toy zigzags.
