[Intro]
Wood block starts dry and short; bell rings on; harmonica carries a reed tone.
A sound has a beginning, a body, and a way of fading.

[Verse 1]
A — Accordion! | Free-reed instrument; bellows push or pull air through reeds while buttons or keys select notes.
B ... B ... Bongos! | Pair of small open-bottom hand drums of different sizes; striking the heads produces contrasting pitches.
C? Clarinet! | Single-reed woodwind with cylindrical bore; covered/open holes and keys change the effective air-column length.
D is Drum. Membranophone whose stretched head vibrates when struck; drum shape and tension affect tone. |

[Verse 2]
E — Euphonium! | Low brass instrument with valves and a conical bore; buzzing lips start the sound.
F ... F ... Flute! | Side-blown woodwind in the concert family; an air jet across the embouchure hole excites the air column.
G? Guitar! | Plucked string instrument with fretted neck; acoustic and electric versions amplify string vibration differently.
H is Harmonica. Free-reed wind instrument; blowing or drawing air through channels makes metal reeds vibrate. |

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument! | General word for a device used to make or control music; it is a category, not one sound or family.
J ... J ... Jaw Harp! | Small lamellophone held at the mouth; a flexible tongue is plucked while the mouth cavity shapes the tone, without biting the reed.
K? Kazoo! | Membrane instrument that changes a hummed voice through a vibrating membrane; blowing silently through it does not create the usual kazoo sound.
L is Lute. Plucked string instrument family with a neck and resonating body; many historical and regional forms exist. |

[Verse 4]
M — Maracas! | Rattles with loose material inside hollow shells; shaking makes particles strike the walls.
N ... N ... Note Card! | Learning card that displays a note symbol or musical prompt; it represents music but makes no sound itself.
O? Organ! | Keyboard instrument that sustains tones using pipes or electronic sound generation; keys can control more than one rank or voice.
P is Piccolo. Small transverse flute pitched an octave above the concert flute in standard orchestral use. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quena! | Traditional Andean end-blown flute with a notched rim and finger holes.
R ... R ... Rhythm Sticks! | Pair of percussion sticks used to tap rhythmic patterns; safe use means striking sticks or approved surfaces, never people.
S? Shaker! | Percussion instrument with loose particles or moving parts that sound when the body is shaken.
T is Tambourine. Frame percussion instrument with jingles, sometimes with a drumhead; shaking or striking activates different sounds. |

[Verse 6]
U — Udu Drum! | Nigerian clay pot percussion instrument; hands strike the body and opening to create resonant tones.
V ... V ... Violin! | Smallest standard orchestral bowed string instrument; bow friction or plucking starts string vibration.
W? Wood Block! | Hollow or resonant wooden percussion block; a beater strike produces a short dry tone.
X is Xylophone. Tuned wooden-bar percussion instrument; bars are struck with mallets and arranged by pitch. |

Y — Yangqin! | Chinese hammered dulcimer; bamboo beaters strike many strings stretched over a trapezoidal soundboard.
Z ... Z ... Zills! | Small finger cymbals used in pairs; metal discs ring when brought together.
