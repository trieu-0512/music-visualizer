[Intro]
A dictionary helps us look up; a journal helps us record; a flashcard asks us to remember before we peek.
Learning tools do different jobs for the mind.

[Verse 1]
A — Alphabet Chart! | Chart displays letter forms and sequence; it can support recognition without replacing sound, writing, or word practice.
B ... B ... Book! | Pages can carry stories, facts, images, or instructions; contents tell the purpose.
C? Crayon! | Colored wax-based drawing stick makes broad marks without liquid ink; classroom versions should be age-appropriate and non-toxic.
D is Dictionary. Word reference gives spelling, meaning, pronunciation, and usage. |

[Verse 2]
E — Envelope! | Folded paper container protects and carries a letter or small flat item; flap closes the opening.
F ... F ... Flashcard! | Study card pairs a prompt with an answer; recall before peeking.
G? Glue! | Adhesive joins materials after it spreads and sets; classroom glue should be non-toxic and used only as directed, never tasted.
H is Highlighter. Translucent marker emphasizes selected text while leaving words visible; highlighting everything defeats its purpose. |

[Chorus]
Find, record, recall—A-B-C!
Choose the tool that fits the thinking job.

[Verse 3]
I — Index Card! | Small stiff card gives limited writing space for one note, label, example, or study prompt.
J ... J ... Journal! | Record collects thoughts, observations, or events over time.
K? Keyboard! | Here it is a computer input device for letters, numbers, and commands?not a musical keyboard.
L is Library Book. Shared book is borrowed for a period and returned so another reader can use the same copy. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marker! | Fiber- or felt-tip writing tool feeds ink to a broad or fine point; cap care helps keep many markers from drying out.
N ... N ... Number Chart! | Ordered grid or line displays numerals so learners can notice sequence, place, and numerical patterns.
O? Origami Paper! | Light square folding paper; classic origami builds forms mainly with creases.
P is Paintbrush. Bristles or synthetic fibers carry paint and release it with strokes; brush shape changes the kind of mark. |

[Chorus]
Find, record, recall—A-B-C!
Choose the tool that fits the thinking job.

[Verse 5]
Q — Question Card! | Prompt asks for recall, explanation, comparison, or discussion; it is not the answer.
R ... R ... Reading Book! | Book selected for reading practice or enjoyment; text difficulty and purpose matter more than the cover.
S? Scissors! | Two pivoting blades cut material by shearing; preschool use requires suitable blunt-tip tools and adult supervision.
T is Tape. Flexible strip carries adhesive on one or both sides to join or hold materials temporarily or permanently. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uniform! | Standardized clothing identifies a school or group; it is part of school routine, not a learning tool by itself.
V ... V ... Vocabulary Card! | Card links a word to meaning, example, picture, or pronunciation.
W? Whiteboard! | Smooth reusable writing surface accepts dry-erase markers and can be wiped for the next idea.
X is X-axis Graph. Horizontal Cartesian axis usually carries x-values paired with vertical y-values. |

[Chorus]
Find, record, recall—A-B-C!
Choose the tool that fits the thinking job.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Pencil! | A pencil with a yellow-painted body still writes through its graphite core; body color does not change the writing mechanism.
Z ... Z ... Zero Card! | Card shows 0: no counted items in some contexts and a place-holding digit in others.
