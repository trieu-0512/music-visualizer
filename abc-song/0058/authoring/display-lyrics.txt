[Intro]
Skeleton on the left, painted reconstruction on the right.
We ask which details are fossil facts and which are careful inference.

[Verse 1]
A — Ankylosaurus! Armor and tail club come from fossils | skin color in a painting is usually reconstruction.
B ... B ... Brachiosaurus! Long neck and longer forelimbs shape the skeleton | posture is reconstructed from joints and comparisons.
C? Carnotaurus! Two brow horns and extremely reduced forelimbs are fossil-supported | exact colors remain artistic.
D is Diplodocus. Long neck, whip-like tail, and peg-like front teeth come from fossils | living movement is reconstructed from anatomy.

[Verse 2]
E — Egg! Fossil eggs preserve shell, shape, and nest context | embryo fossils can sometimes identify the animal more directly.
F ... F ... Fossil! Preserved body part or trace is the evidence layer beneath every gallery reconstruction.
G? Gastonia! Armor plates and spikes are fossil features | spacing and soft-tissue appearance require reconstruction.
H is Herrerasaurus. Triassic predatory dinosaur with grasping hands and long hind limbs | an early dinosaur body plan, not a Jurassic movie monster.

[Chorus]
Bone, infer, draw—A-B-C!
A good picture shows evidence without pretending we know every color.

[Verse 3]
I — Irritator! Spinosaurid with long narrow snout | much of the full-body image must be inferred from relatives because remains are incomplete.
J ... J ... Jurassic Fern! Ferns and fern-like plants help build habitat scenes | background plants need their own fossil evidence too.
K? Kritosaurus! Hadrosaurid known from incomplete skull and skeleton material | restorations must avoid filling gaps with certainty.
L is Lambeosaurus. Hollow head crest is fossil-supported | sound-production ideas are hypotheses tested from anatomy, not recorded calls.

[Verse 4]
M — Maiasaura! Nesting colonies and growth series support parental-care interpretations | behavior picture has unusually rich evidence.
N ... N ... Nest! Fossil nests and eggs are trace/body evidence together | arrangement can reveal breeding behavior.
O? Ornithomimus! Long-legged theropod with feather evidence | museum art should not default to bare reptile skin.
P is Pteranodon. Toothless flying pterosaur with long crest | pterosaur, not dinosaur, with membrane wings.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quetzalcoatlus! Giant azhdarchid pterosaur | enormous winged reptile, not a feathered dinosaur or bird.
R ... R ... Rib Bone! One structural element of the chest | picture scale depends on fitting many bones together correctly.
S? Stegosaurus! Plates and tail spikes are clear fossil features | plate color and display patterns are less certain.
T is Tyrannosaurus. Huge skull, deep jaws, two-fingered forelimbs | feathers in adults remain a nuanced evidence question, not a simple all-or-none claim.

[Chorus]
Bone, infer, draw—A-B-C!
A good picture shows evidence without pretending we know every color.

[Verse 6]
U — Utahraptor! Large dromaeosaurid | feathered reconstruction is supported by its place among feathered theropods.
V ... V ... Velociraptor! Small dromaeosaurid with quill knobs on the forearm in fossils | direct evidence supports feathers.
W? Wannanosaurus! Small pachycephalosaur relative from China | fragmentary remains mean the gallery picture should stay modest.
X is Xixiasaurus. Small theropod known from partial skull and skeleton | image fills missing body parts using close relatives.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yutyrannus! Large tyrannosauroid preserved with filamentous feathers | direct fossil evidence changes the picture dramatically.
Z ... Z ... Zuniceratops! Early ceratopsian with brow horns but simpler frill than later giant ceratopsids | transitional body plan in the gallery.
