[Intro]
Six legs under the lens—then eight legs appear in the next frame.
Count before you call every tiny creature an insect.

[Verse 1]
A — Ant! Six-legged social insect; workers in many species are wingless | antennae bend from the head.
B ... B ... Bee! Six-legged insect with two pairs of wings in adults | many species carry pollen while visiting flowers.
C? Caterpillar! Larval butterfly or moth | six true legs plus fleshy prolegs, so visible 'legs' need careful counting.
D is Dung Beetle. Beetle means hardened elytra over flight wings | dung use is ecology, beetle anatomy is classification.

[Verse 2]
E — Emperor Moth! Large moth with scaled wings | adult is insect; caterpillar was its larval stage.
F ... F ... Flea! Wingless insect compressed side-to-side with powerful jumping legs | still six-legged despite no wings.
G? Grasshopper! Insect with enlarged hind legs for jumping | nymphs resemble smaller wingless adults.
H is Honeybee. Insect with six legs, antennae and two pairs of wings | colony role does not change insect anatomy.

[Chorus]
Count, check, classify—A-B-C!
Legs, wings, antennae tell us which group we see.

[Verse 3]
I — Insect! Adult body plan has head, thorax, abdomen and six legs | wings may be present or absent.
J ... J ... June Bug! Scarab beetle | hardened elytra identify the beetle branch.
K? Katydid! Long-antennaed orthopteran related to crickets | powerful hind legs and leaflike camouflage are common.
L is Leafhopper. Small sap-feeding hemipteran | piercing mouthparts and jumping legs fit its insect group.

[Verse 4]
M — Mantis! Predatory insect with grasping forelegs | six legs total, even when two are specialized as traps.
N ... N ... Nymph! Immature stage in incomplete metamorphosis | same broad insect body plan develops without a pupal stage.
O? Orb Weaver! Spider that builds wheel-shaped webs | arachnid with eight legs, not an insect.
P is Pill Bug. Terrestrial isopod crustacean with many jointed legs and armored segments | not an insect despite living under garden debris.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Butterfly! Butterfly insect with scaled wings | the word queen is part of the species common name, not a colony caste.
R ... R ... Rhinoceros Beetle! Scarab beetle; males of many species bear large horns | horn grows from the exoskeleton.
S? Stick Insect! Six-legged insect shaped like a twig | camouflage can hide the leg count at first glance.
T is Termite. Social insect in the cockroach lineage | workers, soldiers, and reproductives share insect ancestry.

[Verse 6]
U — Underwing Moth! Moth with cryptic forewings and often bright hindwings | two wing layers reveal different patterns.
V ... V ... Vinegar Fly! Small drosophilid fly | one pair of functional wings identifies the fly order.
W? Wasp! Six-legged hymenopteran with two pairs of wings in many adults | many species are parasitoids or predators.
X is Xylocopa Bee. Xylocopa is the carpenter-bee genus | robust bees often nest by tunneling into wood.

[Chorus]
Count, check, classify—A-B-C!
Legs, wings, antennae tell us which group we see.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Butterfly! Butterfly color clue | scaled wings identify the order more reliably than yellow alone.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Longwing! Butterfly with long striped wings | six legs place it firmly inside insects.
