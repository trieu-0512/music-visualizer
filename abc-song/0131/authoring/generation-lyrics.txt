[Intro]
Read the board, pass the gate, queue back from the platform edge, board when the train is ready.
A station journey begins with information, not rushing.

[Verse 1]
A — Automatic Gate! | Powered gate opens or closes access when a valid condition is met; at stations it may control fares or passenger flow, while crossing gates protect road-rail intersections.
B ... B ... Brake! | Train braking system slows wheelsets using friction, electrical/dynamic braking, air pressure, or combinations depending rolling stock.
C? Caboose! | Crew car historically placed near the rear of many North American freight trains; modern freight operations often use other systems instead.
D is Dining Car. Passenger rail car contains tables and food service space; it is part of onboard service, not propulsion. |

[Verse 2]
E — Electric Train! | Train draws electrical energy from overhead wire, third rail, batteries, or other sources depending system; traction motors turn wheels.
F ... F ... Freight Car! | Rail vehicle carries goods rather than passengers; boxcars, hoppers, tank cars, flats, and other types fit different loads.
G? Gate! | Rail/station gate controls access or protects a crossing; the pictured location determines its exact job.
H is High-speed Train. Passenger train designed for sustained high speeds on suitable infrastructure; dedicated track geometry, signaling, and grade separation are common safety requirements. |

[Verse 3]
I — Intercity Train! | Passenger service links cities, usually with fewer stops and longer distances than local transit.
J ... J ... Junction Box! | Protected enclosure houses electrical connections; railway electrical equipment is for trained workers, not passenger handling.
K? Key Switch! | Locked/key-operated control allows authorized operation of equipment; passengers do not operate railway control switches.
L is Locomotive. Rail vehicle supplies tractive power to move itself and often other cars; power may be diesel-electric, electric, or other forms. |

[Verse 4]
M — Monorail! | Railway whose vehicles are guided by or suspended from a single beam/rail structure rather than a conventional pair of running rails.
N ... N ... Number Sign! | Displayed number identifies platform, track, car, route, or other railway information depending context.
O? Overpass! | Bridge carries one route above another, separating traffic levels and reducing conflicts.
P is Platform. Raised or level boarding area beside a track; passengers stay behind edge markings until safe boarding. |

[Chorus]
Read, wait, board—A-B-C!
Platform edge first, train second.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queue Barrier! | Posts, rails, or belts organize waiting lines and keep passenger flow orderly.
R ... R ... Rail! | Steel running surface guides flanged train wheels; tracks are not walking paths.
S? Subway! | Urban rapid-transit railway that may run underground, elevated, or at surface; 'subway' does not mean every section is tunneled.
T is Ticket. Proof of fare/payment or travel authorization; it carries journey information but does not control the train. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Car! | Rail vehicle assigned maintenance, inspection, work, or support functions; exact equipment varies by railway.
V ... V ... Vestibule! | Enclosed end/entry area of a passenger car connects doors and often adjacent cars; passengers follow door and movement rules.
W? Wagon! | Railway term for a freight/passenger vehicle in some regions, while everyday English can also mean a road/hand cart; rail context matters.
X is Xpress Train. Project label for an express-style train service; 'Xpress' is branding/spelling, while express generally means fewer stops or faster service pattern. |

[Chorus]
Read, wait, board—A-B-C!

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Signal! | Railway yellow aspect commonly indicates caution or a restrictive condition ahead, but exact meaning depends on the signaling system; drivers follow local rules.
Z ... Z ... Zone Map! | Map divides a transit network into fare, service, or geographic zones; colors/labels explain boundaries.
