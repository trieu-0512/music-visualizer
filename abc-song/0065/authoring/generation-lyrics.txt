[Intro]
Do not name it from color alone: count legs, check wings, notice antennae.
A field guide works best when several clues agree.

[Verse 1]
A — Ant! Elbowed antennae, narrow waist, six legs | social behavior can add another ID clue.
B ... B ... Beetle! Elytra are the strongest field mark | hard forewings meet in a line down the back.
C? Cricket! Very long antennae and enlarged jumping hind legs | sound can support ID, but body marks come first.
D is Dragonfly. Huge compound eyes and two pairs of long open wings | adult often patrols near water.

[Verse 2]
E — Earwig! Paired forceps at abdomen tip | one unmistakable shape clue.
F ... F ... Firefly! Soft-bodied beetle; many adults have light organs | glow is useful only in species and life stages that actually produce it.
G? Grasshopper! Shorter antennae than katydids, enlarged hind legs | jumping body plan is a strong field mark.
H is Honeybee. Hairy body, pollen-carrying structures, elbowed antennae | flower behavior supports the ID.

[Chorus]
Look, note, check—A-B-C!
One trait starts the guess; several traits build the name.

[Verse 3]
I — Inchworm! Looping geometer-moth caterpillar | fewer middle prolegs create its measuring gait.
J ... J ... Jewel Beetle! Streamlined beetle with often metallic elytra | shape plus wing covers beats color alone.
K? Kissing Bug! Elongate head and piercing mouthparts in triatomines | potentially hazardous; identify from image or expert source, never handling.
L is Leafhopper. Wedge-shaped small insect with jumping hind legs | sap-feeding beak tucked under the head.

[Verse 4]
M — Mantis! Triangular head and raptorial forelegs | predatory posture is a strong clue.
N ... N ... Net-winged Beetle! Raised ridges form a netlike pattern on elytra | texture becomes the field mark.
O? Orb Weaver! Spider with eight legs and orb web | non-insect exception catches careless leg counting.
P is Pill Bug. Many-legged terrestrial isopod with overlapping plates | crustacean, not insect.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Bee! A reproductive colony role, not a species field mark | identifying a queen depends on colony and body context.
R ... R ... Rhinoceros Beetle! Large scarab with horn structures in many males | elytra still identify it as beetle.
S? Stag Beetle! Large mandibles in many males resemble antlers | scarab-like beetle body plus jaw shape narrows ID.
T is Termite. Straight-looking antennae and broad waist contrast with ants | reproductive adults may have equal-length wings.

[Chorus]
Look, note, check—A-B-C!
One trait starts the guess; several traits build the name.

[Verse 6]
U — Ulysses Butterfly! Brilliant blue upper wings with dark margins | range and wing shape must join color for reliable ID.
V ... V ... Vinegar Fly! Tiny drosophilid with one wing pair and attraction to fermenting material | behavior plus fly anatomy.
W? Weevil! Elongated snout and elbowed antennae | specialized beetle profile.
X is Xylophone Beetle, our project X label | keep the field note tied to the provided picture until a more precise taxon is supplied.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Butterfly! Yellow is only a color clue | wing shape, markings, range, and host plants are needed for species ID.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Longwing! Long narrow black wings with yellow stripes | distinctive shape-plus-pattern combination.
