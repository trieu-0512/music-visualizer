[Intro]
A mouse is small; an iguana may grow large; both need more than a box and a bowl.
Body size does not tell us how simple care will be.

[Verse 1]
A — Aquarium! Aquatic habitat is a water-quality system with volume, filtration, heat/light as species require | not decorative container.
B ... B ... Bunny! Rabbit needs large floor space, hay, water, litter options, chew items and social/veterinary care.
C? Collar! Fits some companion animals, not every small animal | correct fit and purpose matter.
D is Dog. Even a small dog needs exercise, training, social contact, veterinary care and species-appropriate diet.

[Verse 2]
E — Ear Cleaner! Veterinary-approved ear product is species/condition specific | adult guidance only.
F ... F ... Feather Toy! Cat enrichment item can be fun under supervision | dangerous if loose parts are swallowed.
G? Grooming Brush! Coat-specific tool | useful to a furry pet, irrelevant to fish and potentially wrong for other species.
H is Hutch. Traditional rabbit housing word | a hutch alone is usually too small to provide the exercise area a rabbit needs.

[Chorus]
Small body, big needs—A-B-C!
Space, diet, light, health: check the species.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguana! Reptile with demanding enclosure size, heat, humidity, diet and UVB needs | 'small animal' label quickly becomes misleading.
J ... J ... Jingle Ball! Noise toy may suit some pets but stress others | enrichment requires choice.
K? Kibble! Formulated dry food applies to some mammals, not a universal diet for rabbits, birds, reptiles or fish.
L is Leash. Dog walking/control tool; some other species may use specialized harnesses, but leash is never universal small-animal gear.

[Verse 4]
M — Mouse! Secure ventilated enclosure needs bedding, hides, nesting material, food and enrichment | tiny body still needs complexity.
N ... N ... Nest! Nesting structure/material suits certain birds and rodents | not every pet needs or should receive the same material.
O? Octopus Toy! Novelty toy must be judged by material and species behavior | shape alone tells us nothing about suitability.
P is Puppy. Young dog needs structured care and rest | small age does not mean small responsibility.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground-dwelling bird needs horizontal floor area, cover, suitable substrate, social setup and species-appropriate nutrition.
R ... R ... Rabbit! Hay-centered diet and large movement space distinguish rabbit care from rodent stereotypes.
S? Seed Mix! Seeds can be part of some bird/rodent diets, but seed-only feeding is often nutritionally incomplete | species formulation matters.
T is Treat. Optional extra, not core nutrition | amount and ingredients depend on species.

[Chorus]
Small body, big needs—A-B-C!
Space, diet, light, health: check the species.

[Verse 6]
U — Undercoat Brush! Designed for certain fur coats | do not apply the 'groom every pet' rule across scales, feathers or skin.
V ... V ... Vest Harness! Fit-sensitive equipment for selected species/individuals | supervision and comfort first.
W? Water Bowl! Common hydration tool for many mammals/birds | aquatic pets and some species require different water systems too.
X is X-ray Vet Image. Veterinary diagnostic evidence applies across many species | interpretation and restraint belong to professionals.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Canary! Small bird still needs flight room, balanced diet, perches, bathing, rest schedule and enrichment.
Z ... Z ... Zip Carrier! Transport container must fit body, ventilation, temperature and stress needs | correct carrier differs by species.
