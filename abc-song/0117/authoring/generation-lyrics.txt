[Intro]
Puzzle at the table, blocks on the floor, kite outside, scooter in a safe riding area.
Finding the toy is only half the hunt; finding its safe space is the other half.

[Verse 1]
A — Action Figure! | Small articulated character figure supports posing and pretend narratives; small accessories can be choking hazards.
B ... B ... Ball! | Round play object rolls or bounces depending material and inflation; size should fit the age and activity.
C? Crayon! | Wax drawing stick adds mark-making to play; it belongs on suitable paper, not toys, walls, or mouths.
D is Dominoes. Flat tiles can be matched by markings or stood in chains; game rules and toppling play are different activities. |

[Verse 2]
E — Excavator Toy! | Toy model represents an excavator with boom, stick, bucket, and tracked/wheeled base depending design.
F ... F ... Finger Puppet! | Small character fits over a finger for storytelling; size and detachable pieces must be age-appropriate.
G? Game Board! | Marked surface organizes spaces, paths, or zones for a game; rules give the marks their meaning.
H is Hoop. Circular ring supports rolling, tossing, stepping, or waist-spinning games depending size and setting. |

[Verse 3]
I — Interlocking Blocks! | Building pieces connect through fitted shapes or studs; connection strength and geometry affect structures.
J ... J ... Jump Rope! | Rope turned for jumping games; clear space and supervised use prevent trips or wrapping around the body.
K? Kite! | Light frame/sail flies in wind on a line; use in open areas far from roads, storms, and power lines.
L is Letter Tiles. Individual tiles display letters for sorting, spelling, or word play; they are movable symbols rather than fixed text. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marble! | Small hard sphere rolls smoothly and can be used in games; its size creates a serious choking hazard for young children.
N ... N ... Number Puzzle! | Puzzle uses numerals or quantities as matching clues, combining shape fitting with number recognition.
O? Octopus Toy! | Octopus-shaped toy is identified by form; material, seams, and detachable parts determine safe use.
P is Plush Bear. Soft stuffed bear toy; secure seams and attached parts matter more than the exact fur texture. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiz Game! | Question-and-answer game tests recall or reasoning; a missed answer is a chance to learn, not a judgment of the player.
R ... R ... Rattle! | Enclosed loose pieces or a moving part create sound when shaken; casing must stay intact so small contents cannot escape.
S? Scooter! | Wheeled ride-on platform steered by handlebars in common designs; helmet, suitable area, and supervision matter.
T is Teddy Bear. Soft stuffed bear-shaped toy supports comfort or pretend play; loose eyes, seams, and stuffing must stay secure. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicycle! | Single-wheel ride-on cycle requires balance skill and protective equipment; it is not a preschool indoor riding toy.
V ... V ... Vehicle Toy! | Broad category for toy representations of transport; wheels, motors, scale, and function vary by model.
W? Wagon! | Low wheeled cart can carry toys or be pulled; riding rules depend on design and adult supervision.
X is X-shaped Puzzle. Puzzle target uses a clear X-shaped piece or outline; the X form is the identification clue. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn Doll! | Figure made by wrapping and tying yarn; loose fibers or long strands require supervised craft/play.
Z ... Z ... Zoom Car! | Project label for a small toy car; do not invent a pull-back, battery, or friction motor unless the pictured model shows one.
