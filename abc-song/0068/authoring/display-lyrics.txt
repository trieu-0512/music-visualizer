[Intro]
Bluebird on the fence—then the next card shows a kiwi from New Zealand.
Our backyard is the starting point, not the habitat for every bird in the alphabet.

[Verse 1]
A — Avocet! Wetland wader with upcurved bill | world-comparison card, not a typical backyard feeder bird.
B ... B ... Bluebird! Cavity-nesting thrush that may use nest boxes and open yards in its range | a genuine yard-edge comparison.
C? Crane! Tall wetland or grassland bird | far larger and more habitat-specific than a backyard songbird.
D is Duck. Webbed feet and broad bill suit water | ponds and wetlands, not seed feeders, define the main comparison.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Large raptor seen overhead in suitable regions | sky visitor at best, never a feeder-table bird.
F ... F ... Finch! Small seed-eating finches can appear in gardens and yards | conical bill fits the home baseline.
G? Goldfinch! Seed specialist often visits gardens or seed heads | one believable backyard-card bird in its native range.
H is Hummingbird. Nectar-feeding birds may visit flower gardens or feeders in the Americas | hovering sets them apart.

[Verse 3]
I — Indian Roller! South Asian bird of open country | world card, not a generic backyard resident.
J ... J ... Jay! Intelligent corvid; some jays commonly visit yards in their ranges | bold behavior fits the home frame.
K? Kiwi! Flightless nocturnal bird endemic to New Zealand | world comparison only, never a backyard claim.
L is Lorikeet. Nectar-feeding parrot of Australasia | may occur in urban areas there, but remains a regional world card.

[Chorus]
Here, there, compare—A-B-C!
One yard opens a window to birds around the world.

[Verse 4]
M — Magpie! Corvid name covers several regional species | some thrive around people, but location matters.
N ... N ... Nuthatch! Small trunk-climbing bird that can visit feeders | head-first descent is a useful backyard field mark.
O? Ostrich! Huge flightless African bird of open habitats | world card deliberately far from the backyard scale.
P is Parrot. Broad group mostly tropical/subtropical | some urban populations occur, but 'backyard' depends entirely on region.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground bird of fields, scrub, or woodland edges | may cross yards locally but does not live on a feeder tray.
R ... R ... Raven! Large corvid with heavy bill | may occur near people but needs a much wider territory than a yard.
S? Sparrow! Many sparrows thrive around human spaces | small ground and shrub foraging fits the home comparison.
T is Tern. Slender coastal or inland-water flyer | world habitat card, not backyard feeder bird.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrellabird! Tropical American forest bird | unmistakable world-comparison species, not a porch visitor.
V ... V ... Vulture! Soaring scavenger may pass high overhead | never attract or approach for a backyard lesson.
W? Wren! Tiny energetic songbird often uses dense yard vegetation in its range | home-scale bird with upright tail in many species.
X is Xantus Hummingbird. Baja California-region hummingbird | geographic range matters before calling it a backyard bird.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellowhammer! Eurasian bunting of farmland and hedges | regional comparison rather than universal yard species.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Finch! Australian grassland finch and common captive bird | world card unless local context truly fits.
