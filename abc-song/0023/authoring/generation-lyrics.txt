[Intro]
One call near. One answer far. Then the trees go still.
We follow the spaces between sounds from A to Z.

[Verse 1]
A — Aspen! Leaves flutter softly between bird calls | a pale trunk marks where one sound came from.
B ... B ... Bear! Heavy paws can press the ground quietly | we give wild animals distance and listen from far away.
C? Chipmunk! Quick rustle under leaves | a small mammal darts before the branch above answers.
D is Deer. Hooves place softly on the trail edge | ears turn toward every nearby sound.

[Verse 2]
E — Evergreen! Needles hold a green pause | a silent shape between one call and the next.
F ... F ... Fox! Light paws, pointed ears | a quiet hunter moving through the gaps in our sound map.
G? Grouse! Ground bird under cover | sudden wings can thunder up from the hush.
H is Hollow Log. Empty space inside fallen wood | a shelter that can deepen a tap or hold complete silence.

[Chorus]
Call, echo, hush—A-B-C!
Branch to branch, the forest answers carefully.

[Verse 3]
I — Insect! Six small legs, tiny body | buzz, click, or wing-whir only when the insect truly makes it.
J ... J ... Jay! A strong bird call jumps from branch to branch | bright sound before the next pause.
K? Knot! A silent twist in wood | one visual marker while our ears wait for another clue.
L is Lynx. Wild cat, tufted ears, quiet paws | almost no sound is still part of the listening map.

[Verse 4]
M — Maple! Leaves rub and rustle when wind arrives | then stop when the air goes still.
N ... N ... Nest! Twigs form a careful cup | we watch from a distance and keep the nest quiet.
O? Oak! Broad branches hold many listening places | leaves answer wind more than they answer us.
P is Pinecone. Woody scales close around seeds | a quiet forest object until it drops or rolls.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Small ground bird in cover | a call may come before the bird itself appears.
R ... R ... River! Steady water makes the longest sound in the song | a flowing line beneath shorter calls.
S? Squirrel! Claws scratch bark, feet land light | quick movement climbs out of our ground-level hearing.
T is Trail. Our own footsteps belong here too | step, stop, then leave room for the forest.

[Verse 6]
U — Underbrush! Leaves and stems make close rustles | low cover can hide the source of a sound.
V ... V ... Violet! A small quiet flower near the trail | color becomes the clue when sound gives us nothing.
W? Wolf! A distant howl can travel across open forest | we listen from far away and never approach wildlife.
X is Xeric Shrub. Dry-site leaves and stems mark a different patch | a visual clue in a song built mostly from sound.

[Chorus]
Call, echo, hush—A-B-C!
Branch to branch, the forest answers carefully.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yew! Evergreen needles stay quiet | we only look and never taste unknown forest plants.
At the trailhead garden, Z is Zinnia—our bright Z helper | layered petals close the listening walk without pretending it grew wild in the forest.

[Interlude]
The trail goes quiet—listen for the woodland words returning.
