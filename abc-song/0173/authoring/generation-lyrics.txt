[Intro]
Heart rate changes, muscles contract, dimples may appear or not, waist and face shapes naturally vary.
Health cannot be read from one look.

[Verse 1]
A — Ankle! | Joint region connects lower leg and foot; several bones and ligaments allow flexing while supporting body weight.
B ... B ... Belly! | Everyday word for abdomen, containing abdominal wall and many digestive/urinary/reproductive organs; outside shape does not reveal health by itself.
C? Chin! | Lower front facial prominence formed mainly by mandible plus soft tissue; chin shape naturally varies.
D is Dimple. Small skin indentation often linked to underlying facial structure/muscle variation; presence or absence is a normal trait. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eye! | Sensory organ focuses light onto the retina; vision also depends on optic nerves and brain processing.
F ... F ... Finger! | Digit contains bones, joints, tendons, nerves, skin, and nails; fingers support touch and precise grip.
G? Grip! | Ability to hold/control objects comes from fingers, thumb, muscles, tendons, joints, sensation, and nervous-system control.
H is Heart. Muscular organ pumps blood through lungs and body; heart rate changes with activity, rest, emotion and health context. |

[Chorus]
Move, rest, vary—A-B-C!
Bodies can differ and still deserve the same respect.

[Verse 3]
I — Incisor! | Front cutting tooth with a relatively sharp edge; humans normally have incisors in upper and lower jaws.
J ... J ... Jaw! | Upper and lower jaw support teeth; the mandible is the movable lower jaw used in chewing and speech.
K? Knuckle! | Prominent joint area where finger bones meet hand or each other; bending comes from the joint, tendons, and muscles.
L is Lips. Soft tissue around mouth opening supports speech, eating, expression and touch. |

[Verse 4]
M — Muscle! | Contractile tissue generates force and movement; muscles also support posture, breathing, circulation, and internal organ functions.
N ... N ... Nose! | Structure supports smell and conditions inhaled air through nasal passages; smell receptors sit high in nasal cavity.
O? Outer Ear! | Visible pinna plus outer ear canal collect/direct sound toward eardrum; hearing continues through middle/inner ear and brain.
P is Palm. Front surface of hand contains skin creases, nerves, muscles/tendons and connective tissue; it supports grip and touch. |

[Chorus]

Bodies can differ and still deserve the same respect.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadriceps! | Group of four muscles at front of thigh primarily extends knee and helps control movement.
R ... R ... Retina! | Light-sensitive neural tissue lines back of eye and converts light into nerve signals sent toward brain.
S? Shoulder! | Region links arm to trunk through several joints, muscles and tendons, giving a wide range of motion.
T is Toe. Foot digit contributes to balance and push-off; big toe is especially important during walking. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uvula! | Small soft-tissue projection hanging from soft palate; it contributes to swallowing/speech functions but is not the sole gag structure.
V ... V ... Voice! | Sound begins when airflow makes vocal folds vibrate, then throat, mouth and nose shape resonance and speech.
W? Waist! | Everyday region between ribs and hips; waist size/shape varies and is not a moral or complete health measure.
X is Xiphoid Bone. Xiphoid process is the small lower part of sternum; it can be cartilaginous when young and ossifies variably with age. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 7]
Y — Yawn! | Involuntary deep inhalation/open-mouth behavior associated with sleepiness, transitions in alertness and social contagion; exact biology is complex.
Z ... Z ... Zygomatic Bone! | Cheekbone forms part of cheek prominence and eye socket; one on each side connects with neighboring skull bones.
