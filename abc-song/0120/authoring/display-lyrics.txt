[Intro]
Frisbee needs space; blocks can use a blanket; marbles are a poor preschool playground choice.
A toy can be good and still belong in a different setting.

[Verse 1]
A — Action Figure! | Small articulated character figure supports posing and pretend narratives; small accessories can be choking hazards.
B ... B ... Blocks! | Separate solid pieces can stack, line up, balance, or build; stability depends on base width and center of mass.
C? Car Toy! | Toy representation of a car; rolling wheels are common, but propulsion method depends on the specific toy.
D is Doll. Human- or character-shaped play figure supports caregiving and storytelling play; parts and accessories must be age-appropriate. |

[Verse 2]
E — Etch-a-Sketch! | Classic mechanical drawing toy uses two knobs to move a stylus horizontally and vertically behind a screen.
F ... F ... Frisbee! | Flying disc glides after a spinning throw; outdoor space and distance from people or roads matter.
G? Game Board! | Marked surface organizes spaces, paths, or zones for a game; rules give the marks their meaning.
H is Hoop. Circular ring supports rolling, tossing, stepping, or waist-spinning games depending size and setting. |

[Chorus]
Pack, space, check—A-B-C!
Choose the toy for the place, not just the letter.

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Ball! | Air-filled flexible ball deforms and rebounds; inflation level and size affect bounce and handling.
J ... J ... Jigsaw! | Puzzle of interlocking pieces reconstructs one image or shape by matching edges and visual clues.
K? Kaleidoscope! | Mirrors inside a tube reflect colored pieces into repeated symmetrical patterns as the view changes.
L is Letter Tiles. Individual tiles display letters for sorting, spelling, or word play; they are movable symbols rather than fixed text. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marble! | Small hard sphere rolls smoothly and can be used in games; its size creates a serious choking hazard for young children.
N ... N ... Nesting Blocks! | Graduated hollow blocks fit inside one another and can also stack, teaching size order and containment.
O? Octopus Toy! | Octopus-shaped toy is identified by form; material, seams, and detachable parts determine safe use.
P is Plush Bear. Soft stuffed bear toy; secure seams and attached parts matter more than the exact fur texture. |

[Chorus]
Pack, space, check—A-B-C!
Choose the toy for the place, not just the letter.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quoits Ring! | Ring used in tossing games toward a peg or target; soft, age-appropriate rings reduce impact risk.
R ... R ... Rattle! | Enclosed loose pieces or a moving part create sound when shaken; casing must stay intact so small contents cannot escape.
S? Stuffed Animal! | Soft fabric animal-shaped toy filled with stuffing; seams and attached features must remain secure.
T is Teddy Bear. Soft stuffed bear-shaped toy supports comfort or pretend play; loose eyes, seams, and stuffing must stay secure. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uno Cards! | Commercial card-game deck uses colors, numbers, and action symbols; play follows its published rules rather than ordinary playing-card rules.
V ... V ... Vehicle Toy! | Broad category for toy representations of transport; wheels, motors, scale, and function vary by model.
W? Wooden Blocks! | Solid wood construction pieces can stack and balance; smooth edges and non-toxic finishes matter.
X is Xylophone Toy. Toy with bar-like keys can introduce pitch patterns; toy tuning may be approximate rather than concert-accurate. |

[Chorus]
Pack, space, check—A-B-C!
Choose the toy for the place, not just the letter.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn Doll! | Figure made by wrapping and tying yarn; loose fibers or long strands require supervised craft/play.
Z ... Z ... Zoom Car! | Project label for a small toy car; do not invent a pull-back, battery, or friction motor unless the pictured model shows one.
