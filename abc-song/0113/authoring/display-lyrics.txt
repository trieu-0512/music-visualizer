[Intro]
Violin can sit in an orchestra; Kazoo usually does not; Yidaki carries a different musical tradition.
An ensemble map gets better when we do not force every instrument into one group.

[Verse 1]
A — Acoustic Guitar! | Hollow-bodied plucked string instrument; vibrating strings drive the soundboard and air in the body.
B ... B ... Bell! | Metal idiophone rings when struck or when an internal clapper hits it; shape and size influence pitch.
C? Clarinet! | Single-reed woodwind with cylindrical bore; covered/open holes and keys change the effective air-column length.
D is Drum. Membranophone whose stretched head vibrates when struck; drum shape and tension affect tone. |

[Verse 2]
E — Euphonium! | Low brass instrument with valves and a conical bore; buzzing lips start the sound.
F ... F ... Flute! | Side-blown woodwind in the concert family; an air jet across the embouchure hole excites the air column.
G? Gong! | Suspended metal percussion disc that vibrates after a mallet strike; size and shape affect pitch and resonance.
H is Harp. Plucked string instrument with strings running between soundboard and neck; each string usually supplies a pitch before pedal/lever changes. |

[Chorus]
String, wind, brass, beat—A-B-C!
Build the ensemble, keep each tradition named.

[Verse 3]
I — Irish Whistle! | Simple fipple flute, often six-holed, used in Irish traditional music; finger holes change the air-column length.
J ... J ... Jingle Bells! | Small bells or clusters that ring when shaken; repeated metal impacts create the jingle.
K? Kazoo! | Membrane instrument that changes a hummed voice through a vibrating membrane; blowing silently through it does not create the usual kazoo sound.
L is Lute. Plucked string instrument family with a neck and resonating body; many historical and regional forms exist. |

[Verse 4]
M — Mandolin! | Small plucked string instrument, commonly with four double courses tuned in fifths.
N ... N ... Note Card! | Learning card that displays a note symbol or musical prompt; it represents music but makes no sound itself.
O? Organ! | Keyboard instrument that sustains tones using pipes or electronic sound generation; keys can control more than one rank or voice.
P is Piano. Keyboard instrument whose keys trigger felt-covered hammers to strike strings in an acoustic piano. |

[Chorus]
String, wind, brass, beat—A-B-C!
Build the ensemble, keep each tradition named.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quena! | Traditional Andean end-blown flute with a notched rim and finger holes.
R ... R ... Rhythm Sticks! | Pair of percussion sticks used to tap rhythmic patterns; safe use means striking sticks or approved surfaces, never people.
S? Saxophone! | Single-reed woodwind, usually metal-bodied; reed vibration starts the sound despite its brass appearance.
T is Trumpet. High brass instrument; buzzing lips feed a cylindrical-to-flared tube while valves redirect air through extra tubing. |

[Verse 6]
U — Ukulele! | Small four-string plucked instrument, commonly tuned with a high G in many soprano/concert setups.
V ... V ... Violin! | Smallest standard orchestral bowed string instrument; bow friction or plucking starts string vibration.
W? Wood Block! | Hollow or resonant wooden percussion block; a beater strike produces a short dry tone.
X is Xaphoon. Compact single-reed wind instrument designed to produce a saxophone-like tone through a simple cylindrical body. |

[Chorus]
String, wind, brass, beat—A-B-C!
Build the ensemble, keep each tradition named.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yidaki! | Yolngu name for a traditional northern Australian didgeridoo-type instrument; lip vibration excites a long hollow tube.
Z ... Z ... Zither! | Family of string instruments whose strings stretch across a body without a projecting neck like a guitar.
