[Intro]
Ripple... ripple... rings on the pond.
Follow the water—our letters flow on.

[Verse 1]
A — Alder. By the flow | roots hold damp banks as branches grow.
B is for Boat. Row, then float | carrying people or things along the river.
Catfish — remember C. Down below | whisker-like barbels wave around its mouth as it goes.
What drifts in with D? Dam! A wall holds water back and changes how the current moves.

[Verse 2]
By the water, E meets Eel. Easy glide | winding body bends from side to side.
F — Frog. Bankside hop | strong back legs kick for swimming, then stop.
G is for Green Algae. Green sheen | soft patches spread in water and over wet surfaces.
Heron — remember H. Long legs pause in shallow water; stillness comes before a step.

[Chorus]
Ripple, ripple—A-B-C!
River words come back to me!

[Verse 3]
What drifts in with I? Iris! Wet-ground bright | flower opens into morning light.
By the water, J meets June Bug. Warm-evening flight | shiny beetle circles into sight.
K — Kingfisher. Flash in sight | dives for a fish, then climbs back to height.
L is for Lily Pad. Flat green leaf floats like a plate on quiet water.

[Verse 4]
Muskrat — remember M. Body low | paddles beside the wet bank where grasses grow.
What drifts in with N? Nest! Twigs held tight | a bird-built place for eggs or chicks inside.
By the water, O meets Otter. Side to side | float, turn, tumble, dive, then glide.
P — Pond. A small body of still water gives plants and animals a place to live.

[Verse 5]
Q is for Quillwort. Quillwort low | spiky-looking leaves in wet places grow.
Reed — remember R. Tall stems show | straight at the water's edge, swaying slow.
What drifts in with S? Salamander! Long and low | four small legs help the moist-skinned body go.
By the water, T meets Trout. Cool-water fish flicks its tail against the current.

[Chorus]
Ripple, ripple—A-B-C!
River words come back to me!

[Verse 6]
U — Underwater Plant. Sway this way | submerged leaves bend with moving water all day.
V is for Vallisneria. Long ribbon-like leaves grow underwater in freshwater.
Waterfall — remember W. Rush and spray | water drops from higher rock and breaks into mist on the way.
What drifts in with X? X-ray Tetra! Small translucent fish with a partly see-through body.

[Verse 7]
By the water, Y meets Yellow Water Lily. Yellow in light | broad leaves float, flower bright.
Z — Zooplankton. Tiny and light | little drifters ride the current day and night.

[Chorus]
Ripple, ripple—A-B-C!
River words come back to me!

[Interlude]
The ripple fades... now listen for what returns.

Remember B ... Boat! Row two slow strokes.
C? ... Catfish! Wiggle fingers like whiskers, then swim.
Find D ... Dam! Hold two palms together like a wall.

[Call and Response 2]
E ... wait ... Eel! Wiggle one hand in a long curve.
Remember F ... Frog! Make one little finger-hop.
G? ... Green Algae! Wave fingers softly like green strands.
Find H ... Heron! Hold two fingers tall, then take one slow step.

[Call and Response 3]
I ... wait ... Iris! Open your hands like a flower.
Remember J ... June Bug! Buzz your fingers in a small circle.
K? ... Kingfisher! Make one quick hand-dive, then hold still.
Find L ... Lily Pad! Hold one flat palm like a floating leaf.

[Call and Response 4]
M ... wait ... Muskrat! Paddle two fingers along an imaginary bank.
Remember N ... Nest! Cup both hands like a nest.
O? ... Otter! Float both palms, then make one little dive.
Find P ... Pond! Make a small calm circle with both hands.

[Call and Response 5]

Remember R ... Reed! Stand one hand tall, then sway.
S? ... Salamander! Walk two fingers, then wiggle a tail.
Find T ... Trout! Flick one hand gently like a tail.

[Call and Response 6]

Remember V ... Vallisneria! Sway one hand like a long ribbon leaf.
W? ... Waterfall! Let both hands tumble gently downward.
Find X ... X-ray Tetra! Make a gentle fish-swim with one hand.

[Call and Response 7]
Y ... wait ... Yellow Water Lily! Hold one flat palm, then open a yellow flower.
Remember Z ... Zooplankton! Let fingertips drift slowly with the current.

River words come back to me!
