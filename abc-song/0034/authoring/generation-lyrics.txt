[Intro]
The first dune hides everything beyond its crest.
Climb the shape, read the horizon, then choose the next gentle route.

[Verse 1]
A — Armadillo! Low armored mammal crosses firm ground | a moving marker before the first sand rise.
B ... B ... Beetle! Tiny legs trace a short path across rippled sand | scale changes when we look down from a dune.
C? Camel! Broad feet travel over sand | tall body becomes a clear shape against the horizon.
D is Dune. Wind-shaped ridge of sand | crest above, slope below, and a sheltered side out of direct wind.

[Verse 2]
E — Euphorbia! Dry-adapted plant shape breaks the open slope | a rooted route marker that does not move with us.
F ... F ... Fennec! Huge ears, small fox body | long shadow stretches when the sun sits low.
G? Gila Monster! Heavy patterned lizard stays close to ground | a low clue we never approach.
H is Horned Lizard. Flat body and pointed scales | shape blends with rough ground below the crest.

[Chorus]
Crest, slope, horizon—A-B-C!
Wind redraws the route, but landmarks help us see.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Sure hooves belong on rocky slopes | a reminder that desert routes are not only loose sand.
J ... J ... Joshua Tree! Branching silhouette stands high enough to guide the horizon | one fixed point beyond shifting ripples.
K? Kestrel! Small falcon holds above the slope | sky position changes while the dune stays beneath.
L is Lizard. Long body, scales, tail | quick low motion crosses from sun into a small shadow.

[Verse 4]
M — Meerkat! Upright lookout shape rises above low scrub | one small vertical marker in wide space.
N ... N ... Nighthawk! Dusk bird enters when the horizon cools | route time changes as well as route shape.
O? Oryx! Long horns and strong hoofed body | a large distant silhouette beyond the next rise.
P is Prickly Pear. Flat cactus pads make a rooted landmark | easy to see, never a reason to leave the safe route.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Small ground bird moves along low cover | a quick crossing on the lee side.
R ... R ... Roadrunner! Long legs race across flatter ground | fast motion between two slow dune shapes.
S? Sand! Millions of tiny grains build the slope | wind rearranges the route one ripple at a time.
T is Tumbleweed. Dried plant can roll with wind after breaking free | a moving landmark that refuses to stay a landmark.

[Chorus]
Crest, slope, horizon—A-B-C!
Wind redraws the route, but landmarks help us see.

[Verse 6]
U — Urial! Wild sheep uses firm dry slopes and rocky country | sure hooves on terrain beyond loose sand.
V ... V ... Viper! Venomous snake may use warm ground and cover | any sighting means more distance, not closer exploration.
W? Wren! Small bird uses scrub and low shelter | a tiny route clue where vegetation gathers.
X is Xerophyte. Dry-adapted plant holds its place through heat | one botanical marker on the open route.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Scorpion! Pale scorpion on desert ground | safety turns this clue into a stop-and-back-away marker.
Z ... Z ... Ziziphus Shrub! Thorny hardy shrub with small fruits | last fixed shape before the final horizon.

[Chorus]
Crest, slope, horizon—A-B-C!
Wind redraws the route, but landmarks help us see.
