[Intro]
Marshaller guides position, luggage cart moves bags, fuel stays controlled, crew checks systems before departure.
A ground turnaround is teamwork inside a restricted ramp.

[Verse 1]
A — Airport! | Facility coordinates aircraft movement, passengers, cargo, security, runways, taxiways, gates, and ground services.
B ... B ... Boarding Pass! | Travel document/token links a passenger to a flight and often seat/gate data; gate/time still need current checks.
C? Cargo Plane! | Aircraft configured mainly to carry freight; loading balance and restraint are critical to safe flight.
D is Drone. Uncrewed aircraft can be remotely piloted or automated; operation must follow local airspace, privacy, and safety rules. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Aircraft engine provides thrust or shaft power; turbine, piston, electric, and other designs work differently.
F ... F ... Flight Deck! | Another term for the aircraft cockpit/crew control area; it is a restricted operational workspace.
G? Gate! | Airport gate is a passenger boarding/arrival area assigned to a flight; it is not the runway or aircraft parking stand itself.
H is Hangar. Large building shelters aircraft for storage or maintenance; active maintenance areas are restricted workspaces. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Slide! | Emergency evacuation slide deploys from some aircraft doors; crew procedures govern use and accidental deployment is hazardous.
J ... J ... Jet! | Aircraft or engine using jet propulsion accelerates a high-speed exhaust stream; jet describes propulsion more than body shape.
K? Kerosene Tank! | Fuel tank stores aviation turbine fuel such as kerosene-based jet fuel; fueling areas are restricted fire-safety zones.
L is Luggage Cart. Ground vehicle/cart moves baggage around airport service areas; ramp traffic is controlled and not passenger play space. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marshaller! | Trained ground worker uses standardized visual signals to guide aircraft positioning; passengers should not copy signals near operating aircraft.
N ... N ... Nose Wheel! | Forward landing-gear wheel(s) support and often steer a tricycle-gear aircraft on the ground.
O? Overhead Bin! | Cabin storage compartment holds approved carry-on items; doors must latch and weight/size limits prevent falling baggage.
P is Propeller. Rotating blades accelerate air to create thrust; propellers are severe hazards and require complete clearance when operating. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadcopter! | Uncrewed rotorcraft with four rotors; changing relative rotor speeds controls lift and attitude.
R ... R ... Radar! | System transmits radio waves and analyzes returns to detect range, direction, weather, or traffic information depending type.
S? Seatbelt! | Aircraft restraint holds a passenger to the seat during turbulence, takeoff, landing, or whenever instructed; buckle stays low and snug.
T is Turboprop. Gas-turbine engine drives a propeller through a gearbox; much of the thrust comes from the propeller. |

[Verse 6]
U — Ultralight! | Very light aircraft category whose legal definition, training, licensing, and operating limits vary by country.
V ... V ... Vertical Stabilizer! | Vertical tail surface provides directional stability; a rudder is commonly hinged to it for yaw control.
W? Windsock! | Fabric cone shows approximate wind direction and gives a rough visual indication of wind strength; it is not a precision anemometer.
X is X-ray Scanner. Airport security imaging system uses controlled radiation to inspect baggage/items; trained staff operate it under safety procedures. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yoke! | Wheel/column-style flight control used in some airplanes to command pitch/roll; many aircraft use sticks or sidesticks instead.
Z ... Z ... Zeppelin! | Rigid airship uses a structural framework and lifting gas for buoyancy; it is lighter-than-air aviation.
