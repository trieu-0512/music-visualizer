[Intro]
A body book can name a joint, show a heart, compare skin, and explain why one clue is not a diagnosis.
Doctors combine history, examination, tests, and context.

[Verse 1]
A — Arm! | Upper limb from shoulder through hand in everyday use; bones, joints, muscles, nerves and vessels coordinate movement.
B ... B ... Belly! | Everyday word for abdomen, containing abdominal wall and many digestive/urinary/reproductive organs; outside shape does not reveal health by itself.
C? Chin! | Lower front facial prominence formed mainly by mandible plus soft tissue; chin shape naturally varies.
D is Dimple. Small skin indentation often linked to underlying facial structure/muscle variation; presence or absence is a normal trait. |

[Verse 2]
E — Elbow! | Joint between upper arm and forearm mainly bends/straightens and helps forearm rotation through nearby joints.
F ... F ... Foot! | Body structure with many bones, joints, muscles, tendons and sensory receptors; it supports weight, balance and walking.
G? Grip! | Ability to hold/control objects comes from fingers, thumb, muscles, tendons, joints, sensation, and nervous-system control.
H is Heart. Muscular organ pumps blood through lungs and body; heart rate changes with activity, rest, emotion and health context. |

[Chorus]

One picture can teach anatomy without diagnosing anyone.

[Verse 3]
I — Iris! | Colored muscular ring of the eye controls pupil size and therefore how much light enters.
J ... J ... Joint! | Connection between bones; different joint types allow movement, stability, or both.
K? Knuckle! | Prominent joint area where finger bones meet hand or each other; bending comes from the joint, tendons, and muscles.
L is Leg. Lower limb region supports standing and movement; hip, thigh, knee, lower leg, ankle, and foot work as a chain. |

[Verse 4]
M — Mouth! | Opening and oral cavity begin digestion and support taste, chewing, swallowing, speech and breathing pathways.
N ... N ... Nose! | Structure supports smell and conditions inhaled air through nasal passages; smell receptors sit high in nasal cavity.
O? Organ! | Body structure made of tissues performing specialized functions; heart, lungs, liver, skin and others are examples.
P is Palm. Front surface of hand contains skin creases, nerves, muscles/tendons and connective tissue; it supports grip and touch. |

[Chorus]
Name, observe, check—A-B-C!

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadriceps! | Group of four muscles at front of thigh primarily extends knee and helps control movement.
R ... R ... Rib! | Curved bone helps form the rib cage, protecting chest organs and moving with breathing.
S? Skin! | Largest organ by surface area, providing barrier, sensation, temperature regulation and immune functions; appearance varies normally across people.
T is Toe. Foot digit contributes to balance and push-off; big toe is especially important during walking. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uvula! | Small soft-tissue projection hanging from soft palate; it contributes to swallowing/speech functions but is not the sole gag structure.
V ... V ... Vision! | Ability to perceive visual information depends on eyes, optic pathways and brain processing; it is more than eye sharpness alone.
W? Wrist! | Joint region links forearm to hand through multiple small carpal bones, ligaments and tendons.
X is Xiphoid Bone. Xiphoid process is the small lower part of sternum; it can be cartilaginous when young and ossifies variably with age. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 7]
Y — Y-shaped Bone Model! | Project teaching model shaped like Y; it demonstrates a bone/model concept but is not proof that a normal human bone universally has this shape.
Z ... Z ... Zinc Bandage! | Project label for a zinc-containing dressing, such as zinc-oxide bandage used in some medical care; selection/application belongs to trained healthcare guidance.
