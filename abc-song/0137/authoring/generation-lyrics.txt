[Intro]
Wing makes lift, fuselage carries, tail stabilizes; a glider can fly without engine thrust after launch.
Flying machines share air, not one identical mechanism.

[Verse 1]
A — Airplane! | Powered fixed-wing aircraft uses forward motion through air to generate wing lift; propulsion and configuration vary.
B ... B ... Boarding Pass! | Travel document/token links a passenger to a flight and often seat/gate data; gate/time still need current checks.
C? Cockpit! | Crew control area contains flight controls, instruments, displays, and communications; passengers do not enter during normal operations.
D is Departure Board. Display lists flights, times, gates, and status; operational changes can update after a passenger first checks it. |

[Verse 2]
E — Elevator Trim! | Trim system reduces sustained pitch-control force by adjusting a trim tab, stabilizer, or control command depending aircraft.
F ... F ... Fuselage! | Main aircraft body houses crew, passengers, cargo, or systems and connects wings/tail in conventional layouts.
G? Glider! | Aircraft designed for unpowered soaring after launch or tow; it trades altitude and rising air for continued flight.
H is Helicopter. Rotorcraft uses rotating wings for lift and can hover; rotor areas are dangerous and approached only under trained procedures. |

[Chorus]
Lift, thrust, stable—A-B-C!
Build the flight idea one force and surface at a time.

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Slide! | Emergency evacuation slide deploys from some aircraft doors; crew procedures govern use and accidental deployment is hazardous.
J ... J ... Jet! | Aircraft or engine using jet propulsion accelerates a high-speed exhaust stream; jet describes propulsion more than body shape.
K? Kite! | Tethered flying object held by aerodynamic forces and a line; fly far from airports, storms, roads, and power lines.
L is Luggage Cart. Ground vehicle/cart moves baggage around airport service areas; ramp traffic is controlled and not passenger play space. |

[Verse 4]
M — Monoplane! | Fixed-wing aircraft with one main wing plane, unlike a biplane's two stacked wing planes.
N ... N ... Navigation Light! | Aircraft position lights help others determine orientation, commonly red left, green right, white aft under standard conventions.
O? Overhead Bin! | Cabin storage compartment holds approved carry-on items; doors must latch and weight/size limits prevent falling baggage.
P is Passenger Plane. Aircraft configured primarily to carry passengers; seating, range, propulsion, and size vary widely. |

[Chorus]
Lift, thrust, stable—A-B-C!
Build the flight idea one force and surface at a time.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queue Lane! | Marked airport line organizes passengers for check-in, security, or boarding; it is a flow-control feature.
R ... R ... Radar! | System transmits radio waves and analyzes returns to detect range, direction, weather, or traffic information depending type.
S? Seatbelt! | Aircraft restraint holds a passenger to the seat during turbulence, takeoff, landing, or whenever instructed; buckle stays low and snug.
T is Tail. Rear aircraft structure includes stabilizing surfaces such as vertical and horizontal stabilizers in many designs. |

[Verse 6]
U — Ultralight! | Very light aircraft category whose legal definition, training, licensing, and operating limits vary by country.
V ... V ... Vertical Stabilizer! | Vertical tail surface provides directional stability; a rudder is commonly hinged to it for yaw control.
W? Wing! | Airfoil surface produces lift through airflow and pressure effects; wings also carry structure, fuel, or controls depending design.
X is X-ray Scanner. Airport security imaging system uses controlled radiation to inspect baggage/items; trained staff operate it under safety procedures. |

[Chorus]
Lift, thrust, stable—A-B-C!
Build the flight idea one force and surface at a time.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Taxiway Sign! | Yellow-background airport signs commonly give taxiway direction/destination information; exact meaning comes from text, arrows, and local standards.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Plane! | Aircraft flying parabolic arcs creates brief microgravity as occupants and cabin follow free-fall together; gravity is not switched off.
