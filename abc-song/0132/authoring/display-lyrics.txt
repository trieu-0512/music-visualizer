[Intro]
Board tells passengers, signal tells crews, junction routes trains, footbridge keeps feet off rails.
Railway rhythm is coordination.

[Verse 1]
A — Arrival Board! | Electronic or printed display lists expected arrivals and platform/service information; times can change with operations.
B ... B ... Boxcar! | Enclosed freight car with sliding/plug doors protects general cargo from weather.
C? Caboose! | Crew car historically placed near the rear of many North American freight trains; modern freight operations often use other systems instead.
D is Dining Car. Passenger rail car contains tables and food service space; it is part of onboard service, not propulsion. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Railway 'engine' can mean locomotive or its power unit; exact meaning depends on context.
F ... F ... Footbridge! | Pedestrian bridge crosses tracks above grade, keeping people away from the running rails.
G? Goods Wagon! | Freight rail vehicle used to carry goods; British/Commonwealth term overlaps freight car.
H is High-speed Train. Passenger train designed for sustained high speeds on suitable infrastructure; dedicated track geometry, signaling, and grade separation are common safety requirements. |

[Chorus]
Signal, route, separate—A-B-C!
The safe path appears when the system agrees.

[Verse 3]
I — Information Board! | Station display gives routes, times, platforms, rules, or notices; passengers use it to plan rather than guess track access.
J ... J ... Junction! | Location where rail routes meet, split, or cross; switches and signals route trains safely.
K? Kiosk! | Small station booth or machine may sell tickets, information, or goods; function depends on station design.
L is Level Crossing. Place where road/path crosses railway at the same level; users obey lights, bells, barriers, and never go around a closed gate. |

[Verse 4]
M — Metro! | Urban rapid-transit rail system; may use tunnels, elevated structures, or surface sections.
N ... N ... Number Sign! | Displayed number identifies platform, track, car, route, or other railway information depending context.
O? Observation Car! | Passenger car designed with large windows or viewing areas for scenery; it is a service car, not a driving cab.
P is Passenger Car. Rail car carries seated or standing passengers; doors, seating, and amenities vary by service. |

[Chorus]
Signal, route, separate—A-B-C!
The safe path appears when the system agrees.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queue Barrier! | Posts, rails, or belts organize waiting lines and keep passenger flow orderly.
R ... R ... Railway Signal! | Visual/electronic signal communicates movement authority or restrictions to train crews; meanings depend on the railway rulebook.
S? Subway! | Urban rapid-transit railway that may run underground, elevated, or at surface; 'subway' does not mean every section is tunneled.
T is Ticket. Proof of fare/payment or travel authorization; it carries journey information but does not control the train. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underground Train! | Train operating on an underground/subway system or tunneled section; stations and signals govern movement.
V ... V ... Viaduct! | Long bridge-like structure carries rail or road across a valley, streets, or low ground using repeated spans/supports.
W? Wagon! | Railway term for a freight/passenger vehicle in some regions, while everyday English can also mean a road/hand cart; rail context matters.
X is X-crossing Sign. X-shaped railway-crossing warning sign marks a road-rail crossing; it warns road users, not trains to cross freely. |

[Chorus]
Signal, route, separate—A-B-C!
The safe path appears when the system agrees.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Signal! | Railway yellow aspect commonly indicates caution or a restrictive condition ahead, but exact meaning depends on the signaling system; drivers follow local rules.
Z ... Z ... Zone Map! | Map divides a transit network into fare, service, or geographic zones; colors/labels explain boundaries.
