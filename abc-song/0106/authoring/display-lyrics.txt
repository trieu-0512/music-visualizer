[Intro]
Felt stays soft, watercolor spreads with water, clay changes under pressure.
Art materials answer differently when we touch, fold, roll, or paint them.

[Verse 1]
A — Art Board! | Rigid backing supports paper or artwork and keeps the working surface flat.
B ... B ... Bead! | Small pierced piece can be threaded for decoration; tiny beads are choking hazards and need close supervision.
C? Crayon! | Wax-based color stick leaves pigment by friction and pressure; classroom crayons should be non-toxic.
D is Dot Marker. Broad round applicator makes repeated circular marks; it is a mark-making tool, not a paint stamp for skin. |

[Verse 2]
E — Easel! | Upright stand holds paper, board, or canvas at a working angle.
F ... F ... Felt! | Nonwoven textile made by matting fibers; edges do not fray like many woven fabrics.
G? Glitter! | Tiny reflective particles add sparkle but can irritate eyes or spread easily; use supervised craft material and avoid inhalation.
H is Hole Punch. Lever tool cuts small paper holes; moving metal parts can pinch, so adults manage preschool use. |

[Chorus]

Material changes the mark we see.

[Verse 3]
I — Ink! | Pigment- or dye-carrying liquid makes marks and can stain; containers stay closed when not in use.
J ... J ... Jewel Sticker! | Adhesive decorative gem-like piece adds texture and shine; small pieces require supervision.
K? Knitting Needle! | Long pointed tool forms knitted loops with yarn; preschoolers observe or use only with direct adult guidance.
L is Loom. Frame holds lengthwise warp threads while other material passes across as weft. |

[Verse 4]
M — Modeling Clay! | Malleable material changes form under pressing, rolling, pinching, and joining; it hardens only if its formulation is designed to.
N ... N ... Notebook! | Bound pages collect sketches, plans, notes, or color tests in sequence.
O? Origami! | Paper-folding practice builds forms through planned creases; traditional designs can avoid cutting or glue.
P is Paint. Pigment in a binder forms a colored layer on a surface; opacity, drying, and cleanup depend on paint type. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilt Square! | Fabric patch is one unit in a larger quilt design; seams join many squares into a surface.
R ... R ... Ribbon! | Narrow woven strip bends and ties easily; long loose lengths can tangle and need supervision.
S? Scissors! | Pivoting blades shear material; child-safe scissors still require correct grip, seated use, and adult supervision.
T is Tape. Adhesive strip joins, masks, or temporarily holds materials; different tapes have different tack and surfaces. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unfinished Wood! | Bare wood has no final protective coating and may have rough fibers or splinters; adult checks it before craft use.
V ... V ... Velvet Paper! | Flocked or velvet-like paper has a soft raised surface that changes how light and touch reveal color.
W? Watercolor! | Water-soluble paint can make transparent washes; more water usually lightens and spreads the pigment.
X is Xylophone Stamp. Project stamp makes a xylophone-shaped printed image; the stamp is an art tool, not the musical instrument itself. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Paint! | Yellow-colored paint follows the same binder-and-pigment behavior as its paint type; color alone does not define opacity.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Craft! | Project craft uses a zipper as a functional or decorative element; exact construction depends on the designed activity.
