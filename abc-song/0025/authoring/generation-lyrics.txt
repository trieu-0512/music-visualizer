[Intro]
One leaf lets go, turns once, and lands without hurrying.
Autumn changes the forest unevenly—that is what makes it worth watching.

[Verse 1]
A — Acorn! Hard oak seed drops and rolls | a small autumn weight beneath loosening leaves.
B ... B ... Birch! Pale bark stays bright | many birch leaves turn yellow before they fall.
C? Chipmunk! Quick paws move among seeds and leaves | autumn ground cover rustles around the small mammal.
D is Deer. Hooves cross cooler soil | ears rise above grass losing some summer height.

[Verse 2]
E — Evergreen! While other leaves loosen, evergreen foliage stays | one deep green note inside changing color.
F ... F ... Fox! Bushy tail passes through copper leaves | soft paws barely move the cold ground.
G? Grass! Green fades toward straw in some patches | thin blades bend under fallen leaves.
H is Hedgehog. Spines slip through leaf cover | cool days make sheltered ground feel deeper.

[Chorus]
Gold turns, red falls—A-B-C!
Some things change; some green stays with the trees.

[Verse 3]
I — Ivy! Climbing leaves keep tracing bark | green against trunks where other foliage thins.
J ... J ... Juniper! Evergreen leaves and berry-like seed cones | a color that does not follow every falling leaf.
K? Knot! As branches show through thinner foliage | the old twist in wood becomes easier to notice.
L is Lynx. Quiet paws cross a colder floor | wild fur moves through longer evening shadow.

[Verse 4]
M — Mushroom! Damp autumn ground can lift caps from leaf litter | soft shapes after rain.
N ... N ... Nest! When leaves thin, old branch-cups can be easier to spot | we still watch from a respectful distance.
O? Oak! Leaves loosen one by one | acorns below carry the tree's next beginning.
P is Pine. Needles stay green while broad leaves fall nearby | another way a forest can hold autumn.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Small ground bird moves through dry cover | quick feet disappear under leaf-color.
R ... R ... River! Cold water keeps moving | fallen leaves turn, spin, and travel along the bank.
S? Spruce! Needle-covered branches remain green | dark shape against gold and bare twigs.
T is Tree. Canopy thins, branches show | the same trunk wears a different season overhead.

[Chorus]
Gold turns, red falls—A-B-C!
Some things change; some green stays with the trees.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Fern! Fronds can fade and fold as weather cools | feathered shapes settling toward the ground.
V ... V ... Violet! Low leaves stay close to the soil | small green shapes beneath the fallen color.
W? Wolf! A distant wild shape crosses cooler country | we observe only from far away.
At the cabin porch, X is Xylem—tiny tubes carry water upward through a tree or plant stem.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! A brief yellow flash among autumn leaves | one small bird-color before the branches open wider.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Swallowtail! On a last warm patch, striped wings pass low | summer-like motion at the edge of the cooling season.

[Interlude]
The trail goes quiet—listen for the woodland words returning.
