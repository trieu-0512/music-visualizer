[Intro]
Finger and thumb help grip, shoulder links arm to trunk, ribs shape a cage, eye sends signals onward.
Body functions come from many parts working together.

[Verse 1]
A — Ankle! | Joint region connects lower leg and foot; several bones and ligaments allow flexing while supporting body weight.
B ... B ... Back! | Posterior trunk includes spine, muscles, ribs, skin, nerves, and connective tissues; posture and movement come from many structures working together.
C? Cheek! | Side of face contains skin, fat, muscles, and part of the cheekbone; cheek movement contributes to expression and chewing.
D is Digestive System. Organ system breaks food into absorbable nutrients and moves waste onward; mouth, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas and other organs contribute different steps. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eye! | Sensory organ focuses light onto the retina; vision also depends on optic nerves and brain processing.
F ... F ... Finger! | Digit contains bones, joints, tendons, nerves, skin, and nails; fingers support touch and precise grip.
G? Grip! | Ability to hold/control objects comes from fingers, thumb, muscles, tendons, joints, sensation, and nervous-system control.
H is Hand. Body part with palm, fingers and thumb supports touch, grasping, communication, and many fine movements. |

[Chorus]
Part, function, together—A-B-C!
One body clue is never the whole body.

[Verse 3]
I — Iris! | Colored muscular ring of the eye controls pupil size and therefore how much light enters.
J ... J ... Joint! | Connection between bones; different joint types allow movement, stability, or both.
K? Knuckle! | Prominent joint area where finger bones meet hand or each other; bending comes from the joint, tendons, and muscles.
L is Leg. Lower limb region supports standing and movement; hip, thigh, knee, lower leg, ankle, and foot work as a chain. |

[Verse 4]
M — Muscle! | Contractile tissue generates force and movement; muscles also support posture, breathing, circulation, and internal organ functions.
N ... N ... Nail! | Hard keratin plate protects the tips of fingers/toes and supports fine touch; nail health cannot diagnose the whole body by itself.
O? Organ! | Body structure made of tissues performing specialized functions; heart, lungs, liver, skin and others are examples.
P is Palm. Front surface of hand contains skin creases, nerves, muscles/tendons and connective tissue; it supports grip and touch. |

[Chorus]
Part, function, together—A-B-C!
One body clue is never the whole body.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quick Pulse! | Pulse is a rhythmic pressure wave caused by each heartbeat; a quicker rate can follow activity, emotion, fever, medicines or illness, so context matters.
R ... R ... Rib! | Curved bone helps form the rib cage, protecting chest organs and moving with breathing.
S? Shoulder! | Region links arm to trunk through several joints, muscles and tendons, giving a wide range of motion.
T is Tongue. Muscular organ helps move food, taste, speak and swallow; taste receptors are distributed across the tongue rather than strict taste zones. |

[Verse 6]
U — Upper Arm! | Region between shoulder and elbow contains humerus plus muscles, nerves and blood vessels.
V ... V ... Vision! | Ability to perceive visual information depends on eyes, optic pathways and brain processing; it is more than eye sharpness alone.
W? Wrist! | Joint region links forearm to hand through multiple small carpal bones, ligaments and tendons.
X is X-ray. Medical imaging uses ionizing radiation to create internal images, especially useful for bones; trained professionals control dose and positioning. |

[Chorus]
Part, function, together—A-B-C!
One body clue is never the whole body.

[Verse 7]
Y — Y-shaped Bone Model! | Project teaching model shaped like Y; it demonstrates a bone/model concept but is not proof that a normal human bone universally has this shape.
Z ... Z ... Zinc Bandage! | Project label for a zinc-containing dressing, such as zinc-oxide bandage used in some medical care; selection/application belongs to trained healthcare guidance.
