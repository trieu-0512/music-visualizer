[Intro]
Read the departure board, join the queue, walk the jetway, buckle the seatbelt; runway and cockpit stay staff-only.
Airport travel has a passenger path and an operational path.

[Verse 1]
A — Airport! | Facility coordinates aircraft movement, passengers, cargo, security, runways, taxiways, gates, and ground services.
B ... B ... Biplane! | Fixed-wing airplane with two main wings stacked vertically; struts/bracing are common in classic designs.
C? Cockpit! | Crew control area contains flight controls, instruments, displays, and communications; passengers do not enter during normal operations.
D is Departure Board. Display lists flights, times, gates, and status; operational changes can update after a passenger first checks it. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Aircraft engine provides thrust or shaft power; turbine, piston, electric, and other designs work differently.
F ... F ... Flight Deck! | Another term for the aircraft cockpit/crew control area; it is a restricted operational workspace.
G? Gate! | Airport gate is a passenger boarding/arrival area assigned to a flight; it is not the runway or aircraft parking stand itself.
H is Helicopter. Rotorcraft uses rotating wings for lift and can hover; rotor areas are dangerous and approached only under trained procedures. |

[Chorus]
Read, queue, board—A-B-C!
Know where the passenger path ends.

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument Panel! | Panel/display area presents flight, engine, navigation, and system information to pilots; exact instruments vary by aircraft.
J ... J ... Jetway! | Enclosed passenger boarding bridge connects terminal gate to an aircraft door when positioned and secured by trained staff.
K? Kerosene Tank! | Fuel tank stores aviation turbine fuel such as kerosene-based jet fuel; fueling areas are restricted fire-safety zones.
L is Landing Gear. Wheels, struts, or other gear support an aircraft on ground and absorb landing loads; many aircraft retract gear in flight. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marshaller! | Trained ground worker uses standardized visual signals to guide aircraft positioning; passengers should not copy signals near operating aircraft.
N ... N ... Navigation Light! | Aircraft position lights help others determine orientation, commonly red left, green right, white aft under standard conventions.
O? Oxygen Mask! | Emergency aviation mask supplies supplemental oxygen during cabin-pressure problems; passengers follow crew instructions when directed.
P is Propeller. Rotating blades accelerate air to create thrust; propellers are severe hazards and require complete clearance when operating. |

[Chorus]
Read, queue, board—A-B-C!
Know where the passenger path ends.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queue Lane! | Marked airport line organizes passengers for check-in, security, or boarding; it is a flow-control feature.
R ... R ... Runway! | Prepared surface used for aircraft takeoff and landing; it is a tightly controlled area, never a pedestrian viewing path.
S? Seatbelt! | Aircraft restraint holds a passenger to the seat during turbulence, takeoff, landing, or whenever instructed; buckle stays low and snug.
T is Tail. Rear aircraft structure includes stabilizing surfaces such as vertical and horizontal stabilizers in many designs. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uniform! | Crew or ground-service clothing identifies role and organization; uniform itself does not grant passengers operational authority.
V ... V ... Visor! | Cockpit/passenger sun visor reduces glare; it is not the same as a head-up display or flight instrument.
W? Wing! | Airfoil surface produces lift through airflow and pressure effects; wings also carry structure, fuel, or controls depending design.
X is X-wing Model. Model of the fictional X-wing starfighter; it is a science-fiction comparison object, not a real certified aircraft type. |

[Chorus]
Read, queue, board—A-B-C!
Know where the passenger path ends.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Taxiway Sign! | Yellow-background airport signs commonly give taxiway direction/destination information; exact meaning comes from text, arrows, and local standards.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Plane! | Aircraft flying parabolic arcs creates brief microgravity as occupants and cabin follow free-fall together; gravity is not switched off.
