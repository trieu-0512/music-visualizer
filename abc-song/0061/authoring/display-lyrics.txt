[Intro]
One insect pierces a leaf; one rolls dung; one flashes light after dark.
Tiny bodies solve different jobs in different ways.

[Verse 1]
A — Aphid! Slender mouthparts tap plant sap | tiny feeder anchored to a leaf.
B ... B ... Beetle! Hardened forewings called elytra protect the flight wings beneath | armor opens before many beetles fly.
C? Caterpillar! Butterfly or moth larva chews and grows | later metamorphosis rebuilds the body for adulthood.
D is Dung Beetle. Many species feed on dung and some roll or bury it | legs turn waste into food and nursery resources.

[Verse 2]
E — Earwig! Forceps-like cerci curve at the abdomen tip | useful for defense, handling, and species-specific behavior.
F ... F ... Firefly! Light organs produce bioluminescence in many species | flashes can carry courtship signals.
G? Gnat! Tiny fly with one pair of true wings | small flight does not mean every gnat bites.
H is Honeybee. Hairy body carries pollen; a proboscis reaches nectar | worker bees also use a stinger defensively.

[Verse 3]
I — Inchworm! Caterpillar of a geometer moth loops because it has fewer middle prolegs than many caterpillars.
J ... J ... Jewel Beetle! Metallic-looking elytra can reflect brilliant color | hard wing covers hide softer flight wings.
K? Kissing Bug! Blood-feeding triatomine insect | observe only; some species can transmit Trypanosoma cruzi.
L is Ladybug. Lady beetle with hardened elytra | many species eat aphids and other small prey.

[Chorus]
Cling, crawl, fly—A-B-C!
Small does not mean simple when the body parts change.

[Verse 4]
M — Mosquito! Female mosquitoes of many species take blood meals; both sexes use plant sugars | some species transmit disease.
N ... N ... Nymph! Immature stage of insects with incomplete metamorphosis | resembles the adult more than a caterpillar does.
O? Orange Butterfly! Scaled wings carry orange color patterns | butterfly flight comes after a larval caterpillar stage.
P is Praying Mantis. Grasping forelegs seize prey | predator observed with hands away.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Butterfly! Orange-brown milkweed butterfly | scaled wings and gradual wingbeats, unrelated to a queen bee caste.
R ... R ... Roach! Cockroach has long antennae and flattened body | fast legs fit narrow shelters.
S? Stick Insect! Long body and legs can resemble twigs | camouflage works best when the insect barely moves.
T is Termite. Social insect colony includes specialized castes | termites are closer relatives of cockroaches than of ants.

[Verse 6]
U — Ulysses Butterfly! Large blue swallowtail from Australasia | dark wings flash vivid blue in motion.
V ... V ... Viceroy Butterfly! Orange-and-black North American butterfly | wing pattern resembles monarch-type warning coloration.
W? Wasp! Narrow-waisted hymenopteran group with many lifestyles | some sting defensively, so observe without handling.
X is Xylophone Beetle, our project X mapping | treat it as a named picture clue rather than inventing anatomy the label does not guarantee.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Butterfly! Yellow wing color can occur in many butterfly groups | color alone is not a species identification.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Longwing! Long narrow black wings striped yellow | tropical butterfly with unusually long adult lifespan for a butterfly.

[Chorus]
Cling, crawl, fly—A-B-C!
