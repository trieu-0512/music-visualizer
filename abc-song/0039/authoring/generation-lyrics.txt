[Intro]
Map open: tundra first, snow route second, open water only where a safe crossing begins.
A good trek changes plan when the ground changes.

[Verse 1]
Start A — Arctic Fox. Thick fur and small body move across tundra | wildlife clue beside the route, never something to follow closely.
B ... B ... Beluga! Pale whale belongs to northern water | a sign that the route has reached sea, not another snow walker.
C? Cod! Fish below cold water | a submerged checkpoint our boots can never reach.
D is Dog Sled. Long runners glide over snow behind a trained dog team | a travel tool used with skilled adult handling.

[Verse 2]
E — Ermine! Small weasel may turn white in winter | quick wildlife crossing near the land leg.
F ... F ... Floe! Flat piece of floating sea ice | route boundary, not ordinary ground to wander onto.
G? Glacier! Huge land-ice mass moves slowly | a landmark that changes the map on a much larger scale.
H is Husky. Thick-coated working dog can be part of trained sled travel | animal partner, not a machine.

[Chorus]
Map, route, change—A-B-C!
Land, ice, water tell us how to travel next.

[Verse 3]
I — Iceberg! Floating ice rises above water while much remains below | navigation means keeping safe distance.
J ... J ... Jellyfish! Soft drifting body in cold water | one water clue after our route leaves the snow.
K? Kittiwake! Seabird crosses above the route | wings ignore the boundaries that matter to people on the ground.
L is Lemming. Small tundra rodent moves low among plants and snow | a land clue near our boot-level world.

[Verse 4]
M — Musk Ox! Shaggy herd animal of Arctic tundra | broad shape watched from respectful distance.
N ... N ... Narwhal! Arctic whale with a possible long spiral tusk | water-route wildlife beyond the ice edge.
O? Orca! Powerful marine mammal surfaces to breathe | another reason water is its own route, not frozen land.
P is Polar Bear. Large Arctic predator crosses ice and snow and can swim | any real encounter means expert safety and distance.

[Verse 5]
Q — Qajaq! Greenlandic traditional narrow boat behind the word kayak | one-person watercraft belongs to skilled Arctic travel.
R ... R ... Reindeer! Reindeer are caribou | broad hooves move over snow and tundra beside our land route.
S? Seal! Flippers take over where feet would fail | marine wildlife at the edge between travel modes.
T is Tern. Pointed wings cross above shore and water | a sky route we only watch.

[Chorus]
Map, route, change—A-B-C!
Land, ice, water tell us how to travel next.

[Verse 6]
U — Umiak! Traditional open Arctic boat carries people and supplies | a different watercraft for a different travel need.
V ... V ... Velvet Scoter! Dark sea duck with pale wing patch | water-and-air clue near the marine leg.
W? Walrus! Heavy marine mammal with tusks and whiskers | keep distance at shore or ice edge.
X is X-ray Fish. Partly transparent tropical freshwater fish | an X mapping helper, clearly not an Arctic expedition species.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow-billed Loon! Large northern waterbird with a yellow bill | one final navigation color near cold water.
Z ... Z ... Zoarcid Fish! Eel-shaped cold-water fish | final clue stays below surface as our route turns back to shore.

Map folded for one beat—what route changes can we remember?
