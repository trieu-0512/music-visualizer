[Verse 1]
Across the snow and ice, A finds Arctic Fox. White in the light | thick fur warm in Arctic white
B — Beluga. A beluga is a pale whale with a rounded forehead that lives in cold northern seas.
What waits at C? Cod! Blue below | ocean swimmer on the go
D is for Dog Sled. A dog sled rides on long runners over snow and can be pulled by a trained team of sled dogs.

[Verse 2]
Ermine — our E. It is a small weasel whose coat can turn white in winter in cold regions.
Across the snow and ice, F finds Floe. An ice floe is a flat piece of floating sea ice.
G — Glacier. Ice moves slow | glacier creeping down below
What waits at H? Husky! It is a thick-coated working dog with strong legs and paws suited to cold conditions.

Across the snow and ice, K finds Kittiwake. Feather light | beak and wings come into sight
L — Lemming. A lemming is a small northern rodent that lives on tundra and eats plants.

[Verse 4]
What waits at M? Musk Ox! Hooves on ground | steady little steps go round
N is for Narwhal. A narwhal is a whale that can have one long spiral tusk.
Orca — our O. Blue below | ocean swimmer on the go

What waits at R? Reindeer! Reindeer are caribou; their broad hooves help them travel over snow and soft ground.
S is for Seal. Sweep and spin | flippers pull the seal back in
Tern — our T. It is a slender seabird with pointed wings that often dives toward the water for food.

[Verse 6]
Across the snow and ice, U finds Umiak. It is a traditional open Arctic boat used to carry people and supplies.
V — Velvet Scoter. A velvet scoter is a dark sea duck with a pale wing patch.
What waits at W? Walrus! Tusks in view | whiskered walrus in the blue
X is for X-ray Fish. An X-ray fish has a partly see-through body that can reveal tiny bones.

[Verse 7]
Yellow-billed Loon — our Y. Wing to sky | feathered bird goes gliding by
Across the snow and ice, Z finds Zoarcid Fish. It is an eel-shaped fish from a family often called eelpouts.
