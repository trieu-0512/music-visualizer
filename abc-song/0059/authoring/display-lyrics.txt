[Intro]
One trackway crosses mud; one pterosaur belongs overhead; one marine reptile never walked this trail.
We follow evidence, not imaginary live dinosaurs.

[Verse 1]
A — Allosaurus! Large Jurassic land predator | three-fingered hands and theropod feet fit a terrestrial trail.
B ... B ... Brachiosaurus! Giant sauropod herbivore | columnar limbs carried an enormous land body.
C? Carnotaurus! South American theropod with brow horns and tiny forelimbs | land-animal clue.
D is Diplodocus. Long-bodied sauropod with long tail and neck | tracks and limb anatomy belong to ground travel.

[Verse 2]
E — Edmontosaurus! Hadrosaur that walked on two or four limbs | broad beak marks a plant-eating trail clue.
F ... F ... Footprint! Trace fossil records contact with ancient ground | the trail itself becomes evidence.
G? Gastonia! Armored herbivore | plates and spikes belong to land, not water armor.
H is Herrerasaurus. Triassic predatory dinosaur from Argentina | an early dinosaur from a much older chapter than Tyrannosaurus.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguanodon! Large herbivorous ornithopod with thumb spike | terrestrial browser along the route.
J ... J ... Jawbone! Fossil jaw reveals feeding anatomy | a trail-side evidence object, not a roaming animal.
K? Kritosaurus! Hadrosaurid herbivore | broad-billed skull fits the plant-eating section.
L is Lesothosaurus. Small lightly built ornithischian | quick terrestrial body in the low-vegetation frame.

[Chorus]
Track, nest, shore—A-B-C!
Change the habitat when the evidence tells us to.

[Verse 4]
M — Mosasaurus! Huge marine squamate reptile | ocean predator, not a dinosaur and not a land-trail walker.
N ... N ... Nest! Eggs and nest structure anchor breeding behavior to a place | habitat evidence without chasing adults.
O? Oviraptor! Feathered beaked theropod known brooding nests | nesting evidence corrected the old egg-thief story.
P is Pteranodon. Flying pterosaur from marine-coastal settings | overhead or shore context, not dinosaur footsteps.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quetzalcoatlus! Giant pterosaur likely spent much time on land as well as flying | still a pterosaur, not a dinosaur.
R ... R ... Raptor! Broad popular label for dromaeosaurid-style predators | use exact genus when evidence allows.
S? Spinosaurus! Giant spinosaurid with sail and crocodile-like snout | evidence supports strong association with aquatic environments, though exact lifestyle remains studied.
T is Tyrannosaurus. Late Cretaceous land predator | deep skull and powerful hind limbs fit terrestrial travel.

[Verse 6]
U — Utahraptor! Large dromaeosaurid from Early Cretaceous North America | terrestrial predator with sickle claws.
V ... V ... Volcano! Geological setting can lay down ash beds used to date and correlate fossil layers | landscape evidence, not animal habitat by itself.
W? Wuerhosaurus! Cretaceous stegosaur from Asia | plated land herbivore on the terrestrial branch.
X is Xixiasaurus. Small theropod from China | limited fossils make exact habitat details uncertain.

[Chorus]
Track, nest, shore—A-B-C!
Change the habitat when the evidence tells us to.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yinlong! Small early ceratopsian from Jurassic China | land herbivore near the base of horned-dinosaur evolution.
Z ... Z ... Zephyrosaurus! Small ornithopod from Early Cretaceous North America | final light-footed land clue.
