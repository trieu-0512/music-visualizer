[Intro]
Sun climbs; the shadow of one cactus gets shorter.
Desert life is not one trick—it is many ways to handle heat and little water.

[Verse 1]
A — Armadillo! Mammal with bony armor plates | low body searches dry ground when conditions are safer.
B ... B ... Beetle! Six legs cross sand or stone | small bodies often use timing and shelter to avoid the fiercest heat.
C? Cactus! Thick tissues can store water | spines replace broad leaves on many desert species.
D is Desert Tortoise. Hard shell above, burrow below | underground shelter helps escape extreme heat.

[Verse 2]
E — Euphorbia! Some desert euphorbias have thick succulent stems | another plant strategy for dry places.
F ... F ... Fennec! Very large ears help release heat and catch faint sounds | small fox built for desert nights.
G? Gecko! Small lizard grips rock or wall | shade and cooler hours matter more than standing in open noon.
H is Hawk. Wings ride rising air above hot ground | a bird using the sky while shadows shrink below.

[Chorus]
Sun, shade, one drop—A-B-C!
Desert life makes every cool place count.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguana! Scaled body and long tail | a lizard seeks the right place and time rather than one magical heat-proof trick.
J ... J ... Jackrabbit! Huge ears can release body heat | long hind legs cross open ground quickly.
K? Kangaroo Rat! Powerful hind legs, small night-active body | desert life often waits for cooler darkness.
L is Limestone. Pale rock holds no water-saving behavior | it simply changes how heat, shade and ground feel around it.

[Verse 4]
M — Meerkat! Small mammal stands to scan, then returns to group or shelter | open sun is only part of its day.
N ... N ... Nopales! Flat prickly-pear pads are succulent cactus tissue | thick green surfaces hold moisture.
O? Oasis! Water and shade gather in one dry landscape | green becomes possible where moisture reaches roots.
P is Palm. Leaf crown lifts over a trunk or stems | oasis shade can spread beneath broad fronds.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quartz! Hard glassy mineral catches sun | a heat-and-light clue, not a living adaptation.
R ... R ... Rattlesnake! Rattle at the tail, scaled body | venomous wildlife is observed only from a safe distance.
S? Scorpion! Pincers, many walking legs, curved stinging tail | another animal we never touch or approach.
T is Tortoise. Low shell and sturdy feet | slow travel makes shade and timing especially important.

[Chorus]
Sun, shade, one drop—A-B-C!
Desert life makes every cool place count.

[Verse 6]
U — Urial! Wild sheep with sure hooves | shade, terrain and access to water shape life in dry country.
V ... V ... Vulture! Broad wings soar efficiently | height and moving air can reduce the need for constant flapping.
W? Wren! Small bird uses cover, shade and brief movement | not every desert strategy is about storing water inside a body.
X is Xerophyte. A plant adapted to dry conditions | the word itself points to surviving where water is scarce.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Scorpion! Pale desert scorpion | keep distance and let shade belong to wildlife too.
Z ... Z ... Ziziphus Shrub! Hardy thorny shrub can bear small fruits | woody shade holds on through dry conditions.
