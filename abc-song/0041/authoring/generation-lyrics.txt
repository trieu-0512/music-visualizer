[Intro]
Hello, mountain—one call, then wait.
The cliff sends part of our voice back across the air.

[Verse 1]
A — Aspen! Pale trunks rise where mountain soil and moisture allow | leaves can tremble in a breeze while our echo stays in the rock.
B ... B ... Bighorn Sheep! Split hooves grip steep terrain | heavy curved horns belong to adults, not to the cliff itself.
C? Cliff! A steep rock face catches our first call | sound returns only after the target name is clear.
D is Douglas Fir. Tall evergreen, hanging cones | a quiet vertical landmark beside louder stone.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Broad wings cross open air | the bird gives no echo, but its shape travels ridge to ridge.
F ... F ... Fir! Evergreen needles hold through cold seasons | branches frame the view without making a sound cue.
G? Glacier! Thick ice moves slowly downhill under its own weight | a long mountain motion, almost silent on our song's timescale.
H is Hawk. Keen-eyed raptor rides moving air | one small silhouette above the echo line.

[Chorus]
Call, return, fade—A-B-C!
Rock gives back a sound, then the valley lets it go.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Sure hooves handle steep rocky ground | curved horns rise above a body built for mountain travel.
J ... J ... Juniper! Evergreen shrub or tree with scale- or needle-like foliage | a low green anchor near stone.
K? Kettle Lake! A depression left when buried glacier ice melted can fill with water | an old ice story held in a quiet basin.
L is Ledge. Narrow shelf of rock | we name it from a safe viewpoint and never step toward an exposed edge.

[Verse 4]
M — Mountain! Slopes rise into ridges and summit | the broad landform carrying every smaller clue.
N ... N ... Nest! Twigs or other materials make a place for eggs and young | we look without disturbing it.
O? Owl! Broad wings and forward-facing eyes | night flight belongs to the bird, not to a daytime echo trick.
P is Pine. Needle bundles and woody cones | evergreen shape holds steady while sound fades.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground bird with quick steps and short rounded body | a low moving clue beneath high rock.
R ... R ... Ridge! Long narrow high ground | one skyline line can separate near slope from far valley.
S? Summit! Highest point of a hill or mountain | a destination we can identify without pretending every route is safe.
T is Trail. A route people follow through outdoor terrain | good paths guide feet while cliffs guide our eyes.

[Chorus]
Call, return, fade—A-B-C!
Rock gives back a sound, then the valley lets it go.

[Verse 6]
U — Urial! Wild sheep with sure hooves | dry mountain slopes fit a different animal than the ibex but demand the same respect.
V ... V ... Vulture! Broad wings soar on rising air | scavenging helps recycle nutrients through the ecosystem.
W? Waterfall! Water drops over rock | continuous rush can mask a tiny echo, so the arrangement leaves the target dry.
At the shelter, X is Xenolith. A rock fragment held inside another rock makes a visible mountain clue.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarrow! Flat clusters of tiny flowers grow on a leafy stem in open mountain meadows.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Trail! Switchbacks bend across a slope | back-and-forth routing can reduce steepness on the climb.

[Chorus]
Call, return, fade—A-B-C!
Rock gives back a sound, then the valley lets it go.
