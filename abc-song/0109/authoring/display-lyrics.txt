[Intro]
Canvas is a surface, paint is a material, brush is a tool, drawing is a result.
Creative work gets clearer when those roles do not blur.

[Verse 1]
A — Apron! | Protective garment helps keep paint or glue off clothing but does not replace hand or surface cleanup.
B ... B ... Brush! | Bristles or fibers carry a medium and release it through strokes; shape and stiffness change the mark.
C? Canvas! | Woven fabric or prepared board provides a durable painting surface; priming changes absorbency.
D is Drawing. Image built mainly from lines, shapes, values, or marks rather than a single specific tool. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eraser! | Material lifts many graphite marks; it cannot clean every ink, paint, or surface without damage.
F ... F ... Frame! | Border or support surrounds artwork and helps present or protect it without changing the image itself.
G? Glitter! | Tiny reflective particles add sparkle but can irritate eyes or spread easily; use supervised craft material and avoid inhalation.
H is Handprint. Printed hand silhouette records palm-and-finger shape; use washable skin-safe material and wash afterward. |

[Verse 3]
I — Ink! | Pigment- or dye-carrying liquid makes marks and can stain; containers stay closed when not in use.
J ... J ... Jewel Sticker! | Adhesive decorative gem-like piece adds texture and shine; small pieces require supervision.
K? Knitting Needle! | Long pointed tool forms knitted loops with yarn; preschoolers observe or use only with direct adult guidance.
L is Loom. Frame holds lengthwise warp threads while other material passes across as weft. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marker! | Ink-fed tip makes broad or fine lines; caps help many markers resist drying.
N ... N ... Needle! | Thin pointed sewing tool passes thread through material; sharp point makes it adult-managed in preschool art.
O? Origami! | Paper-folding practice builds forms through planned creases; traditional designs can avoid cutting or glue.
P is Paint. Pigment in a binder forms a colored layer on a surface; opacity, drying, and cleanup depend on paint type. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilling Paper! | Narrow paper strips are coiled, pinched, and arranged into decorative shapes.
R ... R ... Ribbon! | Narrow woven strip bends and ties easily; long loose lengths can tangle and need supervision.
S? Sponge! | Porous material absorbs and releases paint, making soft blocks, dabs, or textures when pressed.
T is Tempera Paint. Water-based opaque school paint dries matte and cleans differently from permanent artist paints. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Brush! | General-purpose brush can spread paint, glue, or other craft media; exact use depends on its bristles and material.
V ... V ... Velvet Paper! | Flocked or velvet-like paper has a soft raised surface that changes how light and touch reveal color.
W? Watercolor! | Water-soluble paint can make transparent washes; more water usually lightens and spreads the pigment.
X is X-shaped Sticker. Adhesive sticker carries an X outline; shape is the learning clue, not a cutting instruction. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Paint! | Yellow-colored paint follows the same binder-and-pigment behavior as its paint type; color alone does not define opacity.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Craft! | Project craft uses a zipper as a functional or decorative element; exact construction depends on the designed activity.
