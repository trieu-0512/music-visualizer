[Intro]
Check the board, keep the ticket, find the platform, board the passenger car—not the freight car.
A railway journey has stages and different kinds of cars.

[Verse 1]
A — Arrival Board! | Electronic or printed display lists expected arrivals and platform/service information; times can change with operations.
B ... B ... Brake! | Train braking system slows wheelsets using friction, electrical/dynamic braking, air pressure, or combinations depending rolling stock.
C? Coach! | Passenger rail car, especially for seated accommodation; coach is not the locomotive.
D is Diesel Engine. Compression-ignition engine burns diesel fuel; diesel locomotives often use it to drive a generator feeding electric traction motors. |

[Verse 2]
E — Electric Train! | Train draws electrical energy from overhead wire, third rail, batteries, or other sources depending system; traction motors turn wheels.
F ... F ... Freight Car! | Rail vehicle carries goods rather than passengers; boxcars, hoppers, tank cars, flats, and other types fit different loads.
G? Goods Wagon! | Freight rail vehicle used to carry goods; British/Commonwealth term overlaps freight car.
H is High-speed Train. Passenger train designed for sustained high speeds on suitable infrastructure; dedicated track geometry, signaling, and grade separation are common safety requirements. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 3]
I — Intercity Train! | Passenger service links cities, usually with fewer stops and longer distances than local transit.
J ... J ... Junction Box! | Protected enclosure houses electrical connections; railway electrical equipment is for trained workers, not passenger handling.
K? Key Switch! | Locked/key-operated control allows authorized operation of equipment; passengers do not operate railway control switches.
L is Level Crossing. Place where road/path crosses railway at the same level; users obey lights, bells, barriers, and never go around a closed gate. |

[Verse 4]
M — Metro! | Urban rapid-transit rail system; may use tunnels, elevated structures, or surface sections.
N ... N ... Night Train! | Train service scheduled overnight; may include ordinary seats or sleeping accommodation depending route.
O? Observation Car! | Passenger car designed with large windows or viewing areas for scenery; it is a service car, not a driving cab.
P is Passenger Car. Rail car carries seated or standing passengers; doors, seating, and amenities vary by service. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiet Car! | Passenger area with reduced conversation/device noise under operator rules.
R ... R ... Railway Signal! | Visual/electronic signal communicates movement authority or restrictions to train crews; meanings depend on the railway rulebook.
S? Sleeper Car! | Passenger car provides berths or compartments for overnight travel; it supports rest, not train propulsion.
T is Ticket. Proof of fare/payment or travel authorization; it carries journey information but does not control the train. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Car! | Rail vehicle assigned maintenance, inspection, work, or support functions; exact equipment varies by railway.
V ... V ... Viaduct! | Long bridge-like structure carries rail or road across a valley, streets, or low ground using repeated spans/supports.
W? Waiting Room! | Station room provides sheltered waiting; official signs still guide boarding.
X is Xpress Train. Project label for an express-style train service; 'Xpress' is branding/spelling, while express generally means fewer stops or faster service pattern. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Signal! | Railway yellow aspect commonly indicates caution or a restrictive condition ahead, but exact meaning depends on the signaling system; drivers follow local rules.
Z ... Z ... Zone Map! | Map divides a transit network into fare, service, or geographic zones; colors/labels explain boundaries.
