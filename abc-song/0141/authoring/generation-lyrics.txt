[Intro]
Ferry carries people, barge carries cargo, tug works close to ships, wake is only the water left behind.
A harbor alphabet holds more than boats.

[Verse 1]
A — Airboat! | Flat-bottomed boat driven by an aircraft-style propeller above water; propeller cage and trained operation are essential.
B ... B ... Barge! | Broad cargo vessel, often unpowered and pushed/towed on rivers or canals; some barges are self-propelled.
C? Canoe! | Narrow open craft commonly paddled with single-bladed paddles; PFD and water conditions matter.
D is Dinghy. Small boat used independently or as a tender; sail, oar, or motor propulsion depends on design. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Marine engine supplies propulsion power through propeller, jet, or other drive system; type varies by vessel.
F ... F ... Ferry! | Vessel carries passengers, vehicles, or both across a route on a scheduled or repeated service.
G? Gondola! | Traditional Venetian flat-bottomed rowing boat propelled with a single oar from the stern side; not every narrow boat is a gondola.
H is Harbor. Sheltered coastal/water area where vessels anchor, berth, load, or seek protection; traffic follows local navigation rules. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Boat! | Flexible hull gains shape from pressurized air chambers; puncture risk, capacity, PFDs, and conditions matter.
J ... J ... Jolly Boat! | Traditional small ship's boat/tender; project label should stay historical rather than implying a modern standard type.
K? Keel! | Longitudinal structural/underwater member helps strength and, on many sailboats, stability and resistance to sideways drift.
L is Lifeboat. Rescue/survival craft designed to protect people in emergencies; operation belongs to trained crews and procedures. |

[Verse 4]
M — Motorboat! | Small powered boat propelled by inboard, outboard, jet, or other motor system; speed/handling require licensed or trained adults.
N ... N ... Narrowboat! | Long narrow canal boat designed for inland waterways and locks; not an ocean-going shape by default.
O? Oar! | Long lever works from an oarlock to row; unlike a paddle, it is usually fixed against the boat while stroking.
P is Paddleboat. Boat propelled by human pedaling/paddle wheels or other paddle mechanism depending design; rental rules and PFDs still apply. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quay! | Solid waterside structure where vessels berth to load/unload; edges and mooring equipment are work/safety zones.
R ... R ... Raft! | Buoyant platform or simple craft; stability, materials, current, and flotation equipment determine safe use.
S? Sailboat! | Boat uses sails to convert wind force into forward motion while keel/centerboard and rudder help control course.
T is Tugboat. Powerful workboat pushes or tows ships/barges; close-quarters harbor work is professional operation. |

[Verse 6]
U — Utility Boat! | Workboat supports transport, maintenance, inspection, or service jobs; exact layout depends on mission.
V ... V ... Voyage Map! | Navigation/planning map shows route, hazards, places, and scale; real navigation also uses current charts and instruments.
W? Wake! | Disturbed water pattern trails a moving vessel; wake size depends on speed, hull, depth, and conditions.
X is Xebec. Historic Mediterranean sailing vessel, often lateen-rigged with multiple masts; it is a historical craft type. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yawl! | Sailboat rig with a small mizzen mast aft of the rudder post in the traditional definition.
Z ... Z ... Zephyr Sail! | Project poetic label for a sail shown in light wind; zephyr means a gentle breeze, not a standardized sail type.
