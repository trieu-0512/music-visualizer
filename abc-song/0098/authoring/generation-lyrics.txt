[Intro]
Waffle gives a grid; macaron gives two shells; zebra cake gives stripes inside the slice.
Bakery structure can be read like geometry.

[Verse 1]
A — Angel Cake! Tall light porous crumb | foam structure is the picture clue.
B ... B ... Bread! Crust surrounds a cellular crumb | loaf cross-section shows gas pockets.
C? Cookie! Usually flatter than cake, with edge/surface pattern carrying the silhouette.
D is Donut. Ring or filled round geometry | shape varies, so hole is not universal.

[Verse 2]
E — Eclair! Long hollow choux shell designed to hold filling | shell-and-center geometry.
F ... F ... Frosting! Thick surface layer can be smooth, ridged, piped, or swirled | coating geometry.
G? Glaze! Thin coating follows the surface beneath it | translucent/shiny layer.
H is Hot Cross Bun. Rounded bun marked with a cross | top pattern supplies the name clue.

[Verse 3]
I — Ice Cream! Scoop/curl shape is serving geometry, not baked crumb | frozen exception.
J ... J ... Jam Tart! Shallow circular shell with visible filling center | open geometry.
K? Kolache! Soft yeasted round with visible central filling in traditional forms | center-ring contrast.
L is Lemon Tart. Shallow shell plus smooth filling surface | edge versus center.

[Chorus]
Shape, layer, crumb—A-B-C!
Clap after the name; let the structure speak first.

[Verse 4]
M — Macaron! Two round almond-meringue shells, ruffled feet, filling band | layered sandwich geometry.
N ... N ... Nut Bread! Loaf crumb dotted with irregular nut inclusions | inclusion pattern.
O? Orange Cake! Slice exposes cake crumb and possible layers/topping | cross-section clue.
P is Pretzel. Interlaced loop/knot path | topology-like shape is more distinctive than crumb.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Cake! Small rich cake traditionally baked in individual portions | mapping focuses on cake form without inventing one universal decoration.
R ... R ... Raisin Bread! Loaf cross-section dotted with raisin inclusions | embedded pattern.
S? Scone! Wedge, round, or thick disk | crumb is quick-bread-like rather than laminated.
T is Toast. Flat slice with browned surface and porous interior | before/after surface contrast.

[Chorus]
Shape, layer, crumb—A-B-C!
Clap after the name; let the structure speak first.

[Verse 6]
U — Upside-down Cake! Fruit/topping pattern ends on top after inversion | orientation is part of the structure story.
V ... V ... Vanilla Cake! Layers, crumb and frosting form a stacked cross-section | flavor name alone does not define shape.
W? Wholegrain Bread! Loaf crumb can show bran/grain/seed flecks depending formula | grain pattern is irregular, not decorative certainty.
X is X-shaped Pretzel. Two dough paths cross into a clear X outline | geometry drives the mapping.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yeast Roll! Rounded individual bread with soft cellular crumb | rise creates internal pores.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Cake! Alternating light/dark batters create stripe patterns in the cut cake | pattern lives inside.

[Interlude]
The storybook bakery window goes still. One warm chord—then the picture names return.
