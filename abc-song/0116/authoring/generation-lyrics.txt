[Intro]
Blocks stack, a gyroscope spins, a kaleidoscope reflects, a jigsaw fits.
A toy box can hold many different kinds of cause and effect.

[Verse 1]
A — Alphabet Blocks! | Blocks carry letters or pictures on faces; stacking adds spatial play while letter faces support recognition.
B ... B ... Blocks! | Separate solid pieces can stack, line up, balance, or build; stability depends on base width and center of mass.
C? Crayon! | Wax drawing stick adds mark-making to play; it belongs on suitable paper, not toys, walls, or mouths.
D is Dominoes. Flat tiles can be matched by markings or stood in chains; game rules and toppling play are different activities. |

[Verse 2]
E — Etch-a-Sketch! | Classic mechanical drawing toy uses two knobs to move a stylus horizontally and vertically behind a screen.
F ... F ... Frisbee! | Flying disc glides after a spinning throw; outdoor space and distance from people or roads matter.
G? Gyroscope! | Spinning rotor tends to resist changes to its orientation through angular momentum; toy versions demonstrate rotation.
H is Hoop. Circular ring supports rolling, tossing, stepping, or waist-spinning games depending size and setting. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Ball! | Air-filled flexible ball deforms and rebounds; inflation level and size affect bounce and handling.
J ... J ... Jigsaw! | Puzzle of interlocking pieces reconstructs one image or shape by matching edges and visual clues.
K? Kaleidoscope! | Mirrors inside a tube reflect colored pieces into repeated symmetrical patterns as the view changes.
L is Lego Brick. Interlocking plastic construction brick uses studs and tubes to connect; small pieces require age-appropriate supervision. |

[Verse 4]
M — Model Car! | Small-scale representation of a car; wheels may roll, but exact mechanism depends on the model.
N ... N ... Number Puzzle! | Puzzle uses numerals or quantities as matching clues, combining shape fitting with number recognition.
O? Origami! | Paper folding can become a play object or model through creases; it is paper craft, not a manufactured toy mechanism.
P is Puzzle. Problem or object designed to be solved by matching, arranging, reasoning, or fitting pieces. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiz Game! | Question-and-answer game tests recall or reasoning; a missed answer is a chance to learn, not a judgment of the player.
R ... R ... Robot! | Toy or model robot represents a machine that can sense, compute, or move; not every robot toy is autonomous.
S? Scooter! | Wheeled ride-on platform steered by handlebars in common designs; helmet, suitable area, and supervision matter.
T is Teddy Bear. Soft stuffed bear-shaped toy supports comfort or pretend play; loose eyes, seams, and stuffing must stay secure. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uno Cards! | Commercial card-game deck uses colors, numbers, and action symbols; play follows its published rules rather than ordinary playing-card rules.
V ... V ... Velcro Ball! | Project toy uses hook-and-loop material so a light ball can stick to a matching target or mitt.
W? Wagon! | Low wheeled cart can carry toys or be pulled; riding rules depend on design and adult supervision.
X is Xylophone Toy. Toy with bar-like keys can introduce pitch patterns; toy tuning may be approximate rather than concert-accurate. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn Doll! | Figure made by wrapping and tying yarn; loose fibers or long strands require supervised craft/play.
Z ... Z ... Zoom Car! | Project label for a small toy car; do not invent a pull-back, battery, or friction motor unless the pictured model shows one.
