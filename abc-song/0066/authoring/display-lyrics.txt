[Intro]
A dove coos. A woodpecker drums. A kiwi may be easier to spot than to hear.
Bird sound has more than one voice.

[Verse 1]
A — Avocet! Long upcurved bill sweeps shallow water | wading shape may identify it before any call.
B ... B ... Budgie! Small parrot with curved bill and zygodactyl feet | social chirps and chatter can fill a flock.
C? Cardinal! Stout seed-cracking bill and red adult male plumage in Northern Cardinals | whistles carry through brush.
D is Dove. Soft coos are classic contact and courtship sounds | rounded body and small head back up the ear clue.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Large raptor with hooked bill and broad wings | often a visual sky clue rather than a 'songbird' example.
F ... F ... Finch! Small seed-eating bird group with varied calls | bill shape differs among species.
G? Goldfinch! Small finch with conical bill | flight calls and bright plumage can both help identification.
H is Heron. Long legs, neck, and spear-like bill | harsh croaks may surprise anyone expecting a sweet song.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibis! Long downcurved bill probes wet ground | flock calls are only one clue among posture and habitat.
J ... J ... Junco! Ground-feeding sparrow with simple trills in many populations | white outer tail feathers can flash in flight.
K? Kiwi! Flightless nocturnal New Zealand bird with nostrils near the bill tip | ground calls replace any wingbeat clue.
L is Lark. Many larks sing in open habitats, sometimes during display flight | real song example, not a rule for all birds.

[Chorus]
Call, song, drum—A-B-C!
Listen for the source before you name the bird.

[Verse 4]
M — Magpie! Corvid with varied calls and complex social behavior | black-white pattern identifies some familiar species.
N ... N ... Nest! Structure for eggs or young | evidence of bird activity, not a bird and never something to disturb.
O? Owl! Hoots, screeches, whistles, or other calls vary by species | broad wings can be remarkably quiet in flight.
P is Parrot. Curved bill and grasping feet | vocal learning is strong in many parrots, but wild calls serve social functions.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quetzal! Tropical trogon with brilliant plumage in some species | calls and long tail coverts can both identify males.
R ... R ... Robin! Thrush-like song and orange breast identify the American Robin | ground foraging adds a behavior clue.
S? Sparrow! Broad everyday group name covering many small seed-eating birds | chirps and songs vary widely.
T is Toucan. Huge colorful bill makes the visual ID first | croaks and rattles sound nothing like a generic tweet.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Sandpiper! Long-necked grassland shorebird relative | distinctive whistled calls carry over open fields.
V ... V ... Veery! Thrush famous for descending, flute-like song | one strong acoustic specialist in the set.
W? Woodpecker! Bill strikes wood for feeding and drumming | drumming is mechanical communication, not vocal song.
X is Xenops. Small tropical bird that searches bark and branches for insects | behavior can identify it when sound does not.

[Chorus]
Call, song, drum—A-B-C!
Listen for the source before you name the bird.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Small yellow songbird with thin bill | sweet notes belong to a real songbird, not the whole class Aves.
Z ... Z ... Zenaida Dove! Caribbean-region dove with soft cooing calls | dove-like body and voice close the acoustic map.
