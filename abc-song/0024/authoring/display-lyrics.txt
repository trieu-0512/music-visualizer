[Intro]
Notebook open. Pencil ready. Hands stay gentle.
A good scout notices more than a hurried walker.

[Verse 1]
Field note A — Acorn. Hard oak seed with a little cup | sketch the shape and leave forest food where it fell.
B ... B ... Bear! Broad paw shape is a distance clue | if a bear is nearby, we stay far away with an adult.
C? Chipmunk! Quick stripes and a darting shape | observe the small mammal without following it off trail.
D is Deer. Split hoofprints can mark soft ground | the animal itself deserves quiet space.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Wide-wing silhouette high above | one field mark seen without needing to get closer.
F ... F ... Fern! Divided fronds repeat a feather-like pattern | draw the curve instead of pulling a leaf.
G? Grouse! Sturdy ground bird, quick burst of flight | note where it was and let it move away.
H is Hollow Log. Empty spaces can shelter small animals | look at the opening and never reach inside.

[Verse 3]
I — Ivy! Climbing stems trace bark upward | follow the line with your eyes, not by pulling the plant.
J ... J ... Juniper! Evergreen leaves and berry-like seed cones | notice color and shape; do not taste wild plants.
K? Knot! Hard swirl in a trunk or log | a perfect pencil-shape showing where a branch once grew.
L is Log. Fallen wood can become habitat | record bark, cracks, and moss while leaving the log in place.

[Chorus]
Clue, sketch, check—A-B-C!
See it, name it, leave it where it should be.

[Verse 4]
M — Mushroom! Cap and stem rise from damp ground | wild mushrooms are for looking, never tasting.
N ... N ... Nest! Twigs form a cup for eggs or chicks | watch from far enough away not to disturb it.
O? Owl! Round head, broad wings, hooked beak | a silhouette or distant call may be all the clue we need.
P is Pinecone. Overlapping woody scales make a repeating pattern | sketch one where it lies.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Small ground bird, quick feet | one short observation before it returns to cover.
R ... R ... Rabbit! Long ears and strong hind legs | note the shape, then give the animal room.
S? Spruce! Needle-like leaves crowd around twigs | compare the branch shape without breaking one off.
T is Trail. The safest clue is also our boundary | stay on the path where the place asks us to.

[Chorus]
Clue, sketch, check—A-B-C!
See it, name it, leave it where it should be.

[Verse 6]
U — Underbrush! Shrubs and low plants make dense cover | a habitat clue we look into, not push through.
V ... V ... Vine! Long stem climbs or trails | trace its route with one finger in the air.
W? Woodpecker! Beak marks and drumming can point to a trunk | watch upward without crowding the bird.
X is Xylem. Tiny tubes carry water through a stem | note the hidden plant pathway beneath the woodland leaves.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Small yellow bird among leaves | color and song become two field marks at once.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Swallowtail! Pale striped wings and long tails | let the butterfly choose its own path while we sketch.

[Interlude]
Pencil down for one beat—what clues can we recall without touching them?
