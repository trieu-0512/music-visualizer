[Intro]
Start where the light is dim. Now follow one trunk upward.
The rainforest changes every few meters above our heads.

[Verse 1]
A — Anaconda! Heavy scaled body stays close to water and low cover | our vertical map begins near the bottom.
B ... B ... Banana! Huge leaves spread from a tall herb | curved fruit hangs below the green fans.
C? Chameleon! Gripping feet hold branch or twig | eyes scan sideways while the body stays nearly still.
D is Drongo. Agile dark bird crosses an open gap | forked or notched tail cutting through brighter air.

[Verse 2]
E — Emerald Tree Boa! Green coils rest around branches | one snake truly belonging in the tree layer.
F ... F ... Frog! Strong back legs launch from wet ground or leaf | some rainforest frogs climb, others stay low.
G? Gorilla! Powerful primate often moving on the forest floor | grasping hands can also help with climbing.
H is Hornbill. Curved bill and casque show high against a light gap | broad wings cross between crowns.

[Chorus]
Low, middle, high—A-B-C!
Sunlight threads the canopy layer by layer.

[Verse 3]
I — Insect! Six legs can work at every height | under litter, on bark, or among leaves above.
J ... J ... Jaguar! Spotted cat moves mainly through ground cover | but strong claws can take it up a tree.
K? Kapok Tree! Massive trunk rises from the shaded floor | branches spread into the bright upper forest.
L is Liana. Woody vine begins low and climbs by using trees for support | a living line between layers.

[Verse 4]
M — Monkey! Hands grip, feet push, body swings | branch to branch makes the middle and upper forest connected.
N ... N ... Nectar Bat! Small wings move through flower-rich spaces | a long tongue reaches nectar where blooms open.
O? Ocelot! Spotted wild cat works through dense lower cover | a low-layer contrast to the birds overhead.
P is Palm. Trunk or strong stems lift a crown of broad leaves | green fans catching higher light.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Butterfly! Orange-brown wings cross sunlit gaps | small flutter between leaves at different heights.
R ... R ... Rattan! Climbing palm sends long flexible stems through surrounding vegetation | another route upward.
S? Snake! Some stay low, some climb | a long scaled body can use very different rainforest layers.
T is Toucan. Huge bill, perched body | one unmistakable silhouette high in the leafy crown.

[Chorus]
Low, middle, high—A-B-C!
Sunlight threads the canopy layer by layer.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella Leaf! Broad leaf catches light like a green roof | shade forms beneath it.
V ... V ... Vine! Stem reaches and trails through support | vertical green stitching one layer to another.
W? Waterfall! It ignores our neat layers | water drops from high rock toward the forest floor in one long line.
X is Xenops. Small tropical bird works along bark and branches | searching surfaces for insects.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Anaconda! Large blotched snake stays tied to low wet habitat | another reason not to call every rainforest animal a canopy animal.
Z ... Z ... Zingiber Plant! Leafy stems rise from rhizomes underground | a tropical plant closing our map near the lower green.

[Chorus]
Low, middle, high—A-B-C!
Sunlight threads the canopy layer by layer.

Look down from the canopy for one quiet beat—what layers can we remember?
