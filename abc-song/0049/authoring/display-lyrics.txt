[Intro]
Wind moves air. Drizzle moves drops. Frost holds still on a cold edge.
Three textures can share one day without becoming the same thing.

[Verse 1]
A — Anemometer! Wind turns the sensor | instrument belongs to the whoosh family only as a measurement tool.
B ... B ... Boots! Footwear responds to wet or cold ground | gear, not precipitation.
C? Coat! Outer layer changes comfort | response object outside our three physical textures.
D is Drizzle. Fine liquid drops fall lightly | tap texture begins with actual water.

[Verse 2]
E — Eye of Storm! Some tropical cyclones have a calmer center | storm structure, not a rhythm effect.
F ... F ... Frost! Ice crystals form on cold surfaces | hush texture stays still rather than falling.
G? Gust! Brief increase in wind speed | one sharper whoosh inside moving air.
H is Humidity Meter. Hygrometer measures moisture in air | data clue connecting vapor and cloud formation.

[Verse 3]
I — Ice! Solid frozen water | same substance as rainwater, different phase and texture.
J ... J ... Jet Stream! Strong high-altitude wind band | huge-scale whoosh we cannot feel directly from the ground.
K? Kelvin Thermometer! Temperature in kelvins | measurement helps explain phase changes without becoming weather itself.
L is Lightning. Electrical discharge flashes during storms | thunder may follow; shelter beats rhythm play.

[Verse 4]

N ... N ... Nimbus Cloud! Rain-bearing cloud idea | source region for liquid or frozen precipitation depending conditions.
O? Overcoat! Warm outer layer | human response to cold or wet conditions.
P is Puddle. Liquid water collects after rain | tap texture becomes a still ground clue.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilt! Indoor warmth layer | not field gear and not weather.
R ... R ... Raincoat! Water-resistant outerwear | response to liquid precipitation.
S? Sun! Solar energy drives atmospheric heating | clearing does not belong to wind, rain, or snow texture itself.
T is Thunder. Sound follows lightning because air heats and expands rapidly | safety cue, not percussion for children outdoors.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella! Ordinary rain cover | useful in some liquid-rain conditions, unsafe as a lightning solution.
V ... V ... Vapor! Invisible water gas | water can change phase before cloud droplets become visible.
W? Windsock! Fabric tube aligns with wind | visible measurement-like clue for the whoosh family.
X is X-band Radar. Radar can reveal precipitation structure | screen clue connecting rain or snow bands to observation.
