[Intro]
Dock first, gangway next, passenger vessel after that; workboats and rescue craft keep their own jobs.
A boat day has a passenger path just like a station or airport.

[Verse 1]
A — Anchor! | Heavy device grips seabed/riverbed to limit vessel drift; dropping/retrieving it requires awareness of depth, bottom, and lines.
B ... B ... Barge! | Broad cargo vessel, often unpowered and pushed/towed on rivers or canals; some barges are self-propelled.
C? Catamaran! | Boat with two parallel hulls connected by a deck/frame; wide beam can provide stability and space.
D is Dock. Waterside structure/place for mooring and access; wet surfaces, lines, and edges require careful walking. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Marine engine supplies propulsion power through propeller, jet, or other drive system; type varies by vessel.
F ... F ... Ferry! | Vessel carries passengers, vehicles, or both across a route on a scheduled or repeated service.
G? Gangway! | Bridge/ramp connects shore and vessel for boarding; handrails, angle, tide, and secure placement matter.
H is Harbor. Sheltered coastal/water area where vessels anchor, berth, load, or seek protection; traffic follows local navigation rules. |

K? Keel! | Longitudinal structural/underwater member helps strength and, on many sailboats, stability and resistance to sideways drift.
L is Lighthouse. Fixed navigation aid with a distinctive light/mark helps mariners identify coast/hazard; modern navigation also uses electronic systems. |

Q — Quarterdeck! | Traditional ship area on the upper deck toward the stern associated with command/ceremony; exact layout varies by era/vessel.
R ... R ... Rescue Boat! | Fast or specialized craft used by trained rescuers; rescue operations require crew, communication, and safety gear.
S? Submarine! | Submersible vessel controls buoyancy to travel underwater; operation is specialized and not ordinary recreational boating.
T is Tugboat. Powerful workboat pushes or tows ships/barges; close-quarters harbor work is professional operation. |
