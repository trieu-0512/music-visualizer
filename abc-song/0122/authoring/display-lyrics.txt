[Intro]
A disc can glide, a baton passes, a cone can mean stop, a medal does none of those.
Movement words work only when they match the object.

[Verse 1]
A — Archery Target! | Marked surface receives arrows in archery; real archery requires trained supervision, range rules, and controlled shooting lines.
B ... B ... Ball! | Round or oval sport object depending game; material, size, bounce, and rules vary widely.
C? Cone! | Bright marker defines boundaries, turns, stations, or lanes; it organizes space rather than making the movement itself.
D is Diving Board. Springing or rigid platform used for supervised diving at a designed pool; depth, rules, and trained supervision are essential. |

[Verse 2]
E — Equipment Bag! | Bag stores and transports gear; wet or protective items may need drying rather than sealing away.
F ... F ... Frisbee! | Flying disc glides with spin; throwing needs open space away from roads and bystanders.
G? Goal! | Scoring target or boundary in many sports; dimensions and scoring rules depend on the game.
H is Hoop. Circular ring can mark a target or movement station; activity and size determine safe use. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Pool! | Air-supported shallow pool for water play; children still require constant close adult supervision around any water.
J ... J ... Jump Rope! | Rope rotates around a jumper; clear spacing and supervised use prevent trips or entanglement.
K? Kickboard! | Buoyant swim-training board supports arms while practicing kicking; it is not a lifesaving flotation device.
L is Lane Marker. Line, cone, rope, or float system separates activity lanes; it organizes traffic and fair space. |

S? Soccer Ball! | Round association-football ball kicked, passed, or headed under game rules; size varies by age/group.
T is Tennis Ball. Felt-covered hollow rubber ball designed to bounce for tennis; racket striking happens in clear court space. |

W? Whistle! | Signal device can start/stop play or attract attention; loud use stays away from ears.
