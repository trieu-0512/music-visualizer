[Intro]
A footprint tells movement; a rib tells anatomy; neither tells the whole animal alone.
Fossil work begins by asking what kind of evidence we found.

[Verse 1]
A — Allosaurus! Large Jurassic theropod with three-fingered hands and serrated teeth | skeleton gives anatomy, not skin color certainty.
B ... B ... Brachiosaurus! Sauropod with forelimbs longer than hindlimbs | limb proportions help reconstruct its high-shouldered stance.
C? Ceratopsian! Beaks, frills, and horn patterns vary across the group | skull fossils are especially informative.
D is Deinonychus. Dromaeosaurid with a large second-toe claw and feathered relatives | bones plus feather evidence change old reconstructions.

[Verse 2]
E — Edmontosaurus! Large hadrosaur with broad beak and dense dental batteries | skin impressions preserve details beyond bone in some specimens.
F ... F ... Footprint! Trace fossil records a foot touching sediment | can suggest movement and trackmaker size without preserving the body.
G? Giganotosaurus! Huge South American carcharodontosaurid theropod | skull and skeleton distinguish it from Tyrannosaurus.
H is Hadrosaur. Duck-billed dinosaur group | teeth, jaws, crests, and limb bones reveal different parts of life.

[Verse 3]
I — Irritator! Brazilian spinosaurid known from a long narrow skull | incomplete remains mean reconstruction must stay cautious.
J ... J ... Jurassic Fern! Fern-like plants and plant fossils help reconstruct environments | a plant clue, not dinosaur anatomy.
K? Kentrosaurus! Stegosaur with plates toward the front and prominent spikes toward the rear | skeleton maps the armor arrangement.
L is Lambeosaurus. Hadrosaur with a hollow cranial crest | crest shape is preserved in skull fossils.

[Verse 4]
M — Mosasaurus! Giant marine squamate reptile | lived in Late Cretaceous seas and was not a dinosaur.
N ... N ... Nodosaurus! Armored dinosaur without an Ankylosaurus-style tail club | armor fossils show nodosaurid differences.
O? Ornithomimus! Ostrich-like theropod; fossil evidence from relatives and specimens supports feathers | long legs fit fast terrestrial movement.
P is Pachycephalosaurus. Thick domed skull roof is its signature fossil feature | exact behavior of the dome is still debated.

[Verse 5]
Q — Qianzhousaurus! Long-snouted tyrannosaurid | skull proportions are the key comparison clue.
R ... R ... Rib Bone! Rib shape helps reconstruct chest anatomy | one bone must be placed in the larger skeleton.
S? Stegosaurus! Plates and tail spikes preserve a dramatic armor pattern | their exact color is not fossilized in ordinary bone.
T is Triceratops. Horned skull and frill preserve well enough for detailed comparison | famous face built from real bone.

[Verse 6]
U — Utahraptor! Large dromaeosaurid with sickle-clawed second toes | much larger than Velociraptor.
V ... V ... Velociraptor! Small feathered dromaeosaurid from Mongolia | far smaller than its common movie portrayal.
W? Wuerhosaurus! Cretaceous stegosaur from Asia | incomplete fossils still place it among plated stegosaurs.
X is Xixiasaurus. Small long-skulled theropod from China | known from limited material, so fine details remain uncertain.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yinlong! Early ceratopsian from Jurassic China | small skull helps trace ceratopsian evolution before giant frills and horns.
Z ... Z ... Zephyrosaurus! Small ornithopod known from fragmentary fossils | another reminder that paleontology often works with incomplete pieces.
