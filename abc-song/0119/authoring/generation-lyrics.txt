[Intro]
Blocks can become a station; a toy train can travel through it; a doll or figure can change the story.
Pretend play uses objects as possibilities, not fixed scripts.

[Verse 1]
A — Action Figure! | Small articulated character figure supports posing and pretend narratives; small accessories can be choking hazards.
B ... B ... Ball! | Round play object rolls or bounces depending material and inflation; size should fit the age and activity.
C? Car Toy! | Toy representation of a car; rolling wheels are common, but propulsion method depends on the specific toy.
D is Doll. Human- or character-shaped play figure supports caregiving and storytelling play; parts and accessories must be age-appropriate. |

[Verse 2]
E — Excavator Toy! | Toy model represents an excavator with boom, stick, bucket, and tracked/wheeled base depending design.
F ... F ... Finger Puppet! | Small character fits over a finger for storytelling; size and detachable pieces must be age-appropriate.
G? Gyroscope! | Spinning rotor tends to resist changes to its orientation through angular momentum; toy versions demonstrate rotation.
H is Hobby Horse. Pretend horse toy, often a horse head on a stick or rocking form; clear space prevents collisions. |

[Verse 3]
I — Inflatable Ball! | Air-filled flexible ball deforms and rebounds; inflation level and size affect bounce and handling.
J ... J ... Jigsaw! | Puzzle of interlocking pieces reconstructs one image or shape by matching edges and visual clues.
K? Kaleidoscope! | Mirrors inside a tube reflect colored pieces into repeated symmetrical patterns as the view changes.
L is Lego Brick. Interlocking plastic construction brick uses studs and tubes to connect; small pieces require age-appropriate supervision. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marble! | Small hard sphere rolls smoothly and can be used in games; its size creates a serious choking hazard for young children.
N ... N ... Nesting Blocks! | Graduated hollow blocks fit inside one another and can also stack, teaching size order and containment.
O? Origami! | Paper folding can become a play object or model through creases; it is paper craft, not a manufactured toy mechanism.
P is Puzzle. Problem or object designed to be solved by matching, arranging, reasoning, or fitting pieces. |

[Chorus]
Build, place, imagine—A-B-C!
Change the story when the next idea arrives.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quoits Ring! | Ring used in tossing games toward a peg or target; soft, age-appropriate rings reduce impact risk.
R ... R ... Robot! | Toy or model robot represents a machine that can sense, compute, or move; not every robot toy is autonomous.
S? Stuffed Animal! | Soft fabric animal-shaped toy filled with stuffing; seams and attached features must remain secure.
T is Toy Train. Toy representation of railway vehicles; track, coupling, power, and wheel mechanisms depend on the set. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicycle! | Single-wheel ride-on cycle requires balance skill and protective equipment; it is not a preschool indoor riding toy.
V ... V ... Velcro Ball! | Project toy uses hook-and-loop material so a light ball can stick to a matching target or mitt.
W? Wagon! | Low wheeled cart can carry toys or be pulled; riding rules depend on design and adult supervision.
X is X-shaped Puzzle. Puzzle target uses a clear X-shaped piece or outline; the X form is the identification clue. |

[Chorus]
Build, place, imagine—A-B-C!
Change the story when the next idea arrives.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarn Doll! | Figure made by wrapping and tying yarn; loose fibers or long strands require supervised craft/play.
Z ... Z ... Zoom Car! | Project label for a small toy car; do not invent a pull-back, battery, or friction motor unless the pictured model shows one.
