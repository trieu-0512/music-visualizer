[Intro]
A moth begins as a caterpillar; a grasshopper begins as a nymph that already looks grasshopper-like.
Young insects do not all grow by the same route.

[Verse 1]
A — Ant! Complete metamorphosis: egg, larva, pupa, adult | worker adult looks nothing like the grub-like larva.
B ... B ... Bee! Complete metamorphosis also reshapes bee larvae into winged adults | colony care shelters the young.
C? Cricket! Incomplete metamorphosis: egg to nymph to adult | nymph resembles a small wingless cricket.
D is Dragonfly. Aquatic nymph transforms directly into winged adult without a pupal stage | water-to-air life-cycle shift.

[Verse 2]
E — Emperor Moth! Adult moth came through caterpillar and pupa stages | complete metamorphosis changes the body dramatically.
F ... F ... Flea! Complete metamorphosis: wormlike larva becomes pupa, then jumping adult.
G? Gnat! Fly life cycles use larva and pupa before the winged adult | many larvae live in moist habitats.
H is Hercules Beetle. Huge adult beetle begins as a large grub-like larva in decaying wood | complete metamorphosis.

[Verse 3]
I — Inchworm! Geometer-moth caterpillar | larval stage, not a tiny adult worm.
J ... J ... Jewel Beetle! Adult metallic beetle develops from a larva that often tunnels in plant tissue or wood.
K? Kissing Bug! True bug with incomplete metamorphosis | nymph resembles a smaller wingless adult and can also blood-feed.
L is Ladybug. Beetle larva looks elongated and spiky, then pupates into the rounded adult beetle.

[Verse 4]
M — Mantis! Incomplete metamorphosis | mantis nymph resembles a miniature adult without full wings.
N ... N ... Net-winged Beetle! Complete metamorphosis separates larval body from net-ridged adult elytra.
O? Orange Butterfly! Adult butterfly follows caterpillar and chrysalis stages | scaled wings appear only after metamorphosis.
P is Praying Mantis. Same incomplete-metamorphosis path as other mantises | nymph grows through molts rather than a pupa.

[Chorus]
Young, change, adult—A-B-C!
Larva or nymph? The life-cycle path tells us.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Bee! Queen is an adult reproductive caste | she also began as larva, with diet influencing caste development in honeybees.
R ... R ... Roach! Cockroach nymph resembles a smaller adult | incomplete metamorphosis, no pupa.
S? Stag Beetle! Beetle larvae can spend years developing in decaying wood before pupation | adult life is the final stage.
T is Tiger Moth. Caterpillar-to-pupa-to-moth is complete metamorphosis | fuzzy larva and winged adult can look unrelated.

[Verse 6]
U — Underwing Moth! Another complete-metamorphosis example | cryptic adult wings come after caterpillar growth.
V ... V ... Viceroy Butterfly! Caterpillar and chrysalis precede the orange-and-black adult butterfly.
W? Wasp! Complete metamorphosis | larva develops before pupating into a winged or wingless adult caste depending species.
X is Xylocopa Bee. Carpenter bee uses complete metamorphosis inside nest cells | larva, pupa, then adult.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Butterfly! Adult wing color arrives after caterpillar and pupa stages | color does not identify the larva.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Longwing! Adult butterfly comes from a caterpillar specialized on host plants | same individual, radically different stage.
