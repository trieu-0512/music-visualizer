[Intro]
A puzzle has a solution goal; a card game has rules and turns; a doll can be open-ended play.
Game night works better when we know which kind of play we are doing.

[Verse 1]
A — Alphabet Blocks! | Blocks carry letters or pictures on faces; stacking adds spatial play while letter faces support recognition.
B ... B ... Ball! | Round play object rolls or bounces depending material and inflation; size should fit the age and activity.
C? Car Toy! | Toy representation of a car; rolling wheels are common, but propulsion method depends on the specific toy.
D is Doll. Human- or character-shaped play figure supports caregiving and storytelling play; parts and accessories must be age-appropriate. |

[Verse 2]
E — Etch-a-Sketch! | Classic mechanical drawing toy uses two knobs to move a stylus horizontally and vertically behind a screen.
F ... F ... Frisbee! | Flying disc glides after a spinning throw; outdoor space and distance from people or roads matter.
G? Gyroscope! | Spinning rotor tends to resist changes to its orientation through angular momentum; toy versions demonstrate rotation.
H is Hobby Horse. Pretend horse toy, often a horse head on a stick or rocking form; clear space prevents collisions. |

[Verse 3]
I — Interlocking Blocks! | Building pieces connect through fitted shapes or studs; connection strength and geometry affect structures.
J ... J ... Jump Rope! | Rope turned for jumping games; clear space and supervised use prevent trips or wrapping around the body.
K? Kaleidoscope! | Mirrors inside a tube reflect colored pieces into repeated symmetrical patterns as the view changes.
L is Letter Tiles. Individual tiles display letters for sorting, spelling, or word play; they are movable symbols rather than fixed text. |

[Verse 4]
M — Model Car! | Small-scale representation of a car; wheels may roll, but exact mechanism depends on the model.
N ... N ... Nesting Blocks! | Graduated hollow blocks fit inside one another and can also stack, teaching size order and containment.
O? Octopus Toy! | Octopus-shaped toy is identified by form; material, seams, and detachable parts determine safe use.
P is Puzzle. Problem or object designed to be solved by matching, arranging, reasoning, or fitting pieces. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quoits Ring! | Ring used in tossing games toward a peg or target; soft, age-appropriate rings reduce impact risk.
R ... R ... Rattle! | Enclosed loose pieces or a moving part create sound when shaken; casing must stay intact so small contents cannot escape.
S? Scooter! | Wheeled ride-on platform steered by handlebars in common designs; helmet, suitable area, and supervision matter.
T is Toy Train. Toy representation of railway vehicles; track, coupling, power, and wheel mechanisms depend on the set. |

[Verse 6]
U — Unicycle! | Single-wheel ride-on cycle requires balance skill and protective equipment; it is not a preschool indoor riding toy.
V ... V ... Vehicle Toy! | Broad category for toy representations of transport; wheels, motors, scale, and function vary by model.
W? Wooden Blocks! | Solid wood construction pieces can stack and balance; smooth edges and non-toxic finishes matter.
X is X-shaped Puzzle. Puzzle target uses a clear X-shaped piece or outline; the X form is the identification clue. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yo-yo! | Spool-like toy travels up and down a string through rotation; string length and clear space require supervision.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Toy Bag! | Bag with zipper stores toys and loose parts; storage helps keep small components together.
