[Intro]
One low cloud covers the hill; one bright gap opens beside it.
We frame the sky slowly enough to notice what changes.

[Verse 1]
A — Anemometer! Cups or propeller turn with wind | a ground instrument below our cloud gallery.
B ... B ... Boots! Weather boots protect feet from wet, cold, or rough conditions | observer gear stays below the sky.
C? Cloud! Droplets or ice crystals make visible shapes in air | the central frame of the gallery.
D is Dew. Condensed water beads on a cool surface | a ground-level moisture clue under the clouds.

[Verse 2]
E — Eye of Storm! Some strong tropical cyclones develop a relatively calm center | a storm structure seen in data and imagery, not a cloud shape to chase.
F ... F ... Frost! Ice crystals can form on surfaces when they become cold enough | a close ground texture beneath the sky.
G? Gauge! A gauge displays a measured quantity | station context that needs a label telling us what it measures.
H is Hail. Ice lumps from strong thunderstorm clouds can reach the ground | shelter first, observation second.

[Verse 3]
I — Icicle! Frozen drip water forms a hanging point | we look without touching or standing beneath unstable ice.
J ... J ... Jacket! Outer clothing responds to conditions | not a cloud category.
K? Kite! Wind can lift a kite in safe open conditions | never during thunderstorms or near power lines.
L is Low Cloud. Cloud base sits relatively near the ground | a lower sky layer that can hide hills.

[Chorus]
Frame, layer, open—A-B-C!
Clouds cover some of the sky, never all of the story.

[Verse 4]
M — Mittens! Hand coverings help in cold weather | small gear in the foreground of our sky frame.
N ... N ... Night Sky! Cloud cover can hide stars or leave gaps | the gallery changes after sunset.
O? Overcast Sky! Clouds cover nearly all or all of the visible sky | broad sheet instead of separate puffs.
P is Pressure Gauge. Pressure instrument provides a reading | meteorologists care about pressure trends, not just one decorative dial.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiet Sky! A project phrase for calm-looking conditions | not a formal meteorological class.
R ... R ... Rainbow! Sunlight refracts, reflects, and disperses inside water droplets | colored arc appears opposite the Sun direction.
S? Snowflake! Ice-crystal structure grows in cloud | tiny geometry emerging from a huge sky layer.
T is Thermometer. Temperature reading gives one part of the weather picture | instrument stays at the station.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella! Portable rain protection | keep it closed in dangerous high wind or lightning conditions.
V ... V ... Vane! A weather vane points with wind direction | simple arrow below the moving clouds.
W? Windsock! Fabric tube aligns with wind and fills more as wind strengthens | visible ground clue under the gallery.
X is X-band Radar. Weather radar using X-band microwaves can detect precipitation structure | a technical observation tool, not a cloud itself.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Sun! Atmospheric scattering can make the Sun look yellowish | a bright visual anchor between cloud layers.
Z ... Z ... Zephyr! Gentle breeze moves the cloud frame slowly | a soft finish rather than a storm climax.
