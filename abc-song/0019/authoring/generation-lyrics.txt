[Intro]
Yesterday's leaves sink into soil; tomorrow's green begins there.
Down, up, around again—the garden keeps a quiet circle.

[Verse 1]
A — Apron! Before soil and water splash | cloth protects the front of our work clothes.
B ... B ... Bee! Flower to flower | a small visitor can carry pollen as it feeds.
C? Compost! Old plant and food material breaks down | nutrients return to the soil instead of ending the story.
D is Daisy. Petals ring a center | flower today, seeds later in the plant's life cycle.

[Verse 2]
Down below, E — Earthworm. Tunnel and turn | moist soil shifts around its long soft body.
F ... F ... Fern! Curled young fronds unfurl | green returns from a tight spiral.
G? Garden Gate! Open, close | a still boundary around a garden that keeps changing.
H is Herb. Leaf becomes scent and flavor | some herbs are gathered from the garden for use.

[Verse 3]
I — Ivy! Stem reaches, leaf follows | climbing growth keeps searching for light.
J ... J ... Jug! Tip and pour | water leaves the container and returns to the soil.
K? Kneeling Pad! Soft under knees | a helper while we look closely at what happens near the ground.
L is Ladybug. Six little legs cross a leaf | a beetle moving through the living garden web.

[Chorus]
Down in soil, up to sun—A-B-C!
Round and round, the garden cycle runs.

[Verse 4]
M — Marigold! Bud opens gold | flowers mark one bright stage before seeds and fading petals.
N ... N ... Nest! Twigs hold new bird life | another cycle sharing the garden space.
O? Onion! Layers build below | green leaves above feed a bulb thickening underground.
P is Pea. Flower gives way to pod | round peas line up inside for the next part of the story.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quince! Firm yellow fruit | a shrub or small tree carries it after flower and growth.
R ... R ... Rose! Petals open, petals fall | the woody plant keeps living after the bloom.
S? Seed! Coat outside, tiny plant within | a small beginning waiting for the right conditions.
T is Trowel. Soil-moving hand tool | use it only with appropriate adult guidance.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella! Rain stays on the outside | while garden water keeps moving through its own cycle.
V ... V ... Vegetable Bed! Many food plants share prepared soil | roots below, leaves above, harvest in between.
W? Watering Can! Spout pours a gentle arc | water sinks down, then plants pull it up.
X is Xeranthemum. Papery flower heads can dry and hold their shape | one bloom stage lingering after fresh petals.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yarrow! Flat clusters gather many tiny blooms | flowering now before the plant moves on.
Z ... Z ... Zinnia! Layered petals close our alphabet | another flower can make seeds and send the circle on.
