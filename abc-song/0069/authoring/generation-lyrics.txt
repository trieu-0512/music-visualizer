[Intro]
One bird wades. One hovers. One runs because its wings cannot carry it into flight.
Bird movement changes with body and habitat.

[Verse 1]
A — Albatross! Long wings exploit ocean winds for efficient soaring | marine travel covers huge distances.
B ... B ... Bluebird! Perch-and-fly movement between open ground and branches | small thrush, not water specialist.
C? Crane! Long legs wade through shallows and stride across wetlands | broad wings handle migration.
D is Duck. Webbed feet paddle; wings fly in most species | body bridges water and air.

[Verse 2]
E — Egret! Long toes and legs move through shallow water | spear-like bill strikes at prey.
F ... F ... Flamingo! Long legs wade while webbed feet stir shallow water | flight carries flocks between wetlands.
G? Goldfinch! Small perching bird uses quick wingbeats over land | seed-feeding route differs from waders.
H is Hummingbird. Rotating wing motion supports hovering and backward flight | extreme air specialist.

[Chorus]
Wade, fly, run—A-B-C!
Feet and wings tell different travel stories.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibis! Long curved bill probes mud while long legs wade | water-edge movement more than swimming.
J ... J ... Junco! Ground-hopping and short flights through brush | terrestrial songbird pattern.
K? Kiwi! Flightless bird runs and probes forest floor | tiny vestigial wings do not power flight.
L is Lark. Ground bird that can launch into display or travel flight | land and air combine.

[Verse 4]
M — Macaw! Strong parrot wings cross forest openings | grasping feet handle branches after landing.
N ... N ... Nuthatch! Strong feet climb trunks and can move head-first downward | tree-surface specialist.
O? Owl! Broad wings support quiet flight; strong talons seize prey | air movement often begins from a perch.
P is Parrot. Wings fly; zygodactyl feet climb and grip | bill can help with climbing too.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground-running bird with short burst flight | land movement dominates daily life.
R ... R ... Robin! Runs or hops on ground, then flies to perches | mixed small-land-bird movement.
S? Swan! Webbed feet paddle and powerful wings fly | long water takeoff needs space.
T is Tern. Long pointed wings support agile flight over water | many plunge or dip for prey.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrellabird! Tropical forest bird moves between branches and air | display structures do not define locomotion.
V ... V ... Veery! Thrush walks/hops on forest floor and flies through understory | song is separate from movement.
W? Woodpecker! Climbing feet and stiff tail brace against trunks | short flights connect trees.
X is Xantus Hummingbird. Tiny hovering specialist from Baja region | rapid wings contrast with grounded kiwi.

[Chorus]
Wade, fly, run—A-B-C!
Feet and wings tell different travel stories.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Small migratory songbird uses quick flight among shrubs and trees | light body, air route.
Z ... Z ... Zenaida Dove! Strong direct flight alternates with ground feeding | closes the land-air comparison.
