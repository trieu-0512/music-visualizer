[Intro]
On the Moon you still have weight; in orbit you keep falling around Earth.
Different places make different kinds of motion.

[Verse 1]
A — Astronaut! On the Moon an astronaut can walk and hop under lower gravity | in orbit the same person can experience microgravity during free fall.
B ... B ... Booster! Strong launch thrust changes spacecraft speed | nothing about a booster removes gravity.
C? Capsule! Enclosed spacecraft can orbit in free fall | objects inside may seem to float together.
D is Dish Antenna. Communication hardware points or steers a radio beam | no floating metaphor needed.

[Verse 2]
E — Eclipse! Shadow geometry changes visible light | gravity sets orbits, but eclipse itself is an alignment event.
F ... F ... Fuel Tank! Stores propellant | contents can behave differently in microgravity, so spacecraft tank design matters.
G? Gemini Capsule! Crews in orbit experienced weightlessness-like microgravity | historical spacecraft in free fall around Earth.
H is Hubble Telescope. Space telescope orbits Earth | it and its components remain under Earth's gravity while continuously falling around the planet.

[Verse 3]
I — International Space Station! Crew and station share orbital free fall | microgravity is not the absence of gravity.
J ... J ... Jet Pack! Small thrusters can change an astronaut's motion during specialized maneuvering | not everyday spacewalk gear.
K? Krypton Tank! Stores propellant for some electric thrusters | tank remains mass under gravity wherever it travels.
L is Lander. Descends toward a surface and must manage gravity with propulsion or aerodynamics depending destination.

[Chorus]
Stand, fall, float—A-B-C!
Gravity stays; motion changes what weight can feel like.

[Verse 4]
M — Moon! Earth's natural satellite has surface gravity about one-sixth of Earth's | enough to pull dust and astronauts back down.
N ... N ... Nebula! Vast gas-and-dust cloud in interstellar space | not something close enough to drift through on a Moon trip.
O? Orbit! Continuous free-fall path around a body | core idea behind orbital microgravity.
P is Planet. Planetary gravity shapes moons, rings, atmospheres, and spacecraft trajectories | gravity is part of the map.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quasar! Distant active galactic nucleus powered by accretion onto a supermassive black hole | far beyond lunar motion scale.
R ... R ... Rocket! Thrust changes velocity against or with gravity | rocket works in vacuum by expelling propellant.
S? Spacesuit! Pressurized protective system lets crew work in vacuum | suit does not create weightlessness.
T is Thruster. Controlled force changes motion or orientation | small acceleration can matter greatly in orbit.

[Verse 6]
U — Universe! Gravity operates throughout cosmic structure | widest context for our motion lesson.
V ... V ... Visor! Helmet shield manages light and protection | reflective surface can show Earth without changing gravity.
W? World! Broad term for a planetary place | each world can have different surface gravity.
X is Xenon Thruster. Electric propulsion gives gentle sustained acceleration | slow change in velocity, not magical floating.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yoke! Mapping helper for a control shape | some vehicles use yokes, but spacecraft controls vary widely.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Chair! Reclining chair redistributes body pressure and posture | it does not reproduce orbital free fall or lunar gravity.

[Chorus]
Stand, fall, float—A-B-C!
Gravity stays; motion changes what weight can feel like.
