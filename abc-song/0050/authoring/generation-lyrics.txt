[Intro]
Anemometer turns. Thermometer holds a number. Vane points a direction.
A weather station works by combining clues, not by trusting one dial.

[Verse 1]
A — Anemometer! Rotating cups or propeller respond to wind | station begins with wind speed.
B ... B ... Breeze! Light wind is the thing being sensed | phenomenon stays separate from the sensor.
C? Coat! Human outerwear beside the station | not a measuring device and not part of the weather data.
D is Drizzle. Fine liquid rain reaches the ground | visible observation that instruments can accompany.

[Verse 2]
E — Earmuffs! Cold-weather gear for the observer | useful context, zero measurement role.
F ... F ... Fog! Ground-level cloud lowers visibility | station log can record visibility along with numbers.
G? Gauge! A gauge displays a quantity | always name what a particular gauge measures before interpreting it.
H is Hail. Solid ice precipitation from strong thunderstorms | safety observation, not something to catch or collect.

[Chorus]
Measure, compare, log—A-B-C!
One reading joins another before we describe the sky.

[Verse 3]
I — Ice! Frozen water on surfaces can signal low temperatures | direct condition clue beside the thermometer.
J ... J ... Jet Stream! High-altitude strong wind band is tracked with broader meteorological data | our little station cannot measure it directly overhead.
K? Kelvin Thermometer! Temperature instrument can show kelvins | kelvin values are written without a degree symbol.
L is Low Cloud. Cloud base sits relatively low | visual sky observation beyond the instrument panel.

[Verse 4]
M — Mittens! Warm hand coverings for the observer | station context only.
N ... N ... Nimbus Cloud! Rain-cloud term in the mapping | cloud observation belongs beside precipitation notes.
O? Overcast Sky! Nearly complete cloud cover | broad visual category rather than one sensor reading.
P is Pressure Gauge. Pressure instrument adds another number | trends can matter more than decoration on the dial.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilt! Indoor warm layer | not station equipment, so it stays clearly outside the panel.
R ... R ... Rainbow! Optical effect from sunlight and droplets | observation clue no station dial needs to manufacture.
S? Sun! Solar heating drives daily temperature changes | source of energy, not an instrument reading.
T is Thermometer. Measures temperature | one of the station's clearest numeric tools.

[Chorus]
Measure, compare, log—A-B-C!
One reading joins another before we describe the sky.

[Verse 6]
U — Updraft! Rising air inside convection | important weather motion our simple ground station infers only indirectly.
V ... V ... Vane! Weather vane indicates wind direction | different job from anemometer speed.
W? Wind! Moving air is the phenomenon | compare vane direction and anemometer speed to describe it.
At the station shelf, X is X-band Radar | short radio waves help show rain or moving weather.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Sun! Apparent yellowish color can come from atmospheric scattering | visual note, not a sensor output.
Z ... Z ... Zephyr! Gentle breeze | final wind observation returns us to the anemometer and vane.
