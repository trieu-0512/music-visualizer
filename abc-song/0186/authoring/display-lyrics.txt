[Intro]
Castle is a place, Dragon is a mythical character, Quest Map is a clue, Magic Wand is a fictional prop.
A fairy tale gets clearer when story roles stay distinct.

[Verse 1]
A — Armor! | Protective clothing in historical/fantasy stories, often metal or layered material; real armor was heavy equipment, not magical invulnerability.
B ... B ... Bow! | Historical projectile weapon that launches arrows by stored limb/string energy; in this preschool fantasy set it stays picture-only.
C? Castle! | Fortified residence/defensive complex in many historical settings; towers, walls, gates, keeps, and moats vary by place and period.
D is Dragon. Mythical creature appearing in many cultures with very different forms and meanings; no single universal dragon design exists. |

G? Goblin! | Folklore/fantasy creature with many regional/literary versions; appearance and behavior depend on the story.

Q — Quest Map! | Story map marks destinations, hazards, or clues for a fictional quest; plot information can be incomplete or misleading by design.
R ... R ... Royal Throne! | Ceremonial seat associated with a monarch/ruler; it symbolizes office rather than granting power by itself.
S? Spell Book! | Fantasy book containing fictional spells; it is a story device, not a real source of supernatural power.
T is Troll. Folklore creature from Northern European traditions with many later fantasy reinterpretations. |

V ... V ... Village! | Settlement smaller than many towns/cities; in fairy tales it often anchors ordinary life before adventure.
W? Witch! | Figure in folklore, history, religion, and fantasy with widely varying meanings; fantasy witch traits should not be treated as facts about real people.
