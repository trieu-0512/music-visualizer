[Intro]
Catamaran has two hulls, trimaran has three, airboat pushes air, sailboat catches wind.
Watercraft design changes how a vessel meets the water.

[Verse 1]
A — Airboat! | Flat-bottomed boat driven by an aircraft-style propeller above water; propeller cage and trained operation are essential.
B ... B ... Buoy! | Floating marker can indicate channel, hazard, mooring, or information; color/shape meanings depend on local buoyage system.
C? Catamaran! | Boat with two parallel hulls connected by a deck/frame; wide beam can provide stability and space.
D is Dock. Waterside structure/place for mooring and access; wet surfaces, lines, and edges require careful walking. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Marine engine supplies propulsion power through propeller, jet, or other drive system; type varies by vessel.
F ... F ... Ferry! | Vessel carries passengers, vehicles, or both across a route on a scheduled or repeated service.
G? Gondola! | Traditional Venetian flat-bottomed rowing boat propelled with a single oar from the stern side; not every narrow boat is a gondola.
H is Houseboat. Boat designed primarily for living accommodation; some are mobile while others remain moored. |

[Verse 3]
I — Island Ferry! | Ferry service links an island with another shore/island; route role defines it more than hull shape.
J ... J ... Jet Ski! | Personal watercraft propelled by a water jet; high-speed operation requires age/licensing rules, PFD, and trained supervision.
K? Keel! | Longitudinal structural/underwater member helps strength and, on many sailboats, stability and resistance to sideways drift.
L is Lighthouse. Fixed navigation aid with a distinctive light/mark helps mariners identify coast/hazard; modern navigation also uses electronic systems. |

[Verse 4]
M — Motorboat! | Small powered boat propelled by inboard, outboard, jet, or other motor system; speed/handling require licensed or trained adults.
N ... N ... Navigation Light! | Required vessel lights communicate orientation/status at night or low visibility; patterns depend on vessel/activity rules.
O? Ocean Liner! | Large passenger ship designed historically for scheduled ocean crossings; distinct from cruise ship by primary service concept.
P is Paddleboat. Boat propelled by human pedaling/paddle wheels or other paddle mechanism depending design; rental rules and PFDs still apply. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quarterdeck! | Traditional ship area on the upper deck toward the stern associated with command/ceremony; exact layout varies by era/vessel.
R ... R ... Rescue Boat! | Fast or specialized craft used by trained rescuers; rescue operations require crew, communication, and safety gear.
S? Sailboat! | Boat uses sails to convert wind force into forward motion while keel/centerboard and rudder help control course.
T is Trimaran. Multihull craft with one main hull and two smaller outriggers/floats; geometry changes stability and performance. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underwater Scooter! | Diver propulsion vehicle pulls a diver through water; diving training, depth, battery, and entanglement risks require expert use.
V ... V ... Vessel! | General legal/nautical term for a watercraft; it does not specify propulsion, size, or purpose.
W? Water Taxi! | Small passenger vessel provides point-to-point transport on waterways, like a taxi service on water.
X is X-ray Sonar Screen. Project label for a sonar display; sonar uses sound, not X-rays, so 'X-ray' describes see-through-like imagery rather than radiation. |

Z ... Z ... Zodiac Boat! | Zodiac is a brand strongly associated with rigid/inflatable boats; use the pictured model rather than treating the brand as a generic hull type.
