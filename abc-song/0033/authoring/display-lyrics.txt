[Intro]
Stone keeps its shape while one flower opens beside it.
The desert carries two clocks: very slow, and suddenly alive.

[Verse 1]
A — Agave! Thick pointed leaves radiate from one center | living geometry set against hard ground.
B ... B ... Beetle! Six legs cross a stone edge | tiny movement on a surface that hardly moves at all.
C? Camel! Broad feet carry a large mammal over dry ground | one slow traveler crossing a much older landscape.
D is Dune. Wind-built hill of sand | shape changes grain by grain even when the desert looks still.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Broad wings pass over rock | a moving shadow skims a surface that stays.
F ... F ... Fox! Quiet paws move through scrub | soft fur against thorn, stone, and dust.
G? Gila Monster! Bead-like scales pattern a heavy lizard body | living texture beside mineral texture.
H is Horned Lizard. Broad flat body and pointed scales | its outline almost repeats the rough ground around it.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguana! Long tail, back spines | a moving reptile shape cutting across still rock.
J ... J ... Jackrabbit! Long ears rise, hind legs launch | sudden speed over a patient landscape.
K? Kestrel! Small falcon holds or darts in the air | one quick shape above unmoving stone.
L is Limestone. Pale sedimentary rock | layer on layer, a slow record compared with one day's animal tracks.

[Chorus]
Stone stays, petals open—A-B-C!
Some desert moments last; some only pass.

[Verse 4]
M — Mesquite! Woody branches and fine leaves | living shade rooted beside hard mineral ground.
N ... N ... Nighthawk! Dusk-flying bird catches insects in the air | evening movement begins as heat releases.
O? Oasis! Water interrupts dry color | reflection and plant growth gather where the landscape changes.
P is Prickly Pear. Flat pads can hold flowers and fruit | soft bloom rising from a spine-guarded cactus.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Small ground bird crosses gravel and scrub | quick feet between stones.
R ... R ... Rattlesnake! Scaled body, tail rattle | venomous wildlife remains a far-away observation.
S? Scorpion! Pincers and curved stinging tail | small life beside stone, always viewed from distance.
T is Tumbleweed. A dried plant can break free and roll with wind | living tissue becomes a moving desert shape after drying.

[Verse 6]
U — Urial! Wild sheep moves over dry slopes | hooves crossing rock that outlasts every track.
V ... V ... Vulture! Large wings circle above open country | slow-looking flight over slower stone.
W? Wadi! Dry channel can suddenly carry water after rain | one of the desert's sharpest still-to-moving changes.
X is Xerophyte. Dry-adapted plant | living form shaped by scarcity beside rock that needs no water at all.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yucca! Stiff leaves rise from a living base | pale flowers may lift above the sharp green form.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Finch! Small finch, short beak, quick bird movement | a final living flicker over the long-still ground.
