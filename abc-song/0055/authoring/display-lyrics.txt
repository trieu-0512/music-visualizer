[Intro]
A picture is useful; a measurement is better when we know how it was made.
Our space lab asks one question again and again: what is the evidence?

[Verse 1]
A — Astronaut! Human observer and trained operator | crew reports combine with instrument data, not replace it.
B ... B ... Black Hole! We infer black holes from effects on nearby matter, light, motion, gravitational waves, and direct horizon-scale imaging in some cases | evidence without seeing a black interior.
C? Capsule! Spacecraft hardware carries people or cargo | lab distinguishes vehicle from the observations it returns.
D is Dish Antenna. Radio reflector links spacecraft and ground | data can travel farther than the probe itself can return.

[Verse 2]
E — Earth! Satellites, ground stations, and direct sampling provide many kinds of data | our best-known planet becomes the comparison case.
F ... F ... Flag! Symbolic object can appear in mission imagery | it is evidence of human activity only when supported by mission context.
G? Galaxy! Telescopes image starlight, gas emission, dust signatures and motion | one picture contains many physical clues.
H is Helmet. Crew safety hardware | not a science sensor unless special instruments are mounted or carried.

[Chorus]
Ask, observe, check—A-B-C!
Science keeps the clue and tests the story.

[Verse 3]
I — International Space Station! Orbiting laboratory hosts experiments and Earth/space observations | microgravity environment enables specific research.
J ... J ... Jet Pack! Maneuvering device label in our mapping | engineering object, not astronomy evidence.
K? Krypton Tank! Propellant storage for some electric propulsion systems | engineering telemetry can verify tank use and performance.
L is Lunar Rover. Surface vehicle extends where people or instruments can travel | images, samples, and measurements turn movement into evidence.

[Verse 4]
M — Moon! Orbiters, landers, samples, telescopes, and direct human exploration all contribute evidence | multiple methods agree on one nearby world.
N ... N ... Nebula! Gas and dust reveal themselves through images and spectra at different wavelengths | color alone is not the whole measurement.
O? Observatory! Facility organizes instruments, tracking, calibration, and repeated observation | place where evidence is built carefully.
P is Probe. Robotic spacecraft carries sensors to destinations | sends measurements back by radio.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quasar! Spectra, variability, imaging and multiwavelength observations support the active-galactic-nucleus model | extreme brightness needs evidence across bands.
R ... R ... Rover! Mobile science platform carries imaging tools and instruments | surface path becomes a sequence of measured sites.
S? Spacesuit! Protective life-support system | lets humans work safely but does not itself prove an astronomy claim.
T is Telescope. Collects electromagnetic radiation | each design has wavelength, resolution, and sensitivity limits.

[Verse 6]
U — Uranus! Telescopes and Voyager 2 data reveal rings, moons, atmosphere and tilted rotation | evidence comes from more than apparent color.
V ... V ... Venus! Radar, orbiters, landers and spectroscopy reveal a hot dense atmosphere and hidden surface | clouds require instruments that see more than visible light.
W? White Dwarf! Dense stellar remnant identified through temperature, luminosity, spectra, mass and binary behavior | not merely a white point.
X is X-ray Telescope. Detects high-energy X-rays from hot and extreme cosmic environments | usually operates above most of Earth's atmosphere.

[Chorus]
Ask, observe, check—A-B-C!
Science keeps the clue and tests the story.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yoke! Control-interface mapping helper | engineering context stays separate from science evidence.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Chair! Reclining furniture may use the marketing name | a lab note must state clearly that it does not create microgravity.
