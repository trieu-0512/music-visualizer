[Intro]
One creature drinks plant sap, one carries pollen, one hunts another insect.
A garden is a web of jobs, not a list of heroes and villains.

[Verse 1]
A — Aphid! Sap-feeding insect can form colonies on stems and leaves | prey for lady beetles and other predators.
B ... B ... Bee! Many bees move pollen while collecting nectar or pollen | pollination is a plant-insect relationship, not a favor done on purpose.
C? Cricket! Omnivorous or plant-feeding roles vary by species | many males produce chirps by rubbing wing structures.
D is Dragonfly. Adult aerial predator hunts small insects | aquatic nymph lives underwater, linking pond and garden.

[Verse 2]
E — Earwig! Omnivore that may eat plant material, fungi, or small animals | role changes with species and food available.
F ... F ... Firefly! Beetle whose larvae often prey on small soft-bodied animals | adults of many species signal with light.
G? Gnat! Tiny fly; roles range from decomposer larvae to plant feeder or nuisance | 'gnat' covers many groups.
H is Hercules Beetle. Huge rhinoceros beetle; larvae develop in decaying wood | decomposition habitat matters to its life cycle.

[Verse 3]
I — Insect! Six-legged animal group filling many garden roles | one label cannot predict its job.
J ... J ... June Bug! Scarab beetle; larvae often feed underground on roots or organic matter | adults may feed on leaves.
K? Kissing Bug! Blood-feeding triatomine | not a garden handling target; some species can transmit Chagas disease parasites.
L is Leafhopper. Sap-feeding hemipteran can move between plants | some species transmit plant pathogens.

[Chorus]
Feed, pollinate, hunt—A-B-C!
Ask what it does before you decide what it means.

[Verse 4]
M — Mosquito! Aquatic larvae grow in standing water; adults feed on plant sugars, and females of many species also take blood | some transmit disease.
N ... N ... Net-winged Beetle! Adults often show ridged, netlike elytra | larvae of many species live in decaying wood or soil.
O? Orb Weaver! Eight-legged spider catches insects in an orb web | garden predator, not an insect.
P is Praying Mantis. Ambush predator grabs insects and other small prey | useful ecological role does not make it safe to handle.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queen Bee! Reproductive female in a social bee colony | colony role differs from worker tasks such as foraging.
R ... R ... Rhinoceros Beetle! Scarab larvae often develop in decaying plant material | adults carry large horns in many species.
S? Stick Insect! Plant eater camouflaged as twigs or leaves | feeding role differs from mantis predation.
T is Tiger Moth. Moth group with often hairy caterpillars | caterpillars feed on plants while adults occupy a different life stage.

[Chorus]
Feed, pollinate, hunt—A-B-C!
Ask what it does before you decide what it means.

[Verse 6]
U — Underwing Moth! Moth hides bright hindwings beneath cryptic forewings | caterpillars feed on host plants.
V ... V ... Vinegar Fly! Small fly attracted to fermenting fruit and microbes | larvae help process yeasty decaying material.
W? Weevil! Beetle with elongated snout | many species feed on particular plants or seeds.
X is Xylocopa Bee. Carpenter bee often nests in tunnels it excavates in wood | adults also visit flowers.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellowjacket! Social wasp that can sting defensively | observe food-seeking workers from distance and never disturb a nest.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Butterfly! Zebra-patterned butterfly mapping clue | flower visits and host plants connect adult and larval stages.
