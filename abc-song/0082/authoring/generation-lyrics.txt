[Intro]
A shell closes. A cobra raises a hood. A frog's color can warn.
Defense is something we study, not something we test.

[Verse 1]
A — Alligator! Armored skin, broad snout and powerful tail | large crocodilian, always distance-only.
B ... B ... Boa! Heavy-bodied nonvenomous snake that constricts prey | coiling is feeding biology, not a handling game.
C? Chameleon! Color change helps signaling and physiology as well as camouflage | gripping feet anchor the lizard.
D is Dragon Lizard. Project lizard target with claws and long tail | exact species left unspecified.

[Verse 2]
E — Emerald Tree Boa! Green arboreal boa coils on branches | nonvenomous constrictor, observe-only.
F ... F ... Frilled Lizard! Neck frill expands in defensive/display behavior | warning display means give space.
G? Gecko! Adhesive toe structures help many geckos cling to surfaces | escape and camouflage differ by species.
H is Horned Lizard. Spiny scales and flattened body aid defense/camouflage | some species can squirt blood from eyes as extreme defense.

[Verse 3]
I — Italian Newt! Amphibian with moist skin and aquatic breeding | not a scaled reptile.
J ... J ... Jackson Chameleon! East African chameleon; males often carry three facial horns | visual signal, not a weapon for us to imitate.
K? King Cobra! World's longest venomous snake | hood display and venom demand complete distance.
L is Leopard Gecko. Ground-dwelling gecko with spotted pattern and movable eyelids | tail can store fat and may be shed defensively.

[Chorus]

Look at the adaptation; leave the animal space.

[Verse 4]
M — Monitor Lizard! Active predatory lizard group | strong claws/tails and species size require respectful distance.
N ... N ... Nile Crocodile! Armored semi-aquatic predator | still water posture hides powerful movement.
O? Ornate Box Turtle! Terrestrial turtle can close hinged plastron tightly | shell is living skeleton, not a removable house.
P is Poison Frog. Some wild poison frogs carry skin toxins derived largely from diet | never touch wild frogs; captive toxicity can differ.

[Verse 5]
Q — Queensland Frog! Project frog target | moist amphibian skin is the reliable class clue.
R ... R ... Red-eyed Tree Frog! Arboreal frog with gripping toe pads | bright eyes/colors may startle predators.
S? Salamander! Amphibian whose skin can produce defensive secretions in some species | gentle observation only.
T is Toad. Many toads have parotoid glands producing defensive toxins | do not handle or let pets mouth them.

[Verse 6]
U — Uromastyx! Spiny-tailed herbivorous lizard | tail helps defense, while basking supports thermoregulation.
V ... V ... Viper! Venomous snake family with hinged fangs in many species | image-only target.
W? Water Dragon! Semi-aquatic lizard can flee into water | long tail aids swimming and balance.
X is Xenosaurus. Knob-scaled lizard genus from Mexico/Central America | crevice-dwelling body, not a fantasy monster.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow-bellied Slider! Freshwater turtle with shell and yellow markings | defensive response is withdrawal, not a toy trick.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Salamander! Salamander mapping clue | amphibian safety stays no handling unless expert care context.
