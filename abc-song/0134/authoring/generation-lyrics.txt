[Intro]
Metro can run underground; an intercity train goes farther; a boxcar carries freight; a sleeper serves long trips.
One rail alphabet can cross several network types.

[Verse 1]
A — Arrival Board! | Electronic or printed display lists expected arrivals and platform/service information; times can change with operations.
B ... B ... Boxcar! | Enclosed freight car with sliding/plug doors protects general cargo from weather.
C? Coach! | Passenger rail car, especially for seated accommodation; coach is not the locomotive.
D is Diesel Engine. Compression-ignition engine burns diesel fuel; diesel locomotives often use it to drive a generator feeding electric traction motors. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Railway 'engine' can mean locomotive or its power unit; exact meaning depends on context.
F ... F ... Footbridge! | Pedestrian bridge crosses tracks above grade, keeping people away from the running rails.
G? Gate! | Rail/station gate controls access or protects a crossing; the pictured location determines its exact job.
H is Hopper Car. Freight car with sloped bottom gates carries loose bulk material. |

[Verse 3]
I — Intercity Train! | Passenger service links cities, usually with fewer stops and longer distances than local transit.
J ... J ... Junction Box! | Protected enclosure houses electrical connections; railway electrical equipment is for trained workers, not passenger handling.
K? Key Switch! | Locked/key-operated control allows authorized operation of equipment; passengers do not operate railway control switches.
L is Locomotive. Rail vehicle supplies tractive power to move itself and often other cars; power may be diesel-electric, electric, or other forms. |

[Verse 4]
M — Metro! | Urban rapid-transit rail system; may use tunnels, elevated structures, or surface sections.
N ... N ... Night Train! | Train service scheduled overnight; may include ordinary seats or sleeping accommodation depending route.
O? Overpass! | Bridge carries one route above another, separating traffic levels and reducing conflicts.
P is Platform. Raised or level boarding area beside a track; passengers stay behind edge markings until safe boarding. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiet Car! | Passenger area with reduced conversation/device noise under operator rules.
R ... R ... Rail! | Steel running surface guides flanged train wheels; tracks are not walking paths.
S? Sleeper Car! | Passenger car provides berths or compartments for overnight travel; it supports rest, not train propulsion.
T is Train. One or more rail vehicles operating together on a guided track system; signals and timetable/dispatch control safe separation. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underground Train! | Train operating on an underground/subway system or tunneled section; stations and signals govern movement.
V ... V ... Vestibule! | Enclosed end/entry area of a passenger car connects doors and often adjacent cars; passengers follow door and movement rules.
W? Wagon! | Railway term for a freight/passenger vehicle in some regions, while everyday English can also mean a road/hand cart; rail context matters.
X is X-crossing Sign. X-shaped railway-crossing warning sign marks a road-rail crossing; it warns road users, not trains to cross freely. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Signal! | Railway yellow aspect commonly indicates caution or a restrictive condition ahead, but exact meaning depends on the signaling system; drivers follow local rules.
Z ... Z ... Zone Map! | Map divides a transit network into fare, service, or geographic zones; colors/labels explain boundaries.
