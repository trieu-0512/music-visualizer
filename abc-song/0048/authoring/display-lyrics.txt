[Intro]
Before the zipper moves, we check the sky and temperature.
Weather gear works best when it matches the condition.

[Verse 1]
A — Altocumulus! Mid-level patches tell us about the sky | cloud name first, clothing choice later.
B ... B ... Boots! Protective footwear can help with wet, cold, or muddy ground | choose the pair for the condition.
C? Coat! Outer layer adds warmth or weather protection | useful when conditions call for it, not automatically.
D is Drizzle. Very light rain falls in fine drops | a wet-condition clue before rain gear.

[Verse 2]
E — Earmuffs! Ear coverings reduce cold exposure | one small layer for chilly conditions.
F ... F ... Fog! Ground-level cloud lowers visibility | clothing cannot fix visibility, so route safety matters too.
G? Gust! Brief stronger wind can change how cold it feels and how gear behaves | hats and umbrellas need care.
H is Humidity Meter. A hygrometer measures air moisture | information tool, not something to wear.

[Chorus]
Check, choose, adjust—A-B-C!
Wear what helps now; change it when the weather changes.

[Verse 3]
I — Icicle! Frozen drip water signals cold surfaces | look, do not stand under unstable hanging ice.
J ... J ... Jacket! Flexible outer layer can add warmth or weather protection | easy to put on or remove as conditions change.
K? Kelvin Thermometer! Temperature instrument displaying kelvins | a reading helps describe temperature, not pick clothes by itself.
L is Low Cloud. Low cloud can reduce visibility and bring damp conditions | sky clue rather than wardrobe item.

[Verse 4]
M — Mist! Fine droplets near ground can feel damp | light moisture may change what outer layer feels comfortable.
N ... N ... Nimbus Cloud! A rain-cloud label in everyday use | precipitation possibility belongs in the condition column.
O? Overcast Sky! Broad cloud cover blocks much direct sunlight | cloudiness alone does not tell us exact temperature.
P is Puddle. Standing rainwater shows the ground is wet | boots may help more than extra warm layers.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quilt! Warm bedding belongs indoors or at rest | not outdoor weather clothing.
R ... R ... Rainbow! Optical effect from sunlight and water droplets | beautiful clue, no special clothing required.
S? Snowflake! Falling ice crystals indicate snowy conditions | warmth and traction matter more than copying the shape.
T is Thunder. Sound caused by rapid heating and expansion around lightning | go indoors; clothing is not lightning protection.

[Chorus]
Check, choose, adjust—A-B-C!
Wear what helps now; change it when the weather changes.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella! Rain cover can help in ordinary rain | avoid using it as a solution in lightning or dangerous wind.
V ... V ... Vane! Wind-direction instrument points into or with wind orientation depending on design | station clue, not gear.
W? Wind! Moving air changes comfort and can increase heat loss | one reason layers may need adjustment.
X is X-band Radar. Weather radar can show precipitation patterns | information comes before a clothing decision.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Rain Boots! Bright waterproof boots keep feet drier in puddles and rain | color is fun; function is the real clue.
Z ... Z ... Zipper Jacket! Zipper closes an outer layer quickly | final reminder that layers can be adjusted, not fixed all day.

[Chorus]
Check, choose, adjust—A-B-C!
Wear what helps now; change it when the weather changes.
