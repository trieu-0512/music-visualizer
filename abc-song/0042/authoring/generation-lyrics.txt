[Intro]
Start low where two slopes lean toward the valley.
Now trace one line upward until the sky meets rock.

[Verse 1]
A — Alpine Goat! Sure hooves take broken ground | body stays close to the slope while our melody starts to rise.
B ... B ... Boulder! One huge rock sits still | a contour marker that does not need to be a summit.
C? Cliff! Steep exposed rock lifts almost straight | a sharper rise than a walkable trail.
D is Douglas Fir. Tall evergreen follows the slope line | hanging cones point down while the tree rises up.

[Verse 2]
E — Edelweiss! Small starry flower of high mountain habitats | a low plant beneath a large sky.
F ... F ... Falcon! Strong wings cross from one contour line to another | air ignores the route our feet must follow.
G? Goat! Hooves step where footing allows | one living clue on the incline, not a cartoon climber on every cliff.
H is Hawk. Broad circles ride rising air | height comes from flight, not from standing at the crest.

[Verse 3]
I — Icefall! A steep broken section of glacier flows downhill faster than flatter ice | cracked blue-white contour in motion.
J ... J ... Jay! Perch, hop, fly | a bird crossing trees and openings along the slope.
K? Kestrel! Small falcon can hover or perch | one precise sky marker over lower ground.
L is Lynx. Furry wild cat moves quietly over land | broad paws and long legs belong to terrain, not vertical rock faces.

[Chorus]
Low, rise, crest—A-B-C!
Every slope has another way back down.

[Verse 4]
M — Marmot! Stocky mountain mammal lives around burrows and rocky places | a low round body beneath high peaks.
N ... N ... Nest! A protected place for eggs or young | height varies by species, so we do not force one layer.
O? Outcrop! Bedrock appears at the surface | a hard edge where the landform shows what it is made of.
P is Pika. Small round-eared mammal lives among rocks | quick movement stays close to talus and crevices.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Ground bird steps through lower cover | one valley-side contrast to falcon and hawk.
R ... R ... Rope! Strong flexible climbing equipment | children identify it as adult-guided gear, never a cue to climb.
S? Summit! Highest point closes the upward contour | our voice reaches the crest before the route turns down.
T is Trail. A path bends with terrain | good routes avoid pretending the shortest line is always safest.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Meadow! Open grassy or flower-rich ground at elevation | a broad pause after steep rock.
V ... V ... Valley! Low land between higher slopes | the place our contour has been measuring against all along.
W? Waterfall! Water takes the steepest visible direction—down | one clear descending phrase.
X is Xeric Shrub. Woody plant adapted to dry conditions | a low rooted shape on the drier side of our contour.

[Chorus]
Low, rise, crest—A-B-C!
Every slope has another way back down.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Bell! Bell-shaped yellow bloom in the alphabet set | a small color mark, not proof of a specific alpine range.
Z ... Z ... Zigzag Trail! Switchbacks spread one steep climb across many gentler turns | contour becomes route design.
