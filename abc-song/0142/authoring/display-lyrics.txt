[Intro]
Chart the route, read the buoy, check depth, notice the lighthouse, keep rescue procedures ready.
Sea travel begins with information before motion.

[Verse 1]
A — Anchor! | Heavy device grips seabed/riverbed to limit vessel drift; dropping/retrieving it requires awareness of depth, bottom, and lines.
B ... B ... Buoy! | Floating marker can indicate channel, hazard, mooring, or information; color/shape meanings depend on local buoyage system.
C? Canoe! | Narrow open craft commonly paddled with single-bladed paddles; PFD and water conditions matter.
D is Dinghy. Small boat used independently or as a tender; sail, oar, or motor propulsion depends on design. |

[Verse 2]
E — Echo Sounder! | Instrument sends sound pulses downward and times echoes to estimate water depth or detect bottom/fish features.
F ... F ... Fishing Boat! | Vessel used to catch or support harvesting fish; gear and size vary from small boats to large commercial vessels.
G? Gangway! | Bridge/ramp connects shore and vessel for boarding; handrails, angle, tide, and secure placement matter.
H is Harbor. Sheltered coastal/water area where vessels anchor, berth, load, or seek protection; traffic follows local navigation rules. |

[Verse 3]
I — Island Ferry! | Ferry service links an island with another shore/island; route role defines it more than hull shape.
J ... J ... Jet Ski! | Personal watercraft propelled by a water jet; high-speed operation requires age/licensing rules, PFD, and trained supervision.
K? Kayak! | Narrow paddle craft commonly uses double-bladed paddle; PFD, weather, and water skill matter.
L is Lighthouse. Fixed navigation aid with a distinctive light/mark helps mariners identify coast/hazard; modern navigation also uses electronic systems. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marina! | Managed harbor area provides berths and services for recreational/small craft; docks and fuel areas have posted rules.
N ... N ... Narrowboat! | Long narrow canal boat designed for inland waterways and locks; not an ocean-going shape by default.
O? Ocean Liner! | Large passenger ship designed historically for scheduled ocean crossings; distinct from cruise ship by primary service concept.
P is Pier. Structure projects over/along water for access, berthing, fishing, or recreation; edges remain fall hazards. |

[Chorus]
Plan, mark, depth—A-B-C!
Read the water before the journey.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quay! | Solid waterside structure where vessels berth to load/unload; edges and mooring equipment are work/safety zones.
R ... R ... Rescue Boat! | Fast or specialized craft used by trained rescuers; rescue operations require crew, communication, and safety gear.
S? Sailboat! | Boat uses sails to convert wind force into forward motion while keel/centerboard and rudder help control course.
T is Tugboat. Powerful workboat pushes or tows ships/barges; close-quarters harbor work is professional operation. |

[Verse 6]
U — Underwater Scooter! | Diver propulsion vehicle pulls a diver through water; diving training, depth, battery, and entanglement risks require expert use.
V ... V ... Vessel! | General legal/nautical term for a watercraft; it does not specify propulsion, size, or purpose.
W? Wake! | Disturbed water pattern trails a moving vessel; wake size depends on speed, hull, depth, and conditions.
X is X-ray Sonar Screen. Project label for a sonar display; sonar uses sound, not X-rays, so 'X-ray' describes see-through-like imagery rather than radiation. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yawl! | Sailboat rig with a small mizzen mast aft of the rudder post in the traditional definition.
Z ... Z ... Zephyr Sail! | Project poetic label for a sail shown in light wind; zephyr means a gentle breeze, not a standardized sail type.
