[Intro]
Silhouette first. Bill second. Habitat third. Call only if we actually hear it.
A careful birdwatcher lets several clues agree.

[Verse 1]
A — Albatross! Enormous narrow-winged seabird | ocean habitat plus gliding silhouette makes the ID.
B ... B ... Budgie! Small Australian parrot with curved bill and long tail | wild range matters despite common pet familiarity.
C? Crane! Tall legs, long neck, straight bill | wetland or grassland silhouette, not just 'big bird'.
D is Duck. Webbed feet and broad bill support water life | shape, behavior, and plumage narrow the species.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Large hooked bill, broad wings, strong talons | distance prevents size illusion from fooling us.
F ... F ... Finch! Small conical-billed seed eater | bill and flock behavior can guide ID.
G? Goose! Long neck, webbed feet, heavy body | flock formation and calls add context.
H is Heron. Long neck often folds in flight; long legs trail behind | silhouette separates it from crane flight posture.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibis! Downcurved probing bill is the strongest field mark | wetland habitat confirms the shape.
J ... J ... Junco! Small sparrow-like bird feeding near ground | plumage and white tail flashes help by region.
K? Kiwi! Flightless nocturnal New Zealand bird | range alone prevents a false local ID in most of the world.
L is Lorikeet. Brush-tipped tongue suits nectar feeding; curved bill shows parrot ancestry | region and color refine ID.

[Verse 4]
M — Macaw! Huge parrot with long tail and massive curved bill | tropical American range is essential context.
N ... N ... Nuthatch! Climbs bark and often descends head-first | behavior may identify it before plumage.
O? Ostrich! World's largest living bird, flightless with long running legs | African open-habitat card, unmistakable scale.
P is Penguin. Flightless seabird with flipper-like wings and upright stance | Southern Hemisphere marine context matters.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quail! Plump ground bird with short bill | quick ground movement and burst flight support the ID.
R ... R ... Raven! Large corvid with heavy bill and wedge-shaped tail in Common Raven | compare carefully with crows by region.
S? Swan! Very large waterbird with long neck | size, water habitat, and bill shape combine.
T is Toucan. Huge colorful bill on a relatively light body | tropical American forest context prevents confusion.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Sandpiper! Long-necked grassland shorebird relative | often found far from shore in open fields.
V ... V ... Vulture! Soaring silhouette, broad wings, scavenging ecology | identify from distance without approaching food or roosts.
W? Wren! Tiny active bird with often cocked tail | dense cover and rapid movement are strong behavioral marks.
X is Xenops. Small Neotropical bark-foraging bird | narrow bill and branch-creeping behavior guide ID.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Small yellow warbler with thin insect-eating bill | color plus shape, habitat, and range make the ID.
Z ... Z ... Zenaida Dove! Medium dove with soft coo and pointed wings | Caribbean and nearby regional context completes the note.
