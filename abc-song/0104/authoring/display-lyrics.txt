[Intro]
A picture can start a guess; a ruler, map, dictionary, or question can help us check it.
Looking is the beginning of learning, not the end.

[Verse 1]
A — Alphabet Chart! | Chart displays letter forms and sequence; it can support recognition without replacing sound, writing, or word practice.
B ... B ... Book! | Pages can carry stories, facts, images, or instructions; contents tell the purpose.
C? Calculator! | Electronic or mechanical tool performs entered calculations; correct keys still depend on choosing the right mathematical operation.
D is Desk. Work surface supports books, writing, and materials; clear space helps separate active work from stored supplies. |

[Verse 2]
E — Envelope! | Folded paper container protects and carries a letter or small flat item; flap closes the opening.
F ... F ... Flashcard! | Study card pairs a prompt with an answer; recall before peeking.
G? Globe! | Spherical Earth model reduces many shape distortions of flat maps, while remaining tiny-scale.
H is Hole Punch. Lever tool cuts small holes through paper; moving metal parts can pinch, so preschool handling belongs with an adult or teacher. |

[Chorus]
Notice, ask, check—A-B-C!
A good answer can survive another look.

[Verse 3]
I — Ink! | Colored liquid or paste carries pigment or dye to a surface and can stain, so lids and careful handling matter.
J ... J ... Jigsaw Map! | Map puzzle breaks a geographic picture into interlocking pieces; matching shape and location rebuilds the whole.
K? Knowledge Card! | Project card holds a fact, clue, or concept; it is not one standardized product.
L is Lunchbox. Food container; safe temperature control depends on contents and storage. |

[Verse 4]
M — Marker! | Fiber- or felt-tip writing tool feeds ink to a broad or fine point; cap care helps keep many markers from drying out.
N ... N ... Notebook! | Bound pages keep related notes or drawings in order and make earlier work easy to revisit.
O? Organizer! | Compartments or labeled sections keep supplies separated so items can be found and returned efficiently.
P is Pencil. Writing tool usually contains a graphite-and-clay core, not metallic lead; pressure changes line darkness. |

[Chorus]
Notice, ask, check—A-B-C!
A good answer can survive another look.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quiz Card! | Prompt checks recall or understanding; a miss shows what to practice next.
R ... R ... Ruler! | Straight tool marks units such as centimeters or inches; measure from zero.
S? Stapler! | Tool bends metal staples through stacked sheets; staple points and the closing mechanism require adult or teacher management for preschoolers.
T is Textbook. Instructional book organizes a subject into explanations, examples, images, and often practice material. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uniform! | Standardized clothing identifies a school or group; it is part of school routine, not a learning tool by itself.
V ... V ... Violin Case! | Protective container supports and shields a violin and bow during storage or transport; the case itself makes no musical pitch.
W? Whiteboard! | Smooth reusable writing surface accepts dry-erase markers and can be wiped for the next idea.
X is X-axis Graph. Horizontal Cartesian axis usually carries x-values paired with vertical y-values. |

[Chorus]
Notice, ask, check—A-B-C!
A good answer can survive another look.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Pencil! | A pencil with a yellow-painted body still writes through its graphite core; body color does not change the writing mechanism.
Z ... Z ... Zero Card! | Card shows 0: no counted items in some contexts and a place-holding digit in others.
