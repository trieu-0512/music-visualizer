[Intro]
One tail is a club. One mouth is a beak. One animal here flew on a wing membrane.
Prehistoric bodies did not all move the same way.

[Verse 1]
A — Ankylosaurus! Heavy armor and a tail club protected a low-slung herbivore | not every dinosaur defense used horns or speed.
B ... B ... Bone! Hard body tissue can fossilize and preserve anatomy | evidence, not a living dinosaur.
C? Ceratopsian! Beaked plant-eating dinosaur group with frills and, in many members, horns | group clue rather than one species.
D is Deinonychus. Feathered dromaeosaurid with a large curved claw on each second toe | agile predator, not a giant movie monster.

[Verse 2]
E — Egg! Fossil eggs and nests can reveal reproduction and nesting behavior | shell evidence needs context.
F ... F ... Fossil! Preserved remains, impressions, or traces of past life | the main evidence source for this body study.
G? Gastonia! Armored ankylosaur with plates and spikes | defense by body armor rather than a classic Ankylosaurus tail club.
H is Hadrosaur. Duck-billed ornithopod group with broad beaks and complex teeth | many were large plant eaters.

[Verse 3]
I — Iguanodon! Large ornithopod with a distinctive thumb spike | hands and beak give two clear body clues.
J ... J ... Jawbone! Fossil jaw can preserve tooth shape and bite anatomy | evidence about feeding, not a whole animal by itself.
K? Kritosaurus! Hadrosaurid known especially from skull material | a duck-billed dinosaur reconstructed from incomplete evidence.
L is Lesothosaurus. Small early ornithischian with light build and long hind limbs | much smaller body plan than giant sauropods.

[Chorus]
Armor, beak, wing—A-B-C!
Build the body clue before you name the animal.

[Verse 4]
M — Maiasaura! Hadrosaurid known from nesting colonies and young of different ages | fossils support social nesting behavior.
N ... N ... Nodosaurus! Armored nodosaurid with body plates and spikes | unlike Ankylosaurus, nodosaurids lacked a heavy tail club.
O? Oviraptor! Beaked oviraptorid; fossils show adults brooding nests | the old 'egg thief' story came from an early mistaken interpretation.
P is Pteranodon. Large toothless pterosaur flew with membrane wings | flying reptile, not a dinosaur.

[Verse 5]
Q — Qianzhousaurus! Long-snouted tyrannosaurid from Late Cretaceous Asia | skull shape differs from broad-snouted Tyrannosaurus.
R ... R ... Raptor! Everyday label often used for dromaeosaurids | scientific names tell us which animal we actually mean.
S? Stegosaurus! Back plates and paired tail spikes make a distinctive herbivore body | plates were not a shell of armor.
T is Triceratops. Three facial horns, large frill, and beak | ceratopsian body plan in one famous genus.

[Chorus]
Armor, beak, wing—A-B-C!
Build the body clue before you name the animal.

[Verse 6]
U — Unenlagia! South American dromaeosaurid relative | a lightly built feathered theropod, known from incomplete remains.
V ... V ... Volcano! Geological feature can bury, erode, or expose rocks over time | context, not a prehistoric animal.
W? Wuerhosaurus! Stegosaur from Early Cretaceous Asia | plates and tail spikes place it in the stegosaur body plan.
X is Xenoceratops. Ceratopsid with a frilled, horned skull | one branch of the diverse ceratopsian family.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yinlong! Small early ceratopsian from Jurassic China | shows that horned-dinosaur relatives began with modest body size and simpler skull ornament.
Z ... Z ... Zephyrosaurus! Small ornithopod from Early Cretaceous North America | light body and running legs close the comparison.
