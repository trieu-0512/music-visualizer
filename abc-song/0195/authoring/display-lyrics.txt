[Intro]
AI calculates models, quantum sensors measure delicate effects, lasers make coherent light, X-ray arrays detect radiation.
Future science still has to answer to evidence and safety.

[Verse 1]
A — AI Chip! | Processor/accelerator optimized for machine-learning calculations; running a model does not make the chip conscious.
B ... B ... Battery! | Electrochemical device converts stored chemical energy to electrical energy; polarity, voltage, chemistry, charging, and disposal must match the device.
C? Circuit Board! | Rigid/flexible substrate supports and electrically connects components through conductive traces and pads.
D is DAC. Digital-to-analog converter turns digital numeric codes into corresponding analog voltage/current within its range and resolution. |

[Verse 2]
E — Encoder! | Sensor/device converts position, motion, or another state into coded electrical/digital information; rotary and linear encoders are common.
F ... F ... FPGA! | Field-programmable gate array contains configurable digital logic blocks/interconnects that can be reprogrammed for hardware functions.
G? Gyroscope! | Sensor or spinning device measures/maintains orientation; electronic gyroscopes often measure angular rate using MEMS structures.
H is High Voltage. Electrical condition with serious shock/arc hazards; thresholds depend on standards and it is never preschool experimentation. |

[Verse 3]
I — Infrared Sensor! | Sensor detects infrared radiation or uses IR light for ranging/presence depending design; infrared is electromagnetic radiation beyond visible red.
J ... J ... Joystick! | Human input control reports direction and sometimes buttons/axes; it sends commands rather than directly moving a robot joint.
K? Keyboard! | Key-based input device sends codes/commands to a computer or controller; software interprets those inputs.
L is Laser Module. Device makes coherent light; direct eye exposure can injure, so real modules need rated lab safety controls. |

[Verse 4]
M — Microcontroller! | Computer-on-chip combines CPU, memory and peripherals; programs determine behavior.
N ... N ... Network Cable! | Wired medium carries data signals between network devices; connector/category and supported speed depend on cable standard.
O? Oscilloscope! | Instrument plots voltage versus time/signals; trained users connect probes within safe limits.
P is Power Supply. Source converts/provides electrical power at specified voltage/current; current capability and polarity must match load. |

[Verse 5]
Q — Quantum Sensor! | Sensor class uses quantum effects for precise measurement; it is not a quantum computer by definition.
R ... R ... Robot Arm! | Mechanical linkage with joints/actuators positions an end effector; controller and program determine commanded motion.
S? Sensor! | Device detects a physical quantity/event and converts it into usable data/signal; every sensor has limits, noise, and calibration considerations.
T is Transistor. Semiconductor component controls current/voltage and can switch or amplify; bipolar and field-effect types differ. |

[Verse 6]
U — UV Sensor! | Sensor measures ultraviolet intensity; measurement never requires deliberate skin/eye exposure.
V ... V ... Voltage Regulator! | Circuit maintains or converts output voltage within a specified range; it cannot safely ignore current, heat, or input limits.
W? Wheel Encoder! | Encoder measures wheel rotation to estimate speed/distance; wheel slip means estimated travel can differ from true ground movement.
X is X-ray Sensor Array. Multiple X-ray detector elements form images; operation belongs in controlled professional systems. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Yaw Sensor! | Project label for sensor estimating rotation about a vertical/yaw axis, often using gyroscope or fused inertial data; drift/calibration can matter.
Z ... Z ... Zero-voltage Meter! | Project meter label for checking a zero/near-zero voltage condition; zero reading depends on instrument range, connection, and actual circuit state.
