[Intro]
Beet is a root; kale is a leaf; tomato is a botanical fruit.
Color helps us notice produce, but plant part tells another story.

[Verse 1]
A — Apricot! Stone fruit with soft flesh around one hard pit | orange color is visual, pit is structural.
B ... B ... Beet! Swollen root is commonly eaten while leaves are edible too | root-plus-leaf plant.
C? Carrot! Enlarged taproot stores energy underground | leafy tops remain above soil.
D is Daikon. Long radish root with leafy greens | root vegetable, not a pale carrot.

[Verse 2]
E — Eggplant! Botanical berry from a flower, commonly cooked as a vegetable | glossy skin may be purple, white, green, or striped.
F ... F ... Fig! Syconium: a fleshy structure holding many tiny flowers/fruits inside | unusual fruit architecture.
G? Guava! Tropical fruit with fragrant flesh and many seeds | skin color varies with cultivar.
H is Honeydew. Melon fruit with thick rind and pale green flesh in common cultivars | fruit card.

[Verse 3]
I — Iceberg Lettuce! Tight head of crisp leaves | leaf vegetable.
J ... J ... Jalapeno! Chile pepper fruit from a flower | green or red depending maturity; capsaicin creates heat.
K? Kale! Leafy brassica | leaf blade and sturdy petiole/stem are the visual clues.
L is Lemon. Citrus fruit with aromatic peel, segments, and acidic juice | fruit card.

[Chorus]
Root, leaf, fruit—A-B-C!
Color is one clue; plant part is another.

[Verse 4]
M — Melon! Broad fruit category in the gourd family | rind, seed cavity, and flesh vary by type.
N ... N ... Napa Cabbage! Dense head of pale-ribbed leaves | leafy brassica, not a root.
O? Orange! Citrus fruit made of juice-filled segments | rind color alone does not define ripeness everywhere.
P is Peach. Stone fruit with flesh around one hard pit | fuzzy or smooth skin depends on peach/nectarine type.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quandong! Australian native Santalum fruit with red skin and a hard stone | fruit card, not a melon.
R ... R ... Radish! Edible swollen root in many cultivars | leaves may also be eaten.
S? Strawberry! Aggregate accessory fruit: red flesh carries many tiny true fruits on its surface | seeds-looking dots are achenes.
T is Tomato. Botanical berry from a flower | culinary vegetable use does not change the plant part.

[Chorus]
Root, leaf, fruit—A-B-C!
Color is one clue; plant part is another.

[Verse 6]
U — Ulluco! Andean crop grown for colorful edible underground tubers | tuber, not root.
V ... V ... Vidalia Onion! Sweet onion type legally associated with a defined Georgia production area | bulb made of fleshy leaf bases.
W? Watermelon! Large pepo fruit with rind, flesh and seeds | fruit from a flowering vine.
X is Xigua. Xigua is Mandarin pinyin for watermelon | same fruit, language-name clue rather than a new species.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Pepper! Ripe sweet pepper fruit with hollow center and seeds | botanical fruit used as a vegetable.
Z ... Z ... Zinfandel Grape! Grape cultivar producing berries in clusters | cultivar name, not a separate plant part.
