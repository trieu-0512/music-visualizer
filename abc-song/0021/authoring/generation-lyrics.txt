[Intro]
Crunch... then hush. One step settles into leaves.
Before we name the forest, we listen to it.

[Verse 1]
A — Aspen! Small leaves flicker in the breeze | pale trunks stand while the canopy whispers.
B ... B ... Birch! Papery bark curls at the edge | a pale trunk bright between darker trees.
C is Cedar. Green branches hold a soft hush | evergreen foliage gathers close around the trunk.
D? Douglas Fir! Needle-like leaves, hanging cones | tall branches murmur above the trail.

[Verse 2]
E — Evergreen! Some leaves or needles stay through the seasons | green held when other colors change.
F ... F ... Fox! Pointed ears, bushy tail | soft paws cross cover without much sound.
G? Grouse! Ground bird, sturdy body | one sudden wing-burst can break the forest hush.
H is Hedgehog. Stiff spines cover its back | a small body can curl tight for protection.

[Verse 3]
I — Ivy! Stems climb and trail | leaves follow the bark upward without making a footstep.
J ... J ... Juniper! Evergreen leaves, berry-like seed cones | a sharp green shape beside the path.
K? Knot! A hard twist in the wood | the old branch-place makes a swirl in the trunk.
L is Log. Fallen trunk, rough bark | cracks and hollows can shelter small forest life.

[Chorus]
Step, stop, listen—A-B-C!
The quiet woods keep answering softly.

[Verse 4]
M — Maple! Broad leaves lift and turn | a dry one may rasp softly when the breeze moves through.
N ... N ... Newt! Small amphibian near damp cover | careful steps leave it room to move.
O? Owl! Round eyes, hooked beak, broad wings | a night call can travel far between trees.
P is Pinecone. Woody scales overlap around seeds | one cone rests dry beneath the branches.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quartz Rock! Hard mineral, glassy sparkle | silent among leaf litter and roots.
R ... R ... Rabbit! Long ears turn first | quick hind legs carry it through low cover.
S? Squirrel! Claws grip bark | a scratch, a leap, then the branch is still again.
T is Tree. Roots hold, trunk rises, branches spread | the whole woodland soundscape gathers around it.

[Verse 6]
U — Upland Fern! Feathered fronds fan from the floor | green shapes soften the space between trunks.
V ... V ... Vine! Long stem climbs or trails | leaves weave through the vertical forest.
W? Woodpecker! Beak meets wood—tap, tap, drum | the sound points upward before we spot the bird.
At the trail shelter, X is Xylophone—our X-word helper | tuned bars answer the woodland rhythm without pretending to be a forest plant.

[Chorus]
Step, stop, listen—A-B-C!
The quiet woods keep answering softly.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Warbler! Small yellow songbird | a light call can float from leaves above.
Z ... Z ... Zebra Swallowtail! Pale striped wings sweep past | a butterfly moving quietly through the last patch of light.

[Interlude]
No footsteps for one beat—what woodland sounds can we remember?
