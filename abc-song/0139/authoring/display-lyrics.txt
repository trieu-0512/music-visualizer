[Intro]
Airplane uses fixed wings, helicopter uses a main rotor, quadcopter uses four rotors, turboprop drives a propeller with a turbine.
Aircraft families can share the sky without sharing one design.

[Verse 1]
A — Airplane! | Powered fixed-wing aircraft uses forward motion through air to generate wing lift; propulsion and configuration vary.
B ... B ... Boarding Pass! | Travel document/token links a passenger to a flight and often seat/gate data; gate/time still need current checks.
C? Cargo Plane! | Aircraft configured mainly to carry freight; loading balance and restraint are critical to safe flight.
D is Drone. Uncrewed aircraft can be remotely piloted or automated; operation must follow local airspace, privacy, and safety rules. |

[Verse 2]
E — Elevator Trim! | Trim system reduces sustained pitch-control force by adjusting a trim tab, stabilizer, or control command depending aircraft.
F ... F ... Fuselage! | Main aircraft body houses crew, passengers, cargo, or systems and connects wings/tail in conventional layouts.
G? Gate! | Airport gate is a passenger boarding/arrival area assigned to a flight; it is not the runway or aircraft parking stand itself.
H is Hangar. Large building shelters aircraft for storage or maintenance; active maintenance areas are restricted workspaces. |

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument Panel! | Panel/display area presents flight, engine, navigation, and system information to pilots; exact instruments vary by aircraft.
J ... J ... Jetway! | Enclosed passenger boarding bridge connects terminal gate to an aircraft door when positioned and secured by trained staff.
K? Kerosene Tank! | Fuel tank stores aviation turbine fuel such as kerosene-based jet fuel; fueling areas are restricted fire-safety zones.
L is Landing Gear. Wheels, struts, or other gear support an aircraft on ground and absorb landing loads; many aircraft retract gear in flight. |

[Verse 4]
M — Monoplane! | Fixed-wing aircraft with one main wing plane, unlike a biplane's two stacked wing planes.
N ... N ... Nose Wheel! | Forward landing-gear wheel(s) support and often steer a tricycle-gear aircraft on the ground.
O? Oxygen Mask! | Emergency aviation mask supplies supplemental oxygen during cabin-pressure problems; passengers follow crew instructions when directed.
P is Propeller. Rotating blades accelerate air to create thrust; propellers are severe hazards and require complete clearance when operating. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadcopter! | Uncrewed rotorcraft with four rotors; changing relative rotor speeds controls lift and attitude.
R ... R ... Runway! | Prepared surface used for aircraft takeoff and landing; it is a tightly controlled area, never a pedestrian viewing path.
S? Seaplane! | Fixed-wing aircraft designed to take off and land on water using floats or a boat-like hull.
T is Turboprop. Gas-turbine engine drives a propeller through a gearbox; much of the thrust comes from the propeller. |

[Verse 6]
U — Ultralight! | Very light aircraft category whose legal definition, training, licensing, and operating limits vary by country.
V ... V ... Visor! | Cockpit/passenger sun visor reduces glare; it is not the same as a head-up display or flight instrument.
W? Wing! | Airfoil surface produces lift through airflow and pressure effects; wings also carry structure, fuel, or controls depending design.
X is X-ray Scanner. Airport security imaging system uses controlled radiation to inspect baggage/items; trained staff operate it under safety procedures. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Taxiway Sign! | Yellow-background airport signs commonly give taxiway direction/destination information; exact meaning comes from text, arrows, and local standards.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Plane! | Aircraft flying parabolic arcs creates brief microgravity as occupants and cabin follow free-fall together; gravity is not switched off.
