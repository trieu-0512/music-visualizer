[Intro]
Two small marks cross the sand, then disappear at a stone.
The desert keeps clues—but the wind never keeps them forever.

[Verse 1]
A — Agave! Thick pointed leaves form a rosette | a rooted landmark that stays while animal tracks pass by.
B ... B ... Bighorn Sheep! Split hooves mark rocky or sandy ground | curved horns ride above a sure-footed body.
C? Cactus! Spines guard thick water-storing tissues in many desert cacti | a still marker beside moving tracks.
D is Desert Tortoise. Round shell, sturdy feet | low tracks lead toward shade or a burrow during safer hours.

[Verse 2]
E — Eagle! Wide wings leave no footprints in the air | its moving shadow can cross the sand below.
F ... F ... Fox! Light paws print a narrow trail | pointed ears and bushy tail move on ahead.
G? Gila Monster! Heavy-bodied lizard with bead-like scales | short legs carry a patterned body across warm ground.
H is Hawk. Strong wings and grasping feet | a sky clue above the track map rather than on it.

[Verse 3]
I — Ibex! Sure hooves grip rock | a mountain-and-desert goat leaving hard-to-follow marks on stony slopes.
J ... J ... Joshua Tree! Branching trunk and stiff pointed leaves | a landmark tall enough to guide the eye.
K? Kestrel! Small falcon, quick wingbeats | hovering or perching above ground signs we can actually follow.
L is Lizard. Tiny feet and a long tail can draw different lines | scales stay close to the desert surface.

[Chorus]
Print, trace, fade—A-B-C!
Read the sand, then let the wind erase the trail.

[Verse 4]
M — Mesquite! Deep-rooted woody plant with fine leaves | shade and branch shape make a fixed point in the open.
N ... N ... Nopales! Flat green prickly-pear pads | plant food and cactus tissue, not footprints in the sand.
O? Oryx! Long straight horns over a strong hoofed body | a broad desert trail crossing open ground.
P is Prickly Pear. Flat pads, spines, colorful fruit | one cactus landmark holding still through the wind.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quartz! Hard mineral with a glassy sparkle | a bright stone marker that outlasts soft tracks.
R ... R ... Roadrunner! Long legs make quick bird steps | the trail can zig, stop, then race on.
S? Scorpion! Small legs and curved stinging tail | observe any desert scorpion only from a safe distance.
T is Tortoise. Hard shell rides low above sturdy feet | slow marks cross the ground without any branch-climbing story.

[Chorus]
Print, trace, fade—A-B-C!
Read the sand, then let the wind erase the trail.

[Verse 6]
U — Umbrella Thorn! Spreading thorny crown makes shade | a rooted landmark where the track map changes texture.
V ... V ... Viper! Venomous snake can leave a winding body trace | we learn that sign without approaching the animal.
W? Wren! Tiny bird steps may gather near cover | then wings erase the need for a ground trail.
X is Xerus. African ground squirrel of open dry country | quick feet write a small trail between shelter and food.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Scorpion! Pale scorpion against warm sand | a safety clue we identify only from far away.
Z ... Z ... Ziziphus Shrub! Hardy thorny branches can carry small fruits | one last rooted landmark before the wind clears the page.

No new steps for one beat—what tracks and landmarks can we remember?
