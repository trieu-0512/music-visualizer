[Intro]
Ground team prepares, passengers board, aircraft departs, cruises, lands, and clears the runway.
A normal flight day has stages—and some alphabet cards live outside that timeline.

[Verse 1]
A — Airplane! | Powered fixed-wing aircraft uses forward motion through air to generate wing lift; propulsion and configuration vary.
B ... B ... Biplane! | Fixed-wing airplane with two main wings stacked vertically; struts/bracing are common in classic designs.
C? Cargo Plane! | Aircraft configured mainly to carry freight; loading balance and restraint are critical to safe flight.
D is Drone. Uncrewed aircraft can be remotely piloted or automated; operation must follow local airspace, privacy, and safety rules. |

[Verse 2]
E — Engine! | Aircraft engine provides thrust or shaft power; turbine, piston, electric, and other designs work differently.
F ... F ... Flight Deck! | Another term for the aircraft cockpit/crew control area; it is a restricted operational workspace.
G? Glider! | Aircraft designed for unpowered soaring after launch or tow; it trades altitude and rising air for continued flight.
H is Helicopter. Rotorcraft uses rotating wings for lift and can hover; rotor areas are dangerous and approached only under trained procedures. |

[Verse 3]
I — Instrument Panel! | Panel/display area presents flight, engine, navigation, and system information to pilots; exact instruments vary by aircraft.
J ... J ... Jetway! | Enclosed passenger boarding bridge connects terminal gate to an aircraft door when positioned and secured by trained staff.
K? Kerosene Tank! | Fuel tank stores aviation turbine fuel such as kerosene-based jet fuel; fueling areas are restricted fire-safety zones.
L is Luggage Cart. Ground vehicle/cart moves baggage around airport service areas; ramp traffic is controlled and not passenger play space. |

[Verse 4]
M — Monoplane! | Fixed-wing aircraft with one main wing plane, unlike a biplane's two stacked wing planes.
N ... N ... Nose Wheel! | Forward landing-gear wheel(s) support and often steer a tricycle-gear aircraft on the ground.
O? Overhead Bin! | Cabin storage compartment holds approved carry-on items; doors must latch and weight/size limits prevent falling baggage.
P is Passenger Plane. Aircraft configured primarily to carry passengers; seating, range, propulsion, and size vary widely. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadcopter! | Uncrewed rotorcraft with four rotors; changing relative rotor speeds controls lift and attitude.
R ... R ... Radar! | System transmits radio waves and analyzes returns to detect range, direction, weather, or traffic information depending type.
S? Seaplane! | Fixed-wing aircraft designed to take off and land on water using floats or a boat-like hull.
T is Tail. Rear aircraft structure includes stabilizing surfaces such as vertical and horizontal stabilizers in many designs. |

[Verse 6]
U — Uniform! | Crew or ground-service clothing identifies role and organization; uniform itself does not grant passengers operational authority.
V ... V ... Vertical Stabilizer! | Vertical tail surface provides directional stability; a rudder is commonly hinged to it for yaw control.
W? Windsock! | Fabric cone shows approximate wind direction and gives a rough visual indication of wind strength; it is not a precision anemometer.
X is X-wing Model. Model of the fictional X-wing starfighter; it is a science-fiction comparison object, not a real certified aircraft type. |

[Chorus]

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Taxiway Sign! | Yellow-background airport signs commonly give taxiway direction/destination information; exact meaning comes from text, arrows, and local standards.
Z ... Z ... Zero-gravity Plane! | Aircraft flying parabolic arcs creates brief microgravity as occupants and cabin follow free-fall together; gravity is not switched off.
