[Intro]
Checklist first: communicate, carry, steer, thrust, observe.
A rocket mission works because different parts do different jobs.

[Verse 1]
A — Asteroid! Mission target or hazard context, not rocket hardware | small body orbiting the Sun.
B ... B ... Booster! Provides additional thrust during launch | one propulsion stage in the checklist.
C? Capsule! Enclosed spacecraft section can carry crew or cargo | structure and life-support context, not an engine.
D is Dish Antenna. Curved reflector focuses radio waves | communication system sends and receives signals.

[Verse 2]
E — Earth! Launch world and communication home base | destination context rather than subsystem.
F ... F ... Flag! Mission marker or symbol | carries no navigation or propulsion function.
G? Gemini Capsule! Historical two-person NASA spacecraft | engineering example from early crewed orbital missions.
H is Hubble Telescope. Space telescope in Earth orbit observes mainly ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared wavelengths | science instrument, not crew cabin.

[Verse 3]
I — Io! Volcanically active moon of Jupiter | science target far beyond launch hardware.
J ... J ... Jupiter! Giant planet and possible mission destination region | not part of a rocket stack.
K? Krypton Tank! Stores krypton propellant used by some electric-propulsion systems | tank supplies reaction mass to a thruster.
L is Lunar Rover. Surface vehicle for mobility on the Moon | payload or surface system after landing.

[Verse 4]
M — Meteor! Light phenomenon in an atmosphere when a meteoroid enters | not a rock floating beside the rocket.
N ... N ... Nebula! Cloud of gas and dust in space | astronomical target, usually far beyond any classroom mission route.
O? Observatory! Place or platform for astronomical instruments | science context, not launch stage.
P is Planet. Rounded world orbiting a star | destination category, not vehicle hardware.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quasar! Extremely luminous active galactic nucleus powered by matter falling toward a supermassive black hole | distant science target, not a nearby star.
R ... R ... Rover! Mobile robot or crewed surface vehicle | explores after arrival, not during launch.
S? Satellite! Object in orbit | mission payload can become an artificial satellite after deployment.
T is Thruster. Small rocket or electric propulsion device produces controlled force | used for maneuvering or sustained propulsion depending design.

[Verse 6]
U — Universe! Everything cosmic our mission belongs within | definitely not one checklist component.
V ... V ... Venus! Second planet from the Sun | target world, not propulsion hardware.
W? White Dwarf! Dense stellar remnant left by a Sun-like star after it sheds outer layers | astronomy target, not white-colored planet.
X is Xenon Thruster. Electric propulsion ionizes xenon and accelerates ions | efficient, low-thrust system very different from a launch booster.

[Verse 7]
Y — Yellow Star! Everyday color label for some stars | stellar color relates to temperature, but apparent color also depends on viewing and classification.
Z ... Z ... Zodiac Chart! Astronomical chart can mark constellations along the ecliptic | map context, not a device that predicts a mission's future.
