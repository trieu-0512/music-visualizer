[Intro]
Turn a wrist gently, notice skin contact, hear your voice, then pause.
Body awareness is noticing—not proving how far or hard you can move.

[Verse 1]
A — Arm! | Upper limb from shoulder through hand in everyday use; bones, joints, muscles, nerves and vessels coordinate movement.
B ... B ... Back! | Posterior trunk includes spine, muscles, ribs, skin, nerves, and connective tissues; posture and movement come from many structures working together.
C? Chin! | Lower front facial prominence formed mainly by mandible plus soft tissue; chin shape naturally varies.
D is Dimple. Small skin indentation often linked to underlying facial structure/muscle variation; presence or absence is a normal trait. |

[Verse 2]
E — Eye! | Sensory organ focuses light onto the retina; vision also depends on optic nerves and brain processing.
F ... F ... Finger! | Digit contains bones, joints, tendons, nerves, skin, and nails; fingers support touch and precise grip.
G? Gum! | Gingiva is soft tissue around teeth that helps seal/protect tooth-supporting structures; healthy gums still need regular oral care.
H is Hand. Body part with palm, fingers and thumb supports touch, grasping, communication, and many fine movements. |

[Chorus]

Comfort comes before range.

[Verse 3]
I — Iris! | Colored muscular ring of the eye controls pupil size and therefore how much light enters.
J ... J ... Joint! | Connection between bones; different joint types allow movement, stability, or both.
K? Knuckle! | Prominent joint area where finger bones meet hand or each other; bending comes from the joint, tendons, and muscles.
L is Lips. Soft tissue around mouth opening supports speech, eating, expression and touch. |

[Verse 4]
M — Mouth! | Opening and oral cavity begin digestion and support taste, chewing, swallowing, speech and breathing pathways.
N ... N ... Nose! | Structure supports smell and conditions inhaled air through nasal passages; smell receptors sit high in nasal cavity.
O? Outer Ear! | Visible pinna plus outer ear canal collect/direct sound toward eardrum; hearing continues through middle/inner ear and brain.
P is Pupil. Opening at center of iris lets light into eye; it appears black and changes size as iris muscles respond. |

[Chorus]
Move, notice, pause—A-B-C!
Comfort comes before range.

[Verse 5]
Q — Quadriceps! | Group of four muscles at front of thigh primarily extends knee and helps control movement.
R ... R ... Retina! | Light-sensitive neural tissue lines back of eye and converts light into nerve signals sent toward brain.
S? Skin! | Largest organ by surface area, providing barrier, sensation, temperature regulation and immune functions; appearance varies normally across people.
T is Tongue. Muscular organ helps move food, taste, speak and swallow; taste receptors are distributed across the tongue rather than strict taste zones. |

[Verse 6]
U — Upper Arm! | Region between shoulder and elbow contains humerus plus muscles, nerves and blood vessels.
V ... V ... Voice! | Sound begins when airflow makes vocal folds vibrate, then throat, mouth and nose shape resonance and speech.
W? Waist! | Everyday region between ribs and hips; waist size/shape varies and is not a moral or complete health measure.
X is X-ray. Medical imaging uses ionizing radiation to create internal images, especially useful for bones; trained professionals control dose and positioning. |

[Verse 7]
Y — Y-shaped Bone Model! | Project teaching model shaped like Y; it demonstrates a bone/model concept but is not proof that a normal human bone universally has this shape.
Z ... Z ... Zinc Bandage! | Project label for a zinc-containing dressing, such as zinc-oxide bandage used in some medical care; selection/application belongs to trained healthcare guidance.
