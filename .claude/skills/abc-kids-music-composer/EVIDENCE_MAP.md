## Learning-objective separation

Rule family:
- lexical-semantic teaching is not the same job as verbatim song memorization;
- unfamiliar vocabulary deserves conservative first-exposure treatment when melody itself is novel;
- mnemonic hooks may use stronger repetition/rhyme than semantic teaching.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Blair, Hawtof & Morini, “Spoken or sung? Examining word learning in child-directed speech and in song,” Journal of Child Language, 2025, DOI 10.1017/S0305000925100081: in 32 children aged 47–50 months, novel object names were learned better from spoken sentences than from a song using an unfamiliar melody.
- RESEARCH — Hu, Liang & Du, “Singing Enhances Poetry Learning and Memorization in Preschool Children,” Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, 2026, DOI 10.1111/nyas.70335: singing improved learning speed/fluency and long-term poem recall in Mandarin-speaking children aged 4–6.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — `teach-then-sing` is this project's conservative synthesis of those different objectives; neither study directly tests that exact sequence.

Translation caution:
- Blair et al. does not show that speech is always superior to familiar/predictable song;
- Hu et al. studies memorization of known-language poetry, not initial semantic acquisition of novel object labels;
- therefore do not generalize either result into a universal “speech first” or “sing everything” rule.

## Retrieval and spacing

Rule family:
- Round 2 may use retrieval cue -> brief gap -> confirmation;
- successful retrieval across later sessions matters more than massed repetition in one sitting;
- difficult targets may receive more spaced retrieval opportunities.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Gordon & Lowry, “Fostering retention of word learning: The number of training sessions children retrieve words positively relates to post-training retention,” Journal of Child Language 51(3), 2024, DOI 10.1017/S0305000923000752.

Translation caution:
- this evidence does not establish one universal retrieval-gap duration or a fixed repetition count.

## Letter-name / letter-sound structure

Rule family:
- alphabet letters differ in learning difficulty;
- in phonics mode, some English letter names cue their common sounds more directly than others;
- letter-level properties belong in the Learning Block Manifest.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Piasta & Wagner, “Learning letter names and sounds: Effects of instruction, letter type, and phonological processing skill,” Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 105(4), 2010, DOI 10.1016/j.jecp.2009.12.008.
- RESEARCH — multiple studies summarized in that paper found stronger sound learning for letters whose names contain useful sound cues.

Translation caution:
- letter-name sound-cue structure is mainly relevant to phonics/sound instruction; do not over-apply it to a pure letter-name vocabulary song.

## Letter confusability / independent identity

Rule family:
- visually/phonologically confusable letters need more careful isolation;
- ordered ABC singing is not sufficient evidence of independent letter recognition;
- L2/L3 evaluation should sample selected targets out of sequence.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Treiman, Kessler & Pollo, “Learning about the letter name subset of the vocabulary: Evidence from US and Brazilian preschoolers,” Applied Psycholinguistics, 2006: shape similarity strongly predicted confusion; children were especially likely to interchange letters similar in both shape and name.
- RESEARCH — Phillips et al., “IRTs of the ABCs: Children's letter name acquisition,” Journal of School Psychology 50(4), 2012: letters differ in acquisition difficulty; letter-related factors matter.

Translation caution:
- do not hard-code one universal confusable-pair list across cases/locales; derive risk from the actual visual/phonological system.

## Four-beat participation frame

Rule family:
- predictable four-beat call/response is a useful preschool starting frame.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Whitcomb, “Rhythmic Characteristics of Improvisational Drumming Among Preschool Children,” Research & Issues in Music Education 8(1), 2010: in a small 4–5-year-old sample, most responses began immediately after a four-beat call and were themselves four beats with steady beat.

Translation caution:
- small study; use as a heuristic, not a hard gate.

## Prosody / correct adult modeling

Rule family:
- lexical stress must be correct;
- uncertain multi-syllable stress must be verified;
- adult lead is a pronunciation model;
- motif variants must preserve natural stress.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH / LINGUISTIC REFERENCE — standard dictionary pronunciation for target locale.
- PROJECT REGRESSION — `umbrella` must be `um-BREL-la`, not `UM-brel-la`; `eraser` = `e-RA-ser`.

Translation caution:
- do not infer stress from spelling alone.

## Melody simplicity / contour

Rule family:
- child-facing motifs should be narrow, predictable, mostly repeated-note/stepwise;
- contour identity matters more than decorative interval complexity;
- use motif families with stress-preserving variants.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — Gudmundsdottir, “Revisiting singing proficiency in three-year-olds,” Psychology of Music 48(2), 2020, DOI 10.1177/0305735618798031: in self-selected songs, 85% of three-year-olds preserved melodic contour while 24% preserved intervals, supporting contour as a more robust reproduction feature than exact interval structure in that task.
- RESEARCH — Ramsey, “The Effects of Age, Singing Ability, and Instrumental Experiences on Preschool Children's Melodic Perception,” Journal of Research in Music Education 31(2), 1983, DOI 10.2307/3345216: melodic rhythm, contour, tonal center and interval perception varied with age/singing ability.
- RESEARCH CAUTION — Creel, “Ups and Downs in Auditory Development: Preschoolers’ Sensitivity to Pitch Contour and Timbre,” Cognitive Science 40(2), 2016, DOI 10.1111/cogs.12237, found contour discrimination could be good while harder contour-to-character association was weak; contour should be predictable, not assumed to be an automatically strong semantic label.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — narrow child echo tessitura and one-octave-ish overall range remain working defaults pending project audio/child evidence.

## Tempo

Rule family:
- no magic BPM;
- broad preschool starting envelope around 80–100 BPM;
- calibrate from age band, learning objective, phrase density, and response space;
- never raise tempo to rescue overly dense lyrics.

Evidence:
- DESCRIPTIVE CRAFT/REPERTOIRE EVIDENCE — “The use of music on Barney & Friends: implications for music therapy practice and research,” Journal of Music Therapy 38(2), 2001 (PMID 11469919), analyzed 749 structural-prompt presentations across 88 episodes and found 80–100 BPM was the most common tempo range.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — 80–100 BPM is only a broad starting envelope inferred from repertoire plus project needs; the study does **not** establish an optimal developmental tempo.

## Rhyme and density

Rule family:
- natural rhyme can improve mnemonic structure;
- meaning/prosody outrank rhyme;
- shorter sections reduce generator rushing/compression risk;
- add sections rather than overstuffing one section.

Evidence:
- CRAFT_REFERENCE — Bitwize `lyric-writer`, `lyric-reviewer`, and `craft-reference.md` quality gates for rhyme, prosody, section length, density, pronunciation, verse development, and refinement.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — preschool-specific adaptation uses stricter local clarity and objective-aware rhyme rather than blindly copying adult streaming word-count limits.

## Suno local repair / editor

Rule family:
- diagnose localized failure before rewriting the whole song;
- use section-level repair where available;
- use Remaster after educational content/structure/performance are already substantially correct;
- saved voice identity may support series consistency.

Evidence:
- PROVIDER_DOC — Suno Help, “Can I replace a section of a song?”, edited Dec 19 2025.
- PROVIDER_DOC — Suno Help, “How to Use: Song Editor,” edited May 30 2025.
- PROVIDER_DOC — Suno Help, “Remaster,” edited Sep 25 2025.
- PROVIDER_DOC — Suno Help, “Voices FAQ” and “What’s New in v5.5,” edited Mar 26 2026.

Translation caution:
- provider UI names and capabilities change; re-check official documentation before changing workflow rules.

## Multimodal congruence

Rule family:
- audio, visual object, and action should teach the same concept;
- generic movement is for participation; object-specific movement should be semantically congruent;
- retrieval visuals should not spoil the answer before the intended cue gap.

Evidence:
- RESEARCH — “Effects of learning modalities on melodic and rhythmic retention and on vocal pitch-matching by preschool children,” Perceptual and Motor Skills 78(3 Pt 2), 1994, DOI 10.2466/pms.1994.78.3c.1231 (PMID 7524016): auditory and multimodal instruction improved melodic/rhythmic outcomes; kinesthetic-only treatment performed worse than the other modalities in that study.
- RESEARCH — Wang et al., “Do congruent gestures on touchscreens enhance young children's word learning?”, British Journal of Educational Psychology, 2026, DOI 10.1111/bjep.70108 (PMID 42446061): 5–6-year-olds showed better recognition when touchscreen gesture type matched the semantic structure of novel verbs than when mismatched.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — translate these findings conservatively into audio+visual+action congruence; neither paper directly tests AI-generated ABC music videos or our exact visual reveal timing.
- PROJECT_HEURISTIC — exact visual reveal timing remains age-band and production dependent.

## Project empirical — first rhyme-forward ABC L1 fixture (2026-08-08)

Observed on `Say It! Show It! A-B-C!.mp3` generated from the project audit fixture:

- prompt tempo ~92 BPM; measured beat estimate ~89.3 BPM — close enough that tempo itself was not the primary failure;
- Round 1 target-word ASR proxy was very strong (25/26 exact target words with `small.en`; only first `xylophone` was ambiguous);
- Round 2 target-word ASR proxy was weaker (22/26 exact), with ambiguity concentrated late in the 6-line `U–Z` block and on `pig`;
- a requested one-beat retrieval gap realized inconsistently: recognized cue->target gaps averaged about 0.475 s (~0.71 beat at 89.3 BPM), with some near 0.2–0.3 s and `X -> xylophone` effectively immediate in the ASR timing;
- integrated level was about -14.7 LUFS with LRA ~2.6 LU, indicating low macro-dynamic contrast in this render.

Evidence class: **PROJECT_EMPIRICAL — single variant**.

Translation caution:
- do not convert these values into universal hard limits;
- use them to justify measuring actual retrieval gaps at L1 and preferring shorter retrieval chunks when a provider shows late-section degradation;
- ASR disagreement/consensus is an intelligibility proxy, not proof of child perception or lexical stress correctness.

## Rule-system engineering

Rule family:
- distinguish hard gates, objective-specific rules, heuristics, and creative preferences;
- deterministic lint must not claim audio-only properties have passed;
- controlled A/B tests change one major variable at a time;
- repeated project evidence may recalibrate heuristics;
- regression tests protect against rule drift.

Evidence:
- PROJECT_ENGINEERING — derived from reproducibility, debugging, and automation needs.
- PROJECT_EMPIRICAL — update these rules as evaluation logs accumulate.
